What this calculator estimates

Convert a number to scientific notation, E notation, coefficient and exponent.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Number: the value to write in scientific notation, parsed as a finite number, as the limitations note says.
  • Significant figures: how many digits to keep, from 1 to 15. Choose the count explicitly; the limitations note says typed trailing zeros are not used to infer precision.

Method and assumptions

The calculator rewrites the number as a coefficient between 1 and 10 times a power of ten, rounded to the requested significant figures, and also shows the E-notation form and the coefficient and exponent separately.

With the starting inputs 123456789 at 6 significant figures: the coefficient is 1.23457 and the exponent is 8, so the result is 1.23457 times 10 to the 8, written in E-notation as 1.23457e+8.

Worked example

9.999 to three significant figures becomes 1.00 x 10¹, rather than an unnormalized 10 x 10⁰. Zero can display trailing zeros showing the requested precision.

Reading the result

  • The coefficient always falls between 1 and 10; the exponent counts how far the decimal point moved, 8 places here.
  • E-notation is the same value in the compact form many programs accept: 1.23457e+8.
  • Rounding to 6 significant figures is why the result ends at 7 while the input ends at 9; the dropped tail is rounded.

Common mistakes

  • Reading 1.23457 times 10 to the 8 as exactly the input; at 6 significant figures it is the rounded neighbor 123457000.
  • Typing trailing zeros to force precision; the limitations note says the requested figure count alone controls precision.
  • Asking for more than 15 significant figures; the calculator rejects counts outside 1 to 15.
  • Confusing the exponent with the number of digits; an exponent of 8 means a 9-digit integer part.

Frequently asked questions

What does the preserved worked example about 9.999 show?

That rounding can carry into the next power of ten: 9.999 to three significant figures becomes 1.00 times 10 to the 1, normalized, rather than an unnormalized 10 times 10 to the 0.

How are very small numbers shown?

With a negative exponent, such as 0.00456789 becoming 4.57 times 10 to the minus 3 at three significant figures; the significant-figures calculator demonstrates that exact case.

Why choose the figure count myself?

Because typed zeros do not prove precision, as the limitations note explains; declaring the count keeps the result honest about how many digits carry meaning.

Limitations

The input is parsed as a finite floating-point number. Trailing zeros in the original input are not used to infer measurement precision; choose the requested significant figures explicitly.

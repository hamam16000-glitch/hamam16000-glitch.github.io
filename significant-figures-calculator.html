What this calculator estimates

Round a number to a chosen number of significant figures and show scientific notation.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Number: the value to round, such as a measurement result.
  • Significant figures: how many significant digits to keep, from 1 to 15. The limitations note says the tool does not infer precision from how the number was typed.

Method and assumptions

The calculator rounds the number to the requested significant figures, shows the rounded value, rewrites it in scientific notation, reports the order of magnitude as the power of ten, and shows the difference between the entry and the rounded value.

With the starting inputs 0.00456789 at 3 significant figures: the rounded value is 0.00457, the scientific form is 4.57 times 10 to the minus 3, the magnitude is -3 and the difference is 0.0000021100.

Worked example

0.00456789 to three significant figures becomes 0.00457. A value of 1e-100 to three significant figures displays as 1.00e-100 rather than failing because it needs more than 100 decimal places.

Reading the result

  • The rounded value keeps only the digits that carry meaning at the chosen precision; here 0.00457 keeps three.
  • Order of magnitude is the power of ten of the leading digit; -3 means the first significant digit sits in the thousandths place.
  • The difference line shows exactly what rounding removed, 0.0000021100 here, so the cost of the rounding is visible.

Common mistakes

  • Counting leading zeros as significant; in 0.00456789 the first significant digit is the 4, which is why 3 figures give 0.00457, not 0.004.
  • Expecting typed trailing zeros to be honored; the limitations note says precision comes from the figure count you request, not from how the number was typed.
  • Reading the result as a claim about laboratory uncertainty; the limitations note says the tool does not infer measurement precision.
  • Requesting more than 15 figures; the calculator rejects counts outside 1 to 15.

Frequently asked questions

What does the preserved worked example add?

It shows an extreme case still working: a value of 1e-100 to three significant figures displays as 1.00e-100 instead of failing when the decimal expansion would need more than 100 places.

Why is the difference not simply the dropped digits?

Rounding also changes the last kept digit when the dropped part reaches half, so the difference reflects both effects together.

When would the scientific line and the main result differ?

Only in notation; both carry the same rounded value, with the scientific line making the magnitude explicit, 4.57 times 10 to the minus 3 here.

Limitations

This tool applies decimal rounding to a floating-point input. It does not infer laboratory uncertainty or the precision of a number merely from how it was originally typed.

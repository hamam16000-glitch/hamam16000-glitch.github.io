What this calculator estimates

Estimate the premium for a contract, license or court surety bond using bond amount, rate, term, credit and industry risk factors and fees.

How to use it

Enter Required bond amount ($); Base annual bond rate (%); Bond term (years); Credit factor; Industry or project risk factor; Underwriting and filing fees ($). Select Calculate to update the results.

How the calculation works

Bond amount is multiplied by the entered annual rate and term, then adjusted for credit and industry risk. Filing and underwriting fees are added.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the modeled bond cost: $8,256.25 on the starting inputs, for the entered term and fees.
  • The base line applies the entered rate to the bond amount over the term: $6,250.00 on the starting inputs.
  • The risk line shows what the entered credit and industry factors add: $1,656.25 on the starting inputs.
  • The monthly and percent lines reframe the total: $688.02 a month and 3.3% of the bond amount on the starting inputs.

Important limitations

Surety pricing depends on bond type, obligee language, personal and business credit, financial statements, experience, collateral and market conditions.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the factors as percentages; the credit and industry fields are multipliers, and 1.1 raises the base by a tenth.
  • Entering a multi-year term by accident; the base scales linearly with the term.
  • Treating the estimate as a quote; surety pricing is set by the provider after underwriting.
  • Leaving the factors at the starting values; they scale the whole base, so stale entries distort the result.

Frequently asked questions

How is the cost computed?

Bond amount times rate times term gives $6,250.00, scaled by the entered factors and plus fees: $8,256.25 on the starting inputs.

What do the factors do?

They multiply the base: 1.1 and 1.15 together add $1,656.25 on the starting inputs.

Is this a real bond premium?

No. It models the entered assumptions; actual premiums come from the surety provider after underwriting.

What this calculator does

The Loan Calculator estimates periodic loan payments and total borrowing cost from the loan amount, interest rate, and repayment term. It is useful for comparing financing options before reviewing a lender's official offer.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Amount financed: the loan principal in dollars. It can be 0.
  • Annual rate (%): the yearly interest rate as a percent. It can be 0.
  • Term (months): the repayment length in whole months. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator spreads the amount financed into equal monthly payments at the entered rate, so each payment covers that month's interest plus part of the principal, and shows the total interest as all payments minus the amount financed.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 20000 financed at 8 over 60 months gives a payment of $405.53, and 60 payments come to about 24,331.67, so the total interest is $4,331.67.

Worked example and scenario testing

A 10,000 loan at 8% annual interest for 3 years produces a monthly payment a little above 313 under standard monthly amortization. Total paid is higher than 10,000 because interest is charged over the repayment period.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the monthly payment: $405.53 on the starting inputs.
  • The interest line ($4,331.67) is everything paid above the amount financed; a longer term lowers the payment but raises this line, because the balance carries interest for more months.
  • The payment assumes the entered rate holds for the whole term; the model has no fees, no variable-rate path, and no early-payoff date.

Accuracy, assumptions, and limitations

Actual loan offers may include origination fees, insurance, taxes, prepayment rules, variable rates, late fees, or lender-specific rounding not included in a basic calculation.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a monthly rate; the field takes the annual rate as a percent, so 8 means 8 a year.
  • Entering the term in years; the field takes months, so five years is entered as 60.
  • Reading the payment as interest-only; each payment also repays principal, which is why the balance reaches zero exactly at the entered term.

Frequently asked questions

How is the monthly payment computed?

The amount financed is amortized over the term at the entered rate: 20000 at 8 over 60 months is $405.53 on the starting inputs.

Where does the total interest come from?

All payments minus the amount financed: about 24,331.67 paid against 20000 borrowed is $4,331.67 on the starting inputs.

What happens at a zero rate?

The payment becomes the amount divided by the number of months, with no interest.

Privacy and browser-based calculations

Only loan amount, rate, and term are required. Never enter account numbers, government IDs, or lender login details.

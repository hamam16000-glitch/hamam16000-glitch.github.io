What this calculator estimates

Estimate deck materials, labor, railing, permits and contingency from planned deck area.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Deck area (square feet): the deck's surface area, length times width including any stairs landing area the quote prices by area.
  • Decking and framing per square foot ($): the materials rate for decking boards and framing, per square foot. Match the rate to the material you plan, composite or wood.
  • Labor per square foot ($): the labor rate per square foot. If a quote gives labor as a project total, divide by the area before entering.
  • Railing length (linear feet): the total railing length. Measure the edges that get railing, not the deck's full perimeter if some sides have none.
  • Railing cost per linear foot ($): the installed railing rate per linear foot for the railing type you plan.
  • Permit and design costs ($): the fixed permit and design total, only when the other figures exclude it.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for footing or site surprises.

Method and assumptions

The model prices materials and labor per square foot of deck area, adds railing per linear foot and the fixed permit amount, then applies contingency to the subtotal. It also divides the total by the area for an all-in per-foot figure.

With the starting inputs: 300 square feet at $18 gives $5,400.00 in materials, 300 at $20 gives $6,000.00 in labor, 45 feet at $65 gives $2,925.00 in railing, permits add $500, and the $14,825 subtotal at 10 percent contingency gives a total of $16,307.50, or $54.36 per square foot.

Reading the result

  • Materials and labor scale with area; railing scales with its own length, so a long narrow deck costs more railing per square foot than a square one.
  • Railing cost is its own line; railing-heavy designs move it fast.
  • Estimated cost per square foot bundles railing, permits and contingency, so it reads above an advertised decking-material rate.

Common mistakes

  • Using a composite material rate while planning wood, or the reverse; materials rates differ widely by choice.
  • Measuring railing as the full perimeter when one side is against the house with no railing.
  • Entering a project-total labor quote as a per-foot rate without dividing by area.
  • Leaving out footings, stairs or demolition when the quote prices them separately; add them to the permit and design field as fixed amounts.

Frequently asked questions

Where do footings and stairs go?

The model has no separate footing or stair fields. Add their quoted fixed amount to the permit and design field, or confirm they are inside the per-foot rates you entered.

Can I compare materials by changing one field?

Yes. Change the materials rate to each quoted material's rate and compare totals. Keep labor the same unless the quote changes it too.

Why is my per-foot result higher than the decking price I saw advertised?

The result includes labor, railing, permits and contingency, not just boards. Advertised decking prices are usually materials only.

Important limitations

Footings, height, stairs, demolition, site access, engineering, material choice and local code can materially change cost.

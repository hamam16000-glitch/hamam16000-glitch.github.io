What this calculator estimates

Calculate a base raised to an exponent plus common related powers and roots.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Base x: the number being raised to a power. It is also reused for the square, cube, square-root-of-magnitude and reciprocal lines.
  • Exponent y: the power. It may be negative, fractional or zero; a negative base with a non-integer exponent may have no real-number result, as the limitations note warns.

Method and assumptions

The calculator raises the base to the entered exponent, then shows the base squared, cubed, the square root of its absolute value and its reciprocal as reference lines.

With the starting inputs 5 and 4: 5 to the power 4 is 625, the square is 25, the cube is 125, the square root of the absolute value is about 2.2360679775 and the reciprocal is 0.2.

Reading the result

  • x raised to y is the headline; the other lines are the most common special cases around it.
  • Square root of |x| uses the absolute value, so it stays real even for a negative base.
  • Reciprocal of x is 1 divided by x; it shows Undefined when x is zero.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping base and exponent; 4 to the power 5 is 1,024, not 625.
  • Expecting a real result for a negative base with a fractional exponent; the limitations note says that combination may produce no real-number result.
  • Entering very large powers and reading a warning as a wrong answer; the limitations note says huge results can exceed browser numeric limits.
  • Typing a zero base and expecting a reciprocal; that line shows Undefined.

Frequently asked questions

What does a zero exponent give?

Any nonzero base to the power 0 is 1, and the calculator follows that rule.

What does a negative exponent mean?

It is the reciprocal of the positive power: 5 to the power -2 is 1 divided by 25, which is 0.04.

Why does the square-root line use the absolute value?

So the line stays a real number when the base is negative; the root of the magnitude is what most readers want alongside a power check.

Important limitations

Large powers can exceed browser numeric limits, and negative bases with non-integer exponents may not produce a real-number result.

Powers, reciprocals and real-domain limits

For base 2 and exponent −3, the power is 1/8 = 0.125. A fractional exponent of a negative base can fall outside the real-valued browser operation used here; it should not be interpreted as zero. The root helper uses the absolute base as labelled, and the reciprocal requires a nonzero base. Huge or extremely tiny powers can fall outside the representable numeric range.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate the initial interest-only payment, later principal-and-interest payment and total interest for an interest-only mortgage.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Loan amount ($): the amount borrowed. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual interest rate (%): the annual rate for both phases in this model.
  • Total loan term (years): the full length of the loan. It must exceed the interest-only period.
  • Interest-only period (years): the opening phase with interest-only payments.

Method and assumptions

During the interest-only period the calculator charges the monthly interest on the full balance: loan times annual rate over 12, with no principal repaid. Afterward it re-amortizes the unchanged balance over the remaining years for the principal-and-interest payment. It also shows the payment increase and the total interest across both phases.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the interest-only payment is 500,000 times 6.5% over 12, which is $2,708.33. After 10 years the full $500,000.00 balance remains, and amortizing it over the remaining 20 years gives $3,727.87, an increase of $1,019.53. Total interest across both phases is $719,687.76.

Reading the result

  • The interest-only payment is the headline: $2,708.33 on the starting inputs. It is the low initial cost, not the lasting one.
  • The later payment ($3,727.87) and the increase ($1,019.53) are the risk the structure carries: the same debt repaid over fewer remaining years costs more per month.
  • Total interest ($719,687.76) exceeds the modeled $637,722.44 for the same $500,000.00 balance at the same constant 6.5% rate amortized over 30 years, because the balance does not fall during the first 10 years; this compares only the two modeled scenarios without fees, and the limitations note says actual products differ in rate adjustments and terms.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting only for the interest-only payment; the later payment is the one that must be affordable for the majority of the term.
  • Assuming the balance falls during the interest-only period; the principal-remaining line shows it unchanged at $500,000.00 on these inputs.
  • Entering the interest-only period as months; both period fields take years.
  • Setting the interest-only period equal to the total term; the page requires a remaining period to amortize over.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the balance stay flat?

Because interest-only payments cover exactly the interest charged each month. Nothing is left to reduce principal, so after 10 years of $2,708.33 payments the balance is still $500,000.00 on these inputs.

Can I pay principal during the interest-only phase in this model?

No. The model holds the payment at interest-only for the period you enter, then amortizes. To approximate voluntary principal payments, shorten the interest-only period or lower the loan amount.

Is the total interest comparable to a normal mortgage?

Yes, and that comparison is the point: run a standard 30-year payment on the same loan and rate elsewhere and compare total interest. This page shows what deferring principal costs.

Important limitations

Many interest-only loans have adjustable rates, balloon provisions or qualification rules that are not modeled. Taxes, insurance, fees and prepayment terms are excluded.

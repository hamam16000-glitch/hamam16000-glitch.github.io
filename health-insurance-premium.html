What this calculator estimates

Estimate annual employee health-plan cost from premiums, employer contributions, expected medical spending, deductible and coinsurance.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Total monthly plan premium ($): the full monthly premium before any employer contribution.
  • Monthly employer contribution ($): the part of the premium the employer pays. It cannot exceed the total premium; the calculator rejects that.
  • Expected covered medical spending ($): the covered medical charges you expect for the year.
  • Annual deductible ($): what you pay in full before coinsurance starts.
  • Coinsurance after deductible (%): your share of covered charges after the deductible, from 0 to 100.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum ($): the most you pay for covered care in a year; the model caps your spending there.

Method and assumptions

The calculator converts your net premium, total minus employer contribution, to an annual cost. Your medical spending is charged in full up to the deductible, then at the coinsurance percentage, capped at the out-of-pocket maximum and at the spending itself. The headline adds both parts, and the employer's annual contribution is shown for context.

With the illustrative starting inputs: your net premium is 300 per month, $3,600.00 a year. Expected spending of 6,000 crosses the 2,000 deductible, so out of pocket is 2,000 plus 20% of the remaining 4,000, which is $2,800.00, under the 7,000 maximum. The estimated annual total is $6,400.00, about $533.33 per month; the employer contributes $6,600.00 a year.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual cost is the headline: your premiums plus your out-of-pocket spending on the entered charges.
  • Out-of-pocket estimate follows the deductible, coinsurance and cap in order; on higher spending it stops at the out-of-pocket maximum.
  • The limitations note says networks, copays, prescriptions and plan rules are not modeled and directs you to the plan's summary of benefits and coverage, which is the document that governs real costs.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the full premium as your cost when an employer contributes; the model nets the contribution out of your premium share.
  • Applying coinsurance to all spending; it applies only past the deductible, which is why 6,000 of spending costs $2,800.00 here, not 1,200.
  • Ignoring the out-of-pocket maximum; raise expected spending and watch the out-of-pocket line stop at the cap while premiums keep the total growing.
  • Treating the estimate as plan advice; real costs follow the plan's networks, copays and rules, as the limitations note says, so review the plan documents before choosing.

Frequently asked questions

Does this compare plans?

It costs out one plan on your entered spending. Run it once per plan with the same expected spending to compare totals side by side.

What happens above the out-of-pocket maximum?

In this model your covered spending stops costing you at the cap; the plan pays the rest of covered care. What counts as covered, and therefore toward the cap, is defined by the plan documents, as the limitations note says.

Why does the employer contribution matter if I never see it?

It changes your real premium share: the model charges you the total premium minus the contribution, and shows the employer's annual $6,600.00 separately so the full premium stays visible.

Important limitations

Networks, copays, non-covered care, prescriptions, family tiers, HSA contributions and plan rules are not modeled. Review the plan summary of benefits and coverage.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate past and future wage losses after a car accident.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Hourly wage ($): your hourly pay rate. It cannot be negative.
  • Work hours missed: the hours of work lost. It cannot be negative.
  • Lost benefits and bonuses ($): the value of benefits or bonuses lost. It cannot be negative.
  • Expected future lost wages ($): earnings you expect to lose going forward. It cannot be negative.
  • Reduction for claimant fault (%): the percentage the claim is reduced for your share of fault, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the hourly wage by the hours missed and adds lost benefits for the past loss, adds expected future losses, then reduces the total by the fault percentage.

With the illustrative starting inputs: past loss is 35 times 160 plus 1,200, which is $6,800.00. With no future loss and no fault reduction entered, the total is $6,800.00.

Reading the result

  • The total is the headline: $6,800.00 on the starting inputs.
  • The past-loss line ($6,800.00 here) separates documented lost time from the future-loss estimate you enter.
  • The fault line shows what the reduction removes; on the starting inputs it is $0.00 because no fault was entered.

Common mistakes

  • Entering salary instead of an hourly rate; the model multiplies the wage by hours, so an annual figure overstates the loss enormously.
  • Counting hours you were paid for; the model multiplies whatever hours you enter, so count only hours actually lost.
  • Entering the fault percentage as a decimal fraction; the field takes a whole percentage figure.
  • Reading the total as a claim outcome; the model applies simple arithmetic to your figures, and actual wage-loss recovery depends on documentation, fault rules and the insurer, so confirm with the insurer or a licensed professional.

Frequently asked questions

What goes into the past-loss figure?

Hours missed times the hourly wage, plus lost benefits: 160 hours at 35 plus 1,200 gives $6,800.00 on the starting inputs.

How does the fault percentage work?

It reduces the combined past and future loss proportionally. On the starting inputs it is 0, so nothing is removed; at 20% the model would remove a fifth of the total.

Is this what I can recover?

The model only computes the arithmetic on your figures. Documentation standards, fault rules and coverage decide real claims, so confirm with the insurer or a licensed professional.

Important limitations

Actual recoverability depends on evidence, mitigation, taxes, benefits, employment history, insurance coverage and applicable law.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate drywall repair cost from damaged area, finish level and the contractor minimum charge. Starting figures are example inputs, not market prices or recommendations; enter your own measurements and written quotes.

How to use it

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results.

  • Damaged square feet: the area being patched or replaced, not the size of the wall it sits in. For several spots, add their areas together.
  • Low cost per unit ($): the lowest per-square-foot repair quote for a simple patch with basic finishing.
  • High cost per unit ($): the highest per-square-foot quote for finishing you would accept, such as texture matching on a visible wall.
  • Minimum charge, texture and paint ($): the fixed part of the visit: the contractor call-out minimum plus texture matching and paint when those are billed separately from the per-foot rate.

How the calculation works

For each end of the range the calculator multiplies the damaged area by one unit rate and then adds the fixed charges once: low end = area x low rate + fixed, high end = area x high rate + fixed. The headline number is the midpoint of those ends, and the per-unit line divides it by your area.

With the starting figures (100 sq ft, $2.50 and $8.00 per foot, $150 fixed): low end = 100 x $2.50 + $150 = $400.00, high end = 100 x $8.00 + $150 = $950.00, midpoint = $675.00, and midpoint per square foot = $6.75.

Reading the result

  • On small jobs the per-unit line will look high. That is the fixed amount you entered divided by few feet, not an expensive rate.
  • A wide range only shows the spread between the rates you entered; check whether the quotes behind them assume the same finish level, since texture matching on a visible wall is a different scope from a plain patch.
  • Compare a contractor flat quote against both ends of the range, not only the midpoint.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the wall, not the damage. Entering the full wall area for a small patch multiplies the rates by area that is not being repaired.
  • Ignoring the fixed charges. If your quotes include a call-out minimum, texture or paint as separate amounts, leaving the fixed field at zero removes them from both ends and understates the job.
  • Assuming paint is inside the rate. Per-foot repair rates often exclude paint and texture matching; add them in the fixed field or the result understates the job.
  • Treating the midpoint as a quote. It is the average of your two scenarios, not a predicted invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a tiny patch cost so much per foot?

Because the fixed amount you entered is divided by very few square feet in the per-unit line. The rate itself may be unchanged; on small areas the fixed part is the larger share of the total in this model.

Is paint included in the result?

Only if you include it. If your quotes bill paint and texture separately from the per-foot repair rate, add them into the fixed field so they appear in both ends. If a quote already includes them, do not add them again.

Does water damage change the estimate?

Water damage may add work before patching, such as treatment or insulation replacement, which this model does not price. Include such work in the fixed field only if you have a quoted figure for it.

Important limitations

Actual project pricing varies by location, materials, labor, access, permits, contractor minimums and scope changes. Use local written quotes before committing.

Interpreting the low/high midpoint

Use the area of surface being repaired, not total room floor area. Patching, matching texture, repainting and resolving the cause of damage can be separate quote items.

For small repairs the fixed charge is the lever, not the rate: at 20 square feet the $150 fixed part contributes more than half of even the low end, while at 100 square feet each extra $1 on a rate moves that end by $100. Expect the per-unit line to fall as the damaged area grows, because the same fixed charge is spread over more feet.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate a manufactured or mobile-home loan payment and total borrowing cost.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Mobile or manufactured home price ($): the agreed price before tax and fees. It can be 0 but the financed amount must stay positive.
  • Down payment or cash contribution ($): cash you put in up front. It cannot be negative.
  • Sales tax or financed assessment (%): the tax percentage applied to the price and financed. It can be 0.
  • Financed fees and closing costs ($): fees rolled into the loan. It can be 0.
  • Annual interest rate (%): the yearly rate on the financed amount. It can be 0.
  • Loan term (years): the repayment period in years. It must be above 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds tax on the price and the financed fees, subtracts the down payment for the amount financed, and amortizes that over the term in months. It reports the monthly payment, the amount financed, the total interest, the total paid, and the cash up front.

With the illustrative starting inputs: tax is 4% of 125,000, which is $5,000.00, so the financed amount is 125,000 plus $5,000.00 plus 4,500 minus 12,500, which is $122,000.00. Over 240 monthly payments at 8.75% the payment is $1,078.13, the stream totals $258,750.50, and interest is $136,750.50.

Reading the result

  • The monthly payment is the headline: $1,078.13 on the starting inputs for the financed $122,000.00.
  • The total interest ($136,750.50) exceeds the financed amount at this rate and 20-year term, which the total line ($258,750.50) makes concrete.
  • The cash line ($12,500.00) is the down payment only; site, setup or transport costs are outside the loan unless you add them to the financed fees.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the term in months; the field takes years, and 20 entered as years means 240 monthly payments.
  • Leaving out financed fees; on the starting inputs they add $4,500.00 to the balance and accrue interest for 20 years.
  • Comparing the payment against rent without the interest line; the long term more than doubles the cost of the financed amount here.
  • Reading the payment as a lender quote; the model amortizes your figures at one flat rate, and manufactured-home financing terms vary widely by lender and structure, so compare against actual quotes.

Frequently asked questions

Why does interest exceed the amount financed?

At 8.75% over 240 payments the balance is outstanding for two decades, so interest accumulates to $136,750.50 on the starting inputs. A shorter term or lower rate cuts it sharply.

Does the model cover chattel versus mortgage structures?

No. It amortizes whatever financed amount your inputs produce at the entered rate. Enter the rate and term that match the structure you are quoted.

What is financed on the starting inputs?

The 125,000 price plus $5,000.00 of tax and $4,500.00 of fees, minus the $12,500.00 down payment: $122,000.00.

Important limitations

Actual rates, taxes, dealer charges, closing costs, insurance, collateral rules, balloon terms and lender underwriting vary. This is not a loan offer.

Read the financed amount before comparing payments

Confirm whether the price includes land, transport, foundation and installation. A home-only loan and a real-estate mortgage need different quoted terms; the calculator does not choose between them.

The model applies the entered percentage tax to the price, adds financed fees and subtracts the cash contribution. A negative financed amount is floored at zero. It rounds years × 12 to a whole number of monthly payments, with payments at each month end. Total loan payments exclude the down payment; total interest is those payments minus the financed principal.

At zero interest, $12,000 financed for 24 months costs $500 per month and $0 interest. A lower payment from extending the term is different from a lower total borrowing cost. Compare the loan offer’s amount financed, interest rate, APR and term as separate figures. CFPB’s loan comparison guidance explains the distinction.

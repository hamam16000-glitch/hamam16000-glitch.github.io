What this calculator estimates

Estimate a foundation repair budget from crack work, piers, waterproofing, engineering, permits and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Crack repair length (linear ft): the total crack length being repaired, from the repair contractor's assessment.
  • Crack repair cost per linear ft ($): the per-foot crack repair rate for the method quoted, such as injection.
  • Number of piers or supports: how many piers or supports the engineered plan calls for. That plan comes from a professional diagnosis, as the limits section stresses.
  • Installed cost per pier ($): the installed price per pier or support.
  • Drainage and waterproofing ($): the fixed amount for drainage correction and waterproofing that the plan includes.
  • Engineering and inspection ($): the fixed amount for the engineering assessment and inspections.
  • Permits and other work ($): the fixed permit and miscellaneous total.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal. The starting 15 percent is the highest in this family, reflecting how often hidden damage changes the plan.

Method and assumptions

The model prices cracks per linear foot and piers per unit, adds waterproofing, engineering and permit amounts as fixed costs, then applies contingency.

With the starting inputs: 40 feet at $45 gives $1,800.00 for cracks, 8 piers at $1,800 give $14,400.00, waterproofing, engineering and permits add $9,500.00, and the $25,700 subtotal at 15 percent contingency adds $3,855.00, for a total of $29,555.00.

Reading the result

  • Piers and supports is usually the dominant line; the pier count comes from the engineered plan, not from guesswork.
  • Crack repairs alone are cheap; the model shows how stabilization and drainage work dominate a real repair budget.
  • Contingency at 15 percent is deliberate: hidden damage is the norm in foundation work, as the limits section explains.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing cracks while ignoring the pier plan; cosmetic crack repair without stabilization does not fix movement.
  • Choosing the pier count yourself; the limits section is explicit that foundation movement requires professional diagnosis.
  • Skipping drainage work that the plan includes, which is often why the foundation moved in the first place.
  • Cutting contingency to zero to lower the estimate; hidden damage is exactly what this buffer is for.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the contingency default higher than other calculators?

Because hidden damage is common once excavation starts on foundation work. It is an example input, not a rule; set it from your engineer's and contractor's guidance.

Can this page tell me what repair my foundation needs?

No. The limits section says it directly: foundation movement requires professional diagnosis. This page prices the plan you enter; it does not design the plan.

Where do I compare two repair methods?

Run the calculator once per method with that method's pier count, per-pier cost and waterproofing scope, then compare totals.

Important limitations

Foundation movement requires professional diagnosis. Soil, structure, access, stabilization method and hidden damage can make actual costs much higher or lower.

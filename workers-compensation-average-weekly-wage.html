What this calculator estimates

Estimate average weekly wage and a replacement-rate benefit from a wage history.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Gross wages in the lookback period ($): the gross wages earned in the lookback period. It can be 0.
  • Weeks in the lookback period: the weeks the lookback covers. It must be between 1 and 52.
  • Included overtime, bonuses or allowances ($): the extra pay the rules include in the wage base. It can be 0.
  • Benefit replacement rate (%): the percentage of the weekly wage the benefit replaces. It can be 0 and cannot exceed 100.
  • Maximum weekly benefit ($): the weekly benefit cap. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds the included extras to the gross wages, divides by the lookback weeks for the average weekly wage, then applies the replacement rate and caps the result at the maximum weekly benefit. It also shows the weekly benefit, the total wage base, and the benefit's effective share of the wage.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the wage base is 26,000 plus 1,800, which is $27,800.00, the average weekly wage is $1,069.23 over 26 weeks, and the benefit at 66.67% is $712.86, which is under the 1,800 cap, so the effective share stays 66.67%.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the average weekly wage: $1,069.23 on the starting inputs, the wage base spread over the 26 lookback weeks.
  • The benefit line ($712.86) applies the replacement rate and the cap; here the cap does not bind, so the effective share (66.67%) equals the entered rate.
  • When the cap does bind, the effective share falls below the entered rate, which is the model's way of showing the cap's bite.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the weekly wage in the gross field; the model divides the period's total wages by the period's weeks, so enter the full lookback amounts.
  • Entering extras the rules exclude; the extras field is only for pay the applicable rules count in the wage base, and including excludable pay overstates the wage.
  • Reading the benefit as the actual payment; the rate, cap and lookback rules vary by jurisdiction and policy, so the actual benefit follows the insurer's and regulator's own rules, which you should confirm with the plan documents or the insurer.

Frequently asked questions

When does the cap change the benefit?

When the rate times the wage exceeds it. On the starting inputs $712.86 is under the 1,800 cap, so the full 66.67% shows through.

Why does the effective share sometimes differ from the rate?

The cap holds the benefit down, so the benefit divided by the wage falls below the entered rate; here it stays 66.67% because the cap does not bind.

What weeks should the lookback cover?

The model accepts 1 to 52 weeks to match the rules that apply; the right lookback follows the jurisdiction's own rules, so confirm them with the insurer or the regulator's guidance.

Important limitations

States use different lookback periods, included pay items, minimums and maximums.

Match wages and lookback weeks

Use wages that belong to the same lookback period as the entered week count. Included overtime or allowances must not also be embedded in gross wages and added a second time. The replacement percentage and weekly cap are user assumptions rather than a determination of a jurisdiction’s benefit rules. A calculation of average wages is separate from a decision about coverage, compensability or benefit duration.

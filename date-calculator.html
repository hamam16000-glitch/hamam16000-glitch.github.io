What this calculator estimates

Add or subtract a number of days from a calendar date and show the resulting date.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Starting year: the start year, a whole value.
  • Starting month (1-12): the start month, from 1 to 12.
  • Starting day: the start day; the date must exist on the calendar.
  • Days to add or subtract: the day offset; negative values move backward. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator shifts the start date by the entered number of days, forward or backward, and reports the resulting date, the start date, the signed offset, and the resulting weekday.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 100 days after July 24, 2026 is November 1, 2026, shown with the start date July 24, 2026, the offset of +100 days, and the landing weekday, Sunday.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the resulting date: 2026-11-01 on the starting inputs, one hundred calendar days after the start.
  • The offset line (+100 days) echoes the signed shift, so a quick glance confirms whether the move was forward or backward.
  • The weekday line (Sunday) helps when the result feeds a plan, since the same offset from a different start day lands on a different weekday.

Common mistakes

  • Entering months or weeks in the day offset; the field counts calendar days only, so convert first.
  • Entering a date that does not exist; the form rejects invalid calendar dates.
  • Expecting business-day behavior; the model counts every calendar day, weekends included.

Frequently asked questions

How do I subtract days?

Enter a negative offset: the model moves backward from the start date by that many days.

Does the count include weekends?

Yes. Every calendar day counts: 100 days from 2026-07-24 crosses about fourteen weeks to 2026-11-01.

What does the offset line show?

The signed shift applied: +100 days on the starting inputs, or a negative figure when subtracting.

Important limitations

The result treats each offset as a calendar day and does not model business days, holidays or local daylight-saving transitions.

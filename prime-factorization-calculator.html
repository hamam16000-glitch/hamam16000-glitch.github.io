What this calculator estimates

Break a positive whole number into prime factors and review divisor information.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Whole number (2-1,000,000,000): the integer to factor. The accepted range is 2 up to one billion, as the limitations note says, so trial division stays fast in the browser.

Method and assumptions

The calculator divides out each prime factor in ascending order, collects them with their exponents, then derives the number of positive divisors from the exponents and sums the distinct prime factors.

With the starting input 360: the prime factorization is 2^3 times 3^2 times 5, the number is not prime, it has 24 positive divisors, and the distinct prime factors 2, 3 and 5 sum to 10.

Reading the result

  • Prime factorization is the headline: the number rebuilt from primes only, and multiplying it back returns 360.
  • Is the number prime answers the common follow-up directly; a prime input shows a single factor with exponent 1.
  • Number of positive divisors comes from adding one to each exponent and multiplying: (3+1) times (2+1) times (1+1) gives 24.

Common mistakes

  • Entering 1, a decimal, or a value above one billion; the calculator accepts only whole numbers from 2 to 1,000,000,000.
  • Reading the exponents as separate factors; 2^3 is the single factor 2 used three times.
  • Summing all factors with repetition for the factor-sum line; that line sums distinct primes only, 2 + 3 + 5, giving 10.
  • Assuming a large input will be slow; the limitations note caps the range precisely so trial division stays fast.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the divisor count use the exponents?

Each divisor picks an exponent from 0 up to the factor's exponent, so the choices multiply: four choices for the factor 2, three for 3 and two for 5 make 24 divisors of 360.

What does a prime input look like?

The factorization is the number itself with exponent 1, the prime check says Yes, the divisor count is 2 and the factor sum is the number.

Can it factor negative numbers or zero?

No; the accepted range starts at 2. Factor the magnitude and track the sign separately.

Important limitations

The input is capped at one billion so trial division remains fast in a browser.

Exponents count repeated prime factors

For 360, the factorization is 2³ × 3² × 5. The distinct-prime sum is 2 + 3 + 5 = 10, not the sum with repeated factors. The positive-divisor count is (3+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 24. A prime number has only itself in the factorization and exactly two positive divisors. This browser routine accepts integers from 2 through 1,000,000,000.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate a home-addition project budget from square footage, construction, design, permits, utility work and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Addition size (square feet): the new floor area being added. Use the planned conditioned area, not the lot coverage.
  • Base construction cost per square foot ($): the per-foot construction rate for the finish level you expect, from a written quote.
  • Architect and engineering fees ($): the fixed total for design and engineering work. Include it exactly once, here or inside a design-build quote.
  • Permits and inspections ($): the fixed total for permits and required inspections.
  • Utility, HVAC and electrical upgrades ($): the fixed amount for extending or upgrading systems for the addition. Confirm whether the per-foot rate already covers this.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for foundation or tie-in surprises.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the addition area by the base rate, adds design, permit and utility upgrade amounts as fixed costs, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the area for a per-added-foot figure.

With the starting inputs: 600 square feet at $275 gives a base of $165,000.00, professional, permit and utility costs add $46,500.00, and the $211,500 subtotal at 12 percent contingency adds $25,380.00, for a total of $236,880.00, or $394.80 per added square foot.

Reading the result

  • Base construction estimate is the area-times-rate line; everything else is fixed.
  • Estimated cost per added square foot includes design, permits, utility work and contingency, so it reads far above a bare construction rate.
  • Estimated contingency at 12 percent is a large line on a large project; it is an example input to set from your own risk picture.

Common mistakes

  • Counting design fees inside a design-build quote and again in the architect field.
  • Assuming the base rate covers HVAC or electrical tie-ins without confirming with the contractor.
  • Using lot coverage instead of the planned conditioned floor area.
  • Comparing the per-foot result to advertised construction rates that exclude design, permits and system upgrades.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the per-foot result so much higher than the construction rate I entered?

Because the total being divided includes design fees, permits, utility work and contingency, not just construction. The bare rate is the construction-only line.

Where does demolition of the existing wall go?

Add the quoted amount to the utility and upgrades or permits field as a fixed cost. The limitations note lists demolition among the items that vary.

Should I model a second-story addition differently?

Only through your inputs: the per-foot rate and fixed costs your quotes give you for that project type. The arithmetic is the same; the rates are not.

Important limitations

Structural conditions, finishes, location, labor, demolition, foundation, weather, financing, code upgrades and contractor pricing vary.

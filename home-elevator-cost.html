What this calculator estimates

Estimate a residential-elevator project cost from equipment, floors served, construction, electrical work, design, finishes and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Elevator equipment package ($): the base equipment package price for the elevator type, from a written quote. Elevator type is a major cost driver, as the limitations note says.
  • Floors served: how many floors the elevator connects. The model adds a per-floor cost for each floor beyond two.
  • Cost per floor beyond two ($): the added equipment cost per additional floor of travel.
  • Shaft and structural construction ($): the fixed amount for building the shaft and structural work, often the largest line after equipment.
  • Electrical and backup power work ($): the fixed amount for electrical and backup power work.
  • Design, engineering and permits ($): the fixed total for design, engineering and permits.
  • Cab and surrounding finishes ($): the fixed amount for the cab interior and surrounding finishes.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for structural surprises in an existing home.

Method and assumptions

The model adds a per-floor charge for each floor beyond two to the equipment package, adds construction, electrical, design and finish amounts as fixed costs, then applies contingency.

With the starting inputs: a $30,000 equipment package plus one floor beyond two at $8,000 gives $38,000.00, construction and electrical add $23,000.00, design, permits and finishes add $9,000.00, and the $70,000 subtotal at 10 percent contingency adds $7,000.00, for a total of $77,000.00.

Reading the result

  • Equipment and added floors combines the package and per-floor travel costs; the floors-served count moves it.
  • Construction and electrical work is the line retrofitting into an existing structure drives hardest.
  • Contingency at 10 percent is the buffer for what opening walls and floors reveals.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the total travel height price as the package and also paying per-floor charges; the per-floor field covers only floors beyond two.
  • Underestimating shaft construction in a retrofit; it is quoted construction work, not an accessory.
  • Forgetting finishes, then wondering why the real quote exceeds the estimate.
  • Ignoring the limitations note: pit, overhead and accessibility code requirements can change the design and price entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Why charge per floor beyond two?

Because travel height adds equipment and rail cost per floor, and the base package is priced for a standard two-stop unit. Three floors means one added floor in this model's arithmetic.

Where does ongoing maintenance go?

It is outside this model, which estimates the project cost. The limitations note lists maintenance as a real ongoing cost to get from the installer.

How do I compare elevator types?

Run the calculator once per type with each quote's equipment package, per-floor cost and construction figures. Type differences live in those inputs, not in the arithmetic.

Important limitations

Elevator type, travel height, existing structure, pit and overhead requirements, power, accessibility code, finishes, permits and maintenance affect actual cost.

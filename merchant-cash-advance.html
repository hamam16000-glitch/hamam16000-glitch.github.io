What this calculator estimates

Estimate merchant cash advance payback, holdback remittance, repayment time and simple annualized cost.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Advance amount ($): the lump sum you receive. It must be greater than zero.
  • Factor rate: the multiplier that sets the payback amount, as a decimal like 1.35. It must be at least 1.
  • Daily sales holdback (%): the percentage of card sales remitted, which the model applies to monthly sales.
  • Average monthly card sales ($): the card volume the holdback draws on. It must be greater than zero.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the advance by the factor rate for the total payback; the financing cost is the payback minus the advance. The estimated monthly remittance is the holdback percentage of monthly card sales, and the estimated repayment time is the payback divided by that remittance. The simple annualized cost estimate spreads the financing cost over the repayment time: cost over advance, times 12 over the number of months, as a percentage. It is a simple annualization, not a regulated APR disclosure.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the payback is 50,000 times 1.35, which is $67,500.00, so the financing cost is $17,500.00. The monthly remittance is 12% of 80,000, which is $9,600.00, giving an estimated repayment time of about 7 months. The simple annualized cost estimate is $17,500.00 over 50,000, times 12 over 7.03 months, which is 59.73%.

Reading the result

  • Total payback amount is the headline: $67,500.00 on the starting inputs. The financing cost ($17,500.00) is fixed the moment the factor rate is set, regardless of how fast sales repay it.
  • Repayment time moves with sales: the holdback is a percentage of card volume, so slower sales stretch the about 7 months estimate and faster sales shorten it, without changing the total cost.
  • The simple annualized cost estimate (59.73% here) exists for comparison shopping, but it is not a regulated APR; actual offers disclose their own terms, and the limitations note directs you to the provider's documents.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the factor rate as an interest rate; 1.35 is a multiplier on the advance, not a 35% annual rate, and the cost is fixed regardless of repayment speed.
  • Entering the factor rate as a percentage like 135 instead of 1.35; the field takes the decimal multiplier.
  • Assuming the holdback is a fixed monthly bill; it is a percentage of card sales, so actual monthly remittances vary with volume.
  • Comparing the simple annualized estimate directly with a loan APR; the annualization is simple, not a regulated disclosure, so use it as a rough scale only.

Frequently asked questions

Why does faster repayment not reduce the cost?

Because the payback is fixed by the factor rate at signing. Speed only changes how long the remittances run, not their total; that is the structural difference from interest, which accrues over time.

What happens if my card sales drop?

The monthly remittance falls with the holdback percentage applied to a smaller base, so repayment takes longer. Enter a lower sales figure to see the new estimated time; the total payback stays the same.

Is the annualized estimate the APR the provider must disclose?

No. It is this page's simple annualization for rough comparison. Providers disclose their own terms, and some jurisdictions require specific disclosures; rely on the provider's documents for the actual figures, as the limitations note says.

Important limitations

A factor rate is not an APR. Daily remittances, changing sales, renewals and additional fees can make the effective annual cost very different.

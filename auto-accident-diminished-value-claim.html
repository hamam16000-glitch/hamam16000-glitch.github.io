What this calculator estimates

Estimate a vehicle diminished-value claim using pre-loss value and adjustable condition factors.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Vehicle pre-accident market value ($): what the vehicle was worth before the accident. It cannot be negative.
  • Base damage severity (%): the starting percentage for the damage, from 0 to 100.
  • Mileage adjustment factor (%): the mileage multiplier, from 0 to 100.
  • Prior-history and condition factor (%): the multiplier for prior history and condition, from 0 to 100.
  • Repair quality factor (%): the multiplier for repair quality, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the severity percentage to the pre-accident value for a base figure, multiplies the mileage, history and repair-quality factors together for a combined adjustment, and applies it to the base for the claim estimate, also showing the adjusted value after the claim.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the base is 10% of 32,000, which is $3,200.00. The factors combine as 70% times 90% times 85%, which is 53.55%, so the claim is $3,200.00 times 53.55%, which is $1,713.60, and the adjusted value is $30,286.40.

Reading the result

  • The claim estimate is the headline: $1,713.60 on the starting inputs.
  • The base ($3,200.00) is what severity alone would claim; the combined 53.55% factor scales it for mileage, history and repair quality.
  • Every factor is one you enter, so the estimate moves directly with your assessments; it is a structured guess, not an appraisal.

Common mistakes

  • Entering factors as decimals; the fields take percentages, so 0.7 reads as 0.7% rather than 70%.
  • Setting severity to the repair cost share instead of the page's damage scale; the model multiplies whatever percentages you enter.
  • Reading the estimate as a settlement figure; the model applies a formula of your own factors, and actual diminished-value outcomes depend on the insurer, the evidence and the jurisdiction, so treat it as a starting point and confirm with the insurer or a licensed professional.
  • Leaving a factor at 100% by default; each one scales the claim, so an unconsidered 100% assumes no reduction from that factor.

Frequently asked questions

How do the factors combine?

They multiply: 70% times 90% times 85% gives 53.55% on the starting inputs, and that combined factor scales the $3,200.00 base to $1,713.60.

Where does the severity percentage come from?

From you. The model applies whatever damage percentage you enter to the pre-accident value; it does not assess the damage itself.

Will an insurer pay this figure?

The model produces an estimate from your inputs only. Diminished-value handling varies by insurer and jurisdiction, so present the supporting evidence and confirm with the insurer or a licensed professional.

Important limitations

Insurer formulas and state claim rules differ; an appraisal or comparable-sales analysis may be required.

The factors are entered assumptions, not a claim valuation

The page applies the selected percentage and adjustment factors to the entered pre-accident value. It does not inspect the vehicle, establish a market comparable or determine whether a claim is payable. Ask what evidence supports each factor before comparing scenarios. Repair cost, a settlement offer and the post-repair change in resale value are different quantities; inserting one as another would change the meaning of the result.

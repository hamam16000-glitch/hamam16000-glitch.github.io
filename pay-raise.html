Quick summary: Calculate the dollar and percentage impact of a salary increase.

Formula, worked scenarios and limitations

Compare two annual amounts on the same basis

Enter current annual salary and proposed annual salary before tax. The form compares cash salary alone. It does not convert an hourly rate into annual pay, add bonuses or put a value on benefits.

Annualize both figures consistently before comparing them. If contracted hours change, a rise in annual salary may still be a fall in hourly pay. Use salary and hourly conversion to examine that separate question.

Calculate the amount before the percentage

Annual change = new salary − current salary. Percentage change = annual change ÷ current salary × 100, provided current salary is positive. The displayed monthly change is the annual change divided by 12.

For 60,000 increasing to 66,000, the annual increase is 6,000, the raise is 10.00% and the average monthly increase is 500. That monthly amount is an annual average, not a promise about the next payslip.

Percentage raises do not simply add

A later 5% raise on 66,000 adds 3,300 and produces 69,300. Relative to the original 60,000, the combined increase is 15.5%, because the second raise applies to the higher salary. Enter 60,000 and 69,300 to check the combined change.

For a reduction from 66,000 to 60,000, the fall is approximately 9.09%, rather than 10%. The reference amount has changed.

Timing and take-home pay

If a 6,000 annual raise starts halfway through a year, this form still reports the full annualized increase. With exactly six months at the higher rate, the extra gross pay for that year would be 3,000 before any other changes.

Taxes, payroll deductions, inflation, overtime, paid weeks and benefits are excluded. A change from zero current pay has a money difference but no defined percentage raise. Use the paycheck planner for a separate calculation using entered deduction assumptions.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the percentage raise: 10.00% on the starting inputs, the increase measured against the 60,000 current salary.
  • The increase line ($6,000.00) is the dollar view of the same change; the percentage and the dollars move together on a fixed current salary.
  • The monthly line ($500.00) spreads the increase across twelve months, the figure to compare against a monthly budget.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the new salary when checking the percentage; the model divides by the current salary, so 6,000 over 60,000 gives 10.00%, not a share of 66,000.
  • Entering hourly rates in the annual fields; both fields are yearly salaries, so convert hourly pay first.
  • Reading the raise as take-home; the model works on gross salary, and the net effect depends on withholdings outside the model.

Frequently asked questions

What if the new salary is lower?

The increase and the percentage go negative, which the model shows as-is; the monthly line then shows the monthly reduction.

Can the current salary be zero?

The model reports that the percentage is undefined at zero starting pay, since a raise percentage needs a base to divide by.

Does the model annualize a monthly raise?

Both fields are annual; a monthly raise of $500.00 matches the monthly line here, so multiply a monthly change by twelve before entering it.

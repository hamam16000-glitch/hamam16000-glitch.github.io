What this calculator estimates

Estimate an FHA-style monthly housing payment with editable mortgage-insurance assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Home price ($): the purchase price. It must be greater than the down payment.
  • Down payment ($): the cash you put down.
  • Interest rate (%): the annual note rate.
  • Loan term: the amortization period from the dropdown: 15, 20 or 30 years.
  • Upfront MIP rate (%): the upfront mortgage insurance premium percentage, financed into the loan in this model. Program rates change; use the current published figure.
  • Annual MIP rate (%): the annual mortgage insurance premium, charged monthly in this model.
  • Annual property tax ($): the yearly property tax, divided by 12.
  • Annual homeowners insurance ($): the yearly insurance premium, divided by 12.

Method and assumptions

The calculator subtracts the down payment for the base loan, adds the upfront program fee to it for the financed loan amount, and amortizes that over the term for principal and interest. The monthly program fee is the annual fee rate on the base loan divided by 12, and taxes and insurance are the annual amounts divided by 12. The headline is the full monthly total.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the base loan is 350,000 minus 12,250, which is 337,750. The upfront fee at 1.75% adds $5,910.63, so the financed loan amount is $343,660.63. Principal and interest over 30 years at 6.5% is $2,172.17, the monthly program fee is $154.80, taxes and insurance add $500.00, and the total monthly payment is $2,826.97.

Reading the result

  • The total monthly payment is the headline: $2,826.97 on the starting inputs, principal and interest plus the program fee plus taxes and insurance.
  • Financing the upfront fee raises the loan amount (to $343,660.63 here) rather than requiring cash at closing; the cost is spread into every payment instead.
  • Program fee rates and rules change and depend on the loan; keep both fee fields aligned with current published program figures and the lender's quote, as the limitations note says.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the upfront fee as part of the down payment; it is a percentage of the base loan, financed on top of it.
  • Using monthly tax or insurance figures; both fields take annual amounts.
  • Forgetting the program fee when comparing with a conventional loan; on these inputs it adds $154.80 every month plus the financed upfront amount.
  • Treating the result as a qualification decision; eligibility and actual terms come from the lender and program rules, as the limitations note says.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my financed amount larger than the price minus down payment?

Because the upfront program fee is added to the loan: 337,750 of base plus $5,910.63 of fee gives the $343,660.63 financed amount on the starting inputs.

Does the monthly program fee ever end in this model?

The model charges it for the whole term. Actual program rules on duration and cancellation vary; the lender's documents and current program guidance govern, as the limitations note says.

Where do closing costs go?

They are outside this model, which covers the monthly payment only: financed loan, principal and interest, program fee, taxes and insurance.

Important limitations

The upfront premium is financed. The annual-premium estimate uses the original base balance and is not a full declining-balance insurance schedule. Enter applicable rates from a current lender quote. The tool does not determine FHA eligibility, mortgage limits, premium duration, closing costs or future tax and insurance changes.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual flood insurance premium from building and contents coverage, editable rates, policy fees and mitigation discounts.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Building coverage ($): the coverage amount for the structure.
  • Annual rate per $1,000 building coverage ($): the yearly rate per thousand dollars of building coverage.
  • Contents coverage ($): the coverage amount for belongings.
  • Annual rate per $1,000 contents coverage ($): the yearly rate per thousand dollars of contents coverage.
  • Annual policy fees ($): flat yearly fees added before the discount.
  • Mitigation discount (%): a percentage credit for mitigation measures, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies each rate per $1,000 of its coverage, adds the fees for the gross premium, subtracts the mitigation discount for the annual premium, and shows the monthly equivalent along with the building and contents components.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the building component is $550.00 and the contents component is $232.50, plus 75 of fees, for a gross of 857.50. The 10% discount removes $85.75, leaving an annual premium of $771.75, about $64.31 per month.

Reading the result

  • Estimated monthly flood insurance premium is the headline; Estimated annual premium sits beside it.
  • Building and contents components show where the premium comes from; on these inputs the building carries most of it.
  • The limitations note is direct: flood zone, elevation and program rules drive real premiums, and this is not an NFIP or private-market quote.

Common mistakes

  • Entering rates per $100; this page's rates apply per $1,000 of coverage, as the field labels say.
  • Applying the discount to the building component only; it applies to the gross premium including fees.
  • Treating the estimate as an NFIP price; the limitations note says explicitly it is not an NFIP or private-market quote, and real premiums follow flood zone, elevation and program rules.
  • Forgetting contents coverage prices separately; the two components use different rates here, 2.2 and 3.1 per $1,000.

Frequently asked questions

Why is contents coverage more expensive per $1,000 here?

Only because the illustrative starting rates say so, 3.1 against 2.2; both rates are your editable inputs, and actual rates come from the flood program or private insurer.

Does standard homeowners insurance cover floods?

Flood is typically a separate policy, which is why this page exists; what your homeowners policy excludes is stated in its own documents.

What does the mitigation discount represent?

A credit for measures that reduce flood damage, entered as your own percentage; whether an insurer offers one and at what size comes from them.

Important limitations

Flood zone, elevation, construction, foundation type, occupancy, claims history and program rules drive real premiums. This is not an NFIP or private-market quote.

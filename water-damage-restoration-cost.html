What this calculator estimates

Estimate water-damage restoration cost from affected area, extraction, drying, demolition, mold treatment, reconstruction and insurance assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Affected area (sq ft): the area the restoration contractor's assessment identifies as affected.
  • Extraction and cleanup per sq ft ($): the per-foot rate for water extraction and cleanup.
  • Drying equipment days: how many days drying equipment runs, from the contractor's plan.
  • Drying and monitoring per day ($): the daily rate for drying equipment and monitoring.
  • Demolition and disposal per sq ft ($): the per-foot rate for removing unsalvageable material.
  • Mold prevention or remediation ($): the fixed amount for mold prevention or remediation in the scope.
  • Reconstruction and finishes ($): the fixed amount to rebuild and refinish. This is often the largest line.
  • Estimated insurance-covered percentage (%): your assumption for how much of the gross the policy pays. The policy documents and the adjuster determine the real figure; the limits section lists exclusions, limits and adjuster decisions as outcome drivers.
  • Insurance deductible ($): your policy's deductible, from the policy documents.

Method and assumptions

The model prices extraction and demolition per square foot and drying per day, adds mold treatment and reconstruction for a gross cost, then estimates an insurer-paid amount as the covered percentage of gross minus the deductible, floored at zero, and shows the remainder as your out-of-pocket cost. The insurance lines are simple arithmetic on your assumptions; the policy and the adjuster determine any actual claim outcome.

With the starting inputs: 900 square feet at $3.50 extraction plus $4 demolition, and 4 drying days at $850, give $10,150.00 for mitigation, mold treatment and reconstruction add $19,800.00 for a gross of $29,950.00. Seventy percent of that is $20,965, minus the $2,500 deductible gives an estimated insurer-paid $18,465.00 and an out-of-pocket of $11,485.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated gross restoration cost is the full project figure before any insurance arithmetic.
  • Estimated insurer-paid amount is the model's simple percentage-minus-deductible estimate; treat it as a budgeting assumption, never as a claim prediction.
  • Extraction, drying and demolition is the mitigation phase; Reconstruction and mold treatment is the rebuild phase, and the rebuild is usually bigger.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the insurer-paid line as what your insurer will pay; only the policy terms and the adjuster determine that.
  • Entering a covered percentage without checking the policy's exclusions and limits, which the limits section lists.
  • Forgetting the deductible applies once, subtracting it per line instead of once against the covered amount.
  • Underestimating reconstruction; mitigation is the start, not the bulk, of a water-damage budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is the insurer-paid amount a claim estimate I can rely on?

No. It is the entered percentage of the gross minus the deductible, nothing more. Policy exclusions, limits, depreciation and adjuster decisions, all listed in the limits section, determine any real claim outcome; your insurer is the only source for that answer.

What if the insurer-paid line is zero?

The covered percentage of gross did not exceed the deductible, so the model's out-of-pocket equals the gross. Check the two insurance inputs before reading into it.

Where do emergency call-out rates go?

In the per-foot and per-day rates you enter. The limits note flags emergency rates; use the rates from the actual emergency quote.

Important limitations

Source category, contamination, hidden moisture, emergency rates, policy exclusions, limits, depreciation, adjuster decisions and rebuilding scope can change outcomes.

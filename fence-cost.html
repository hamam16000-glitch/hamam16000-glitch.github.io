What this calculator estimates

Estimate installed fence cost from linear footage, material and labor rates, gates, removal, permits and contingency.

How to use it

Enter the fence length in linear feet, measured along the whole planned line including returns and corners. Material cost per linear foot is the price of posts, panels or pickets, rails and hardware for one foot in your chosen material. Labor cost per linear foot is the installer charge per foot and varies by region, terrain and material. Add the number of gates with the installed cost per gate, then old fence removal and survey and permit costs as flat dollar amounts. Contingency is a percentage buffer for surprises such as rock, roots or utility conflicts; the starting value of 8 percent is an example input, not a recommendation. Select Calculate to update the results.

How the calculation works

Material cost multiplies length by the material rate: 220 ft x $28 = $6,160. Labor multiplies length by the labor rate: 220 ft x $22 = $4,840. Gates add 2 x $650 = $1,300, and removal and permits add $1,800 + $900 = $2,700, giving a subtotal of $15,000. Contingency then adds 8% of that subtotal, $1,200, for a total estimate of $16,200. The results panel groups removal, permits and contingency into one line of $3,900.

How to interpret the result

The estimate works out to about $73.64 per installed linear foot ($16,200 divided by 220 ft). Comparing that per-foot figure with contractor quotes is more useful than comparing totals, because quotes bundle different items. If a quote lands far below the estimate, check whether it includes removal, permits and gates; if far above, check the material grade, post depth and terrain assumptions. Treat the result as a planning figure: firm pricing needs an on-site quote.

Important limitations

Terrain, corners, post depth, property surveys, material grade, access and local code can substantially affect installed fence pricing.

Common mistakes

Measuring only one straight side and forgetting returns, corners and gate openings understates the length. Setting contingency to zero leaves no room for rock, slope or utility surprises. Entering a retail material price while comparing contractor quotes can distort the comparison, since an installer per-foot rate may already include materials. Removal and permit allowances are flat per project, not per foot, so they matter more on short fences.

Frequently asked questions

Does the estimate include gates?

Yes, as a separate line: each gate adds its installed cost on top of the per-foot material and labor for its opening.

How much contingency should I use?

There is no universal right number; the starting value of 8 percent is an example input, not a recommendation. Raise it for sloped, rocky or hard-to-access sites, and lower it for a simple replacement on flat ground.

Why is my contractor quote different?

Quotes bundle different items and reflect local labor, access, post depth and material grade. Compare per-foot pricing and check whether removal, permits and gates are included.

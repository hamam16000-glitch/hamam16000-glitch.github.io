What this calculator estimates

Estimate PayPal payment-processing fees and the amount to charge or receive after fees.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Payment amount ($): the average payment you receive. It cannot be negative.
  • Number of payments: how many payments the period covers.
  • Percentage fee (%): the processor's percentage rate; the starting value is an illustrative input, and your applicable rate comes from your account's current pricing.
  • Fixed fee per payment ($): the flat per-transaction charge added on top of the percentage.
  • Other total fees ($): any additional period fees, such as currency conversion or monthly charges.
  • Optional target net per payment ($): what you want to keep per payment after fees; the calculator works out the gross amount to request so that net lands on target.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the payment amount by the count for the gross, then totals the fees: the percentage on the gross plus the fixed fee per payment plus any other fees. Net is gross minus fees, and the effective rate divides total fees by the gross. The charge line inverts the fee math on the target net: it divides the target plus the fixed fee by (1 minus the percentage rate), the amount to request so the net lands on target under the entered fee structure.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the gross is 250 times 20, which is $5,000.00. Fees are 3.49% of the gross, which is $174.50, plus 20 times 0.49, which is $9.80, totaling $184.30. Net is $4,815.70, the effective rate is 3.69%, and to net exactly 250 per payment the request line reads $259.55.

Reading the result

  • Total fees is the headline: $184.30 on the starting inputs, across all entered payments.
  • The effective rate (3.69%) sits above the entered percentage because the fixed fee adds a per-payment charge; smaller average payments push it higher.
  • The charge line ($259.55) answers the inverse question: what to bill so the fee structure leaves the target net.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the rate from memory; processing rates vary by payment type, region and agreement, so use the rate your account actually charges when the figure matters.
  • Reading the figures as statement reconciliation; this is a planning estimate, and refunds, currency conversion and cross-border charges follow the provider's own rules.
  • Ignoring the fixed fee when comparing small payments; at a 250 average it adds 0.2 points here, but at a 25 average payment it would add about 2 points.
  • Assuming the charge line guarantees the net; it inverts the entered rate and fixed fee exactly, but any other fees on the payment still come out of the net.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the effective rate higher than the percentage fee?

Because the fixed per-payment fee adds cost that the percentage alone does not show: 184.30 on 5,000 works out to 3.69% on the starting inputs.

How does the charge amount work?

It solves the fee equation in reverse: (250 plus 0.49) divided by (1 minus 0.0349), which is $259.55 on the starting inputs, so after the percentage and fixed fee the net lands on 250.

Are these PayPal's official rates?

The page computes from whatever rates you enter; the starting values are illustrative. Confirm the applicable rates on the provider's current pricing page or your agreement before relying on a result.

Important limitations

Rates vary by product, country, currency, card type, nonprofit status, micropayments, disputes and custom pricing. Taxes and refunds are not fully modeled.

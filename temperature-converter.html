What this calculator estimates

Convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, Kelvin and Rankine temperatures.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Temperature in Celsius: the temperature to convert. It cannot be below absolute zero, -273.15 Celsius, as the limitations note says; the calculator rejects lower entries.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the Celsius value by 9/5 and adds 32 for Fahrenheit, adds 273.15 for Kelvin, and multiplies the Kelvin value by 9/5 for Rankine, then shows the Celsius entry back for reference.

With the starting input 25 Celsius: that is 77 Fahrenheit, 298.15 Kelvin and 536.67 Rankine.

Reading the result

  • Fahrenheit is the headline conversion; Kelvin and Rankine are the absolute scales alongside it.
  • Kelvin equals Celsius plus 273.15, so the two move together degree for degree.
  • Rankine relates to Fahrenheit the way Kelvin relates to Celsius: an absolute scale with Fahrenheit-sized degrees.

Common mistakes

  • Entering Fahrenheit in the Celsius field; 25 Fahrenheit would convert as if it were 25 Celsius, a very different temperature.
  • Forgetting that Celsius-to-Fahrenheit needs both the 9/5 factor and the 32 offset; multiplying alone gives 45, not 77.
  • Entering a value below absolute zero; the calculator rejects it, per the limitations note.
  • Reading Rankine as Kelvin; both are absolute, but 536.67 Rankine and 298.15 Kelvin are different-sized degrees.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no Fahrenheit input?

This page starts from Celsius. From Fahrenheit, subtract 32 and multiply by 5/9 to get Celsius, then use this page for the other scales.

What is absolute zero here?

-273.15 Celsius, the floor the limitations note enforces; Kelvin and Rankine both read zero there.

Where does 273.15 come from?

It is the defined offset between the Celsius and Kelvin scales: zero Celsius is 273.15 Kelvin, and the calculator applies that definition.

Important limitations

The input cannot be below absolute zero.

Offsets make temperature ratios misleading

Celsius and Fahrenheit use different zero points, so 20 °C is not twice as hot as 10 °C in an absolute-temperature sense. For −40 °C, Fahrenheit is also −40 °F, Kelvin is 233.15 K and Rankine is 419.67 °R. Kelvin = Celsius + 273.15; Rankine = Kelvin × 9/5. Absolute zero is −273.15 °C, and this form rejects a lower temperature.

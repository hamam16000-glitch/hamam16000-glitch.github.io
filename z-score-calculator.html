What this calculator estimates

Calculate a z-score, deviation from the mean and approximate normal-distribution percentile.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Observed value: the data point to place within the distribution.
  • Population mean: the mean of the reference distribution.
  • Standard deviation: the spread of the reference distribution. It must be greater than zero, and the calculator rejects zero or negative entries.

Method and assumptions

The calculator subtracts the mean from the observed value and divides by the standard deviation for the z-score, shows the raw deviation, and converts the z-score to an approximate percentile using the normal distribution, stating which side of the mean the value sits on.

With the starting inputs 85, 70 and 10: the deviation is 15, the z-score is 15 divided by 10, which is 1.5, the approximate percentile is 93.3193% and the position is above the mean.

Reading the result

  • Z-score counts standard deviations from the mean; 1.5 means the value sits one and a half spreads above average.
  • Deviation from the mean is the same gap in original units, 15 here.
  • Approximate percentile assumes a normal distribution, as the limitations note warns; 93.3193% means about that share of a normal population falls below the observed value.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a zero or negative standard deviation; the calculator requires it to be greater than zero.
  • Reading the percentile as exact for non-normal data; the limitations note says it is an approximation that assumes a normal distribution.
  • Swapping the observed value and the mean; that flips the sign of the z-score.
  • Confusing the z-score with the percentile; 1.5 is the score, 93.3193% is its approximate percentile.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good z-score?

The score only states position: 0 is exactly the mean, positive is above, negative is below. Whether a position is good depends entirely on context, and the calculator does not judge it.

Why is the percentile approximate?

It comes from a normal-distribution curve fitted to your mean and standard deviation; real data may not be normal, so the limitations note labels it an approximation.

Can I use a sample mean and standard deviation?

Yes, enter them in the same fields; the z-score arithmetic is identical, and only the interpretation changes.

Important limitations

The percentile is an approximation that assumes a normal distribution.

The sign identifies which side of the mean

A value of 60 with mean 70 and positive standard deviation 5 has z = −2. A value of 80 has z = +2. The standard score itself uses only (value − mean) / standard deviation. Interpreting the percentile as an actual population percentile additionally assumes a normal distribution; a z-score alone does not establish that distribution.

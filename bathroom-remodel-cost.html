What this calculator estimates

Estimate bathroom remodeling materials, labor, fixtures, permits and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Bathroom area (square feet): the floor area of the bathroom being remodeled, length times width. Measure only the room being renovated.
  • Materials per square foot ($): the material cost rate for tile, flooring, paint and similar finishes. Use the rate from a written quote or supplier estimate for the finish level you expect.
  • Labor as % of materials and fixtures: labor expressed as a percentage of materials plus fixtures. Ask contractors whether their labor figure is a percentage or a fixed price; convert fixed prices before entering.
  • Fixtures and specialty items ($): the fixed total for the vanity, toilet, tub or shower, faucets and similar items. Include each item exactly once: here, or inside a bundled materials quote, never both.
  • Permits and design fees ($): the fixed permit and design amount for the project, only when the other figures exclude it.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the whole subtotal. Use it for unknowns like water damage or plumbing surprises behind walls.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the area by the materials rate, adds fixtures, applies the labor percentage to that combined base, adds permits and design fees, then applies contingency to the subtotal. It also divides the total by the area for a per-square-foot figure.

With the starting inputs: 80 square feet at $150 gives $12,000.00 in materials, plus $6,000 fixtures makes an $18,000 base. Labor at 40 percent adds $7,200.00, permits add $800, and contingency at 10 percent adds $2,600.00, for a total of $28,600.00, or $357.50 per square foot.

Reading the result

  • Base materials moves with both the area and the materials rate, so a higher-end finish level raises this line directly.
  • Estimated labor is a percentage of materials plus fixtures, not of the total; upgrading fixtures raises the labor line too.
  • Estimated cost per square foot includes fixtures, permits and contingency, so it will read higher than a materials-only rate you see advertised.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a labor quote as a dollar total in a field that expects a percentage of materials and fixtures.
  • Counting fixtures both in the fixtures field and inside a bundled materials rate.
  • Using the whole home's square footage instead of the bathroom's floor area.
  • Reading the per-square-foot result as comparable to a materials-only advertised rate; the result bundles labor, fixtures, permits and contingency.

Frequently asked questions

Why does labor rise when I pick nicer fixtures?

The model computes labor as a percentage of materials plus fixtures, so more expensive fixtures raise the labor line. That mirrors percentage-based labor quotes; if your contractor quoted fixed labor, convert it to the matching percentage of your materials and fixtures total.

Where does a shower conversion go?

Add the tub-to-shower conversion amount to fixtures and specialty items if it is priced as a package, or raise the materials rate and labor percentage if it is priced as materials plus labor. Enter it exactly once.

Is the per-square-foot result a market rate?

No. It is this model's total divided by your area, with your inputs. It includes fixtures, permits and contingency, so it is not comparable to a materials-only rate.

Important limitations

Water damage, plumbing relocation, structural work, accessibility requirements, local labor rates and finish selections can cause large differences.

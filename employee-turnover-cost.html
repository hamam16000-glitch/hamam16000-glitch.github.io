What this calculator estimates

Estimate the cost of replacing an employee from recruiting, vacancy, training, productivity loss and separation expenses.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual salary of departing employee ($): the departing employee's gross yearly salary. It can be 0, though the ratio line then reads 0.00%.
  • Recruiting and hiring cost (% of salary): your estimate of recruiting and hiring cost as a percentage of salary. It can be 0.
  • Onboarding and training cost (% of salary): your estimate of onboarding and training cost as a percentage of salary. It can be 0.
  • Position vacant (weeks): the weeks the seat stays empty. It can be 0.
  • Weekly vacancy or overtime cost ($): the weekly cost of covering the empty seat, such as overtime. It can be 0.
  • New-hire ramp period (months): the months the replacement takes to reach full output. It can be 0.
  • Average productivity gap during ramp (%): your estimate of the output gap while ramping. It can be 0 and cannot exceed 100.
  • Separation, severance and administration ($): the one-off cost of the exit itself. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator prices four buckets: recruiting plus training as the entered percentages of salary, the vacancy as weeks times the weekly coverage cost, the ramp as salary per month times the ramp months times the productivity gap, plus the fixed separation cost. It reports the total and the total as a share of salary.

With the illustrative starting inputs: hiring is 18% plus 12% of 85,000, which is $25,500.00, the vacancy is 8 times 1,800, which is $14,400.00, the ramp is $9,916.67, and adding the 7,500 separation gives $57,316.67, which is 67.43% of salary.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the total turnover cost: $57,316.67 on the starting inputs, the four buckets combined.
  • The bucket lines show where the cost sits: $25,500.00 in hiring, $14,400.00 in vacancy coverage, $9,916.67 in ramp drag, plus the 7,500 separation cost.
  • The ratio line (67.43%) restates the total against the 85,000 salary, the figure usually compared when weighing retention spending against replacement.

Common mistakes

  • Entering recruiting and training as dollar amounts; those two fields are percentages of salary, while the separation field is a dollar amount.
  • Entering the ramp gap as a decimal; the gap field is a percentage from 0 to 100, not a fraction.
  • Treating the vacancy cost as lost output valuation; the weekly field is the coverage cost you enter, such as overtime, not a modeled revenue loss.
  • Reading the total as an exact figure; every percentage and weekly cost is your own estimate, so the model prices the scenario you describe rather than measuring the actual departure.

Frequently asked questions

How is the ramp cost computed?

Salary per month times the ramp months times the productivity gap: $9,916.67 on the starting inputs.

Does the model value lost institutional knowledge?

No. It sums the entered cost buckets only; knowledge loss and morale effects are real but sit outside the arithmetic.

Why express the total as a share of salary?

The ratio, 67.43% on the starting inputs, scales the cost to the role, which is the form usually weighed against retention offers or programs.

Important limitations

Actual turnover cost depends on role scarcity, customer impact, team disruption, overtime, knowledge loss, severance terms and time to full productivity.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual landlord insurance budget from dwelling value, an editable base rate, units, liability, loss-of-rent and deductible assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Insured dwelling value ($): the value insured for the building.
  • Base annual rate per $1,000 of value ($): the yearly rate per thousand dollars of dwelling value.
  • Number of rental units: how many units the property has; units past the first each add the per-unit premium. It must be at least 1.
  • Additional premium per extra unit ($): the flat annual amount added for each unit beyond the first.
  • Liability coverage add-on ($): the flat annual cost of the liability add-on.
  • Loss-of-rent coverage add-on ($): the flat annual cost of the loss-of-rent add-on.
  • Deductible and risk adjustment (%): a percentage adjustment applied to everything, negative for a credit or positive for a surcharge; it must stay above -100.
  • Other annual endorsements ($): any other flat yearly endorsements.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the base rate per $1,000 of dwelling value, adds the per-unit premium for units past the first plus the three flat add-ons, then applies the percentage adjustment to the combined figure. It also shows the monthly equivalent and the cost per unit.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the base premium is $1,912.50, the second unit adds 325, and with liability at 350, loss-of-rent at 425 and other endorsements at 250 the add-ons total $1,350.00. The 5% adjustment lifts the combined figure to $3,425.63, about $285.47 per month or $1,712.81 per unit.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual premium is the headline; Monthly equivalent and Cost per unit restate it for budgeting.
  • Base premium covers the dwelling at the quoted rate; Add-ons total collects the per-unit and flat coverages before the adjustment.
  • The limitations note lists location, construction, claims and underwriting as real pricing inputs, so use insurer quotes for actual figures.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the total per-unit premium for all units; the model adds the per-unit figure only for units past the first.
  • Applying the adjustment only to the base; the percentage applies to the base and the add-ons together here.
  • Entering a surcharge as 105 instead of 5; the field takes the signed percentage adjustment itself, and negative values work as credits.
  • Treating the estimate as a quote; location, construction, age, claims and catastrophe exposure shape actual premiums, per the limitations note.

Frequently asked questions

Can the adjustment be negative?

Yes; enter a negative percentage for a deductible or risk credit, down to but not including -100. A positive value works as a surcharge.

Why does only the second unit onward cost extra?

The model treats the first unit as covered by the base premium and charges the per-unit figure for each additional unit; that is its convention, and insurers price multi-unit properties their own way.

Is loss of rent included automatically?

Only as the flat add-on you enter; what loss-of-rent coverage actually pays is defined by the policy, not this page.

Important limitations

Actual premiums depend on location, construction, age, claims, occupancy, coverage limits, replacement cost, deductible, catastrophe exposure and insurer underwriting.

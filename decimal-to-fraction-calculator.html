What this calculator estimates

Convert a decimal number into a simplified fraction using an adjustable precision limit.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Decimal value: the decimal number to convert, positive or negative.
  • Maximum denominator: the largest denominator the search may use. The starting 10000 is an example; a smaller limit runs faster but may miss a closer fraction, which the limitations note explains.

Method and assumptions

The calculator tries every denominator from 1 up to the entered limit, keeps the numerator and denominator with the smallest error, and reports that fraction, its decimal value and the remaining error, plus a mixed-number display when the fraction is improper in absolute value.

With the starting inputs 0.625 and a limit of 10000: the best fraction is 5/8, the decimal is 0.625 and the error is 0, because 5 divided by 8 is exactly 0.625. The mixed-number display stays 5/8 because the fraction is proper.

Worked example: a separate negative input

This second example uses the input -0.625, separate from the starting input 0.625 above. -0.625 converts exactly to -5/8. It is a negative proper fraction, so its mixed-number display remains -5/8, not a positive 0 5/8.

Reading the result

  • Fraction is the closest match the search found within the denominator limit.
  • Decimal shows the fraction converted back, so you can compare it with your entry.
  • Error is the gap between your entry and the found fraction; 0 means the match is exact, as with 0.625 and 5/8.

Common mistakes

  • Setting a tiny denominator limit and expecting an exact match for a long decimal; the limitations note says the limit can miss the intended fraction.
  • Reading a nonzero error as a bug; some decimals are approximations of repeating fractions, so a small error is the honest answer.
  • Expecting a whole part in the mixed display for a proper fraction; 5/8 stays 5/8 because its absolute value is below one.
  • Forgetting the sign; the preserved worked example shows -0.625 converting to -5/8, with the sign kept at the front.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my repeating decimal not come out exact?

A decimal like 0.333... as typed is a finite approximation. The calculator finds the closest fraction within the limit and shows the small error; the limitations note says the limit is not a guarantee of recovering the intended fraction.

What does the denominator limit actually change?

Only how far the search goes. A larger limit can find closer or exact matches for awkward decimals; a smaller one answers faster with coarser matches.

How are negative decimals handled?

The sign is carried at the front of the fraction, as the worked example's -5/8 shows; the magnitude is converted the same way as a positive entry.

Limitations

Some decimal inputs are approximations to repeating fractions. The displayed error measures the approximation used. A denominator search limit is not a guarantee of recovering an intended exact fraction from every rounded decimal.

A bounded approximation, not an exact decimal expansion

The search chooses a close fraction whose denominator is at most the entered limit, up to 100,000. For 0.333333 with limit 100, 1/3 is close but differs by about 0.000000333333. A repeating fraction can therefore be a better short representation without being identical to the input. The approximation-error output quantifies the difference; component-size limits protect integer precision.

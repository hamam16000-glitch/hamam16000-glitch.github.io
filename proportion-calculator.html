    Using this calculator

    Use this form when two ratios are intended to be equal and the missing value is the numerator of the second ratio. Enter a, b and c in the displayed equation a/b = x/c. Denominators must be positive. This fixed arrangement makes the unknown and the reference quantities explicit.

    Calculation method

    a / b = x / c
    x = (a / b) × c

    Dividing a by b gives the quantity per one reference unit. Multiplying that ratio by c scales the quantity to the new reference amount. The calculation assumes direct proportionality: doubling the reference amount doubles the numerator.

    Worked examples

    Scale a recipe

    If 3 cups of an ingredient serve 4 equal portions, 20 portions need 3 ÷ 4 × 20 = 15 cups under the same recipe ratio.

    Keep unit order consistent

    If a drawing uses 2 cm for 5 m, enter a = 2, b = 5 and c = 12 to find the drawn length for 12 m: 4.8 cm.

    Understanding the result

    The output inherits the numerator’s unit. The b and c inputs must share a reference unit, and any required unit conversion must happen before entry. A negative numerator can represent a signed quantity; it does not establish that a real recipe, price or distance can be negative.

    Boundaries and assumptions

    This is a direct-proportion model, not an inverse-proportion solver or a general equation parser. Quantity discounts, fixed setup charges and curved relationships do not follow a constant ratio. Inputs are limited to magnitudes of 10¹², with positive denominators, and results beyond 10¹⁸ are rejected. A zero numerator produces a zero missing numerator.

    Questions and reference

    Can I solve for a denominator?

    Rearrange your known relationship into the displayed a/b = x/c form first. This form always returns x.

    Why does a fixed delivery fee break the model?

    A total containing a fixed fee does not scale in direct proportion to quantity.

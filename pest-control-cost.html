What this calculator estimates

Estimate first-year pest-control cost from initial treatment, recurring visits, specialty treatment, exclusion work and service discounts.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Initial inspection and treatment ($): the one-time charge for the first visit, including inspection. Use the initial-visit line from a written quote.
  • Recurring visits per year: how many follow-up visits the plan includes in one year. Match this to the quoted plan, not to a guess.
  • Cost per recurring visit ($): the price of each follow-up visit. If the plan is priced annually, divide by the number of visits instead of double-entering.
  • Termite, rodent or specialty treatment ($): the fixed amount for any specialty treatment beyond general pest service. Enter zero if none is needed.
  • Sealing and exclusion work ($): the fixed amount for sealing entry points or exclusion work, only when quoted separately.
  • Annual-plan discount (%): the discount percentage the provider applies for an annual plan, from the quote itself. The model applies it to the whole gross.

Method and assumptions

The model adds the initial treatment to the year's recurring visits, adds specialty and exclusion work, then subtracts the annual-plan discount percentage from that gross. It also divides the net by twelve for an average first-year monthly figure.

With the starting inputs: initial $350 plus 4 visits at $125 gives $850.00 in service, specialty and exclusion add $1,550.00, the $2,400 gross at an 8 percent discount removes $192.00, and the net is $2,208.00, averaging $184.00 per month in the first year.

Reading the result

  • Initial and recurring service is the line the visit count moves; each added visit adds one visit cost.
  • Estimated annual-plan discount is a percentage of the gross, so more specialty work also grows the discount.
  • Average monthly first-year cost spreads the whole first year, including one-time work, over twelve months; later years cost less if they only repeat the visits.

Common mistakes

  • Entering an annual plan price as the per-visit cost, multiplying the plan by the visit count.
  • Applying a discount percentage that was quoted only for the service line to a model that applies it to the whole gross.
  • Leaving specialty treatment at the default when only general service is needed.
  • Reading the monthly average as the ongoing monthly rate; it includes one-time first-year work.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the first-year monthly average higher than the plan's monthly price?

The average spreads the initial treatment, specialty work and exclusion over twelve months along with the visits. A plan's advertised monthly price usually covers recurring visits only.

Where does a termite warranty or renewal fee go?

Add it to the specialty treatment field as a fixed amount for the year being modeled. Check the quoted terms for what the fee covers.

What discount should I enter?

Only the percentage the provider wrote into the quote for the annual plan. If no discount was quoted, enter zero.

Important limitations

Pest type, infestation size, property size, treatment method, warranties, follow-up frequency and regional pricing affect actual quotes.

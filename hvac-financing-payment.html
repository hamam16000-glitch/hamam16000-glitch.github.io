What this calculator estimates

Estimate the monthly payment and total financing cost for an HVAC project.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • HVAC project price ($): the installed price of the system: equipment plus labor and materials, from the contractor's quote.
  • Rebates and incentives ($): manufacturer, utility or program rebates that reduce the price before financing.
  • Down payment ($): cash paid upfront; it stays outside the loan.
  • Annual financing rate (%): the APR on the financing offer, from 0 to 100.
  • Financing term in months: the repayment length, from 1 to 240.
  • Financing fees ($): origination or dealer fees; the model adds them to the financed amount.

Method and assumptions

The calculator takes the project price, subtracts the rebates and the down payment, adds the financing fees, and amortizes that financed amount over the term at the annual rate for the monthly payment. The total cost adds the down payment to all monthly payments, and the financing-cost line nets the total against the price after rebates.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the financed amount is 17,000 minus 1,500 minus 2,000 plus 350, which is $13,850.00. Over 84 months at 9.5% the payment is $226.36. The total is 84 payments plus the down payment, which is $21,014.59, and financing costs $5,514.59 beyond the $15,500.00 price after rebates.

Reading the result

  • The monthly payment is the headline: $226.36 on the starting inputs, covering the financed amount over the entered term.
  • The financing-cost line ($5,514.59) is what using the financing adds over paying the after-rebate price in cash, on these inputs.
  • The total-cost line ($21,014.59) includes the down payment, so it reads as the full outlay over the financing period, not only the loan's payments.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the equipment-only price; the field expects the full installed quote or the financed amount comes out low.
  • Leaving out rebates you qualify for; they cut the financed amount directly, dollar for dollar.
  • Comparing offers by monthly payment alone; a longer term lowers the payment but usually raises the financing-cost line.
  • Treating the financing cost as the system's efficiency savings; the model prices the loan only, and energy savings or warranty terms are outside it, as the limitations note says.

Frequently asked questions

What if I pay cash?

Set the financed portion to zero by entering a down payment equal to the price minus rebates: the payment line reads $0.00 and the financing-cost line shows $0.00.

Why does the fee raise the payment?

Because the model finances the fees with the project: they are inside the $13,850.00 on the starting inputs, not paid separately.

Can I shorten the term to save?

Yes, on this arithmetic: fewer months at the same rate means less interest. Try a shorter term on the starting inputs and compare the financing-cost line before deciding what fits the budget.

Important limitations

Promotional deferred-interest terms, contractor financing and tax credits may work differently.

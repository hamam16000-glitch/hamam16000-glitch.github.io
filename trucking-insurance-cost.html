What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual commercial-trucking insurance budget from truck count, base premium, operating risk, driver history, deductible and endorsements.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Insured trucks: how many trucks the policy covers; the base premium multiplies by this. It must be greater than zero.
  • Base annual premium per truck ($): the starting yearly premium for one truck.
  • Operating and cargo risk factor: a multiplier for operations and cargo, where 1 means no adjustment. It must be greater than zero.
  • Driver-history surcharge (%): a percentage surcharge for driver records.
  • Deductible discount (%): a percentage credit, from 0 to 100.
  • Fleet endorsements and fees ($): flat endorsements and fees added at the end.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the per-truck base by the truck count and the risk factor, applies the driver surcharge and the deductible discount in order, then adds endorsements and fees. It also shows the monthly equivalent, the net adjustment, and the cost per truck.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 5 trucks at 10,000 each times the 1.25 factor gives $62,500.00. The 10% surcharge and 5% discount bring it to 65,312.50, an adjustment of $2,812.50, and adding 2,500 of endorsements gives a total of $67,812.50, about $5,651.04 per month or $13,562.50 per truck.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual premium is the headline; Monthly equivalent divides it by 12.
  • Net adjustment shows what the surcharge and discount together add over the factored base: $2,812.50 here, with the surcharge outweighing the discount.
  • Cost per truck restates the total across the fleet; the limitations note lists authority, radius, commodities and driver records as real pricing inputs, so use insurer quotes for actual figures.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the surcharge and discount to the per-truck base before multiplying; they apply to the fleet-wide factored figure here, which changes rounding but not the structure.
  • Reading the surcharge and discount as offsetting; 10% up then 5% down nets to 4.5% up, because they compound, which is why the adjustment is $2,812.50.
  • Entering the total fleet premium as the base; the base field is per truck and multiplies by the count.
  • Treating the estimate as a quote; authority, radius, commodities, loss runs, filings and underwriting shape actual premiums, per the limitations note.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an actual trucking-insurance quote?

No. Actual premiums depend on authority, radius, commodities, vehicle value, driver records, loss runs, limits, filings, state, mileage and underwriting, as the limitations note says; this is a budgeting model with editable rates.

Why do the surcharge and discount compound?

The model applies them in sequence to the factored fleet premium: first the surcharge percentage, then the discount percentage on the result.

Does the per-truck figure change with fleet size?

It stays flat in this model because the base multiplies linearly; only the flat endorsements spread differently. Real fleets often price non-linearly, which the limitations note's factors cover.

Important limitations

Actual premiums depend on authority, radius, commodities, vehicle value, driver records, loss runs, limits, filings, state, mileage and insurer underwriting.

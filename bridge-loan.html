What this calculator estimates

Estimate interest-only payments, lender fees and total payoff for a short-term real-estate bridge loan.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Bridge loan amount ($): the amount borrowed. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual interest rate (%): the annual rate; the model charges interest-only monthly payments.
  • Loan term (months): the length of the bridge. It must be at least 1 month.
  • Origination fee (%): a one-time percentage of the loan, charged at closing in the model.
  • Exit fee (%): a one-time percentage charged at payoff.

Method and assumptions

The calculator treats the bridge as interest-only: the monthly payment is the loan times the annual rate over 12, with no principal repaid during the term. Total interest is that payment times the months. Origination and exit fees are percentages of the loan amount, and the total payoff adds the principal, all interest and both fees.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the monthly interest-only payment is 250,000 times 10.5% over 12, which is $2,187.50. Over 12 months interest totals $26,250.00. The 2% origination fee is $5,000.00 and the 0.5% exit fee is $1,250.00, so the estimated total payoff and financing cost is $282,500.00.

Reading the result

  • The monthly interest-only payment is the headline: $2,187.50 on the starting inputs, the carrying cost to budget during the bridge.
  • The total payoff ($282,500.00) is what leaving the loan actually costs on these inputs: principal plus interest plus fees, the number to compare against the benefit of bridging.
  • Fees are flat percentages here, so they weigh more on shorter terms; on a 12-month bridge the $6,250.00 of fees is a quarter of the interest, but on a bridge of only a few months it would exceed it.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting the monthly payment to reduce the balance; this model is interest-only, and the full principal is due at payoff.
  • Forgetting the exit fee; both fee lines apply, on entry and on exit.
  • Entering the rate as a monthly figure; the field takes the annual rate.
  • Comparing the interest-only payment with an amortizing loan's payment; the bridge defers principal, which is why the payoff line matters more than the monthly line.

Frequently asked questions

When does the principal get repaid?

At the end, in one payoff, in this model. The monthly payment covers interest only, which is how most bridge products are structured; your loan documents govern the actual schedule, as the limitations note says.

Why show fees separately if the payoff includes them?

So you can see what the loan costs beyond interest. On the starting inputs origination and exit fees add $6,250.00, money owed regardless of how long you hold the loan (the interest is the part that scales with time).

Can I model a shorter bridge?

Yes. Lower the months and the interest line shrinks while the fees stay fixed, which shows why short bridges are mostly a fee decision.

Important limitations

Bridge loans may include minimum interest, appraisal, legal, extension, draw and default charges. The calculator does not model changing balances or cross-collateralization.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate home electric-vehicle charger installation cost from equipment, wiring distance, labor, panel upgrades, permits and incentives.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Level 2 charger equipment ($): the price of the charger unit itself, before installation.
  • Base electrical installation labor ($): the base labor amount for the installation, from a written quote.
  • Panel-to-charger wiring distance (ft): the wiring run length from the panel to the charger location. Distance drives the wiring line.
  • Wiring and conduit cost per ft ($): the per-foot installed rate for wiring and conduit.
  • Panel or service upgrade ($): the fixed amount for a panel or service upgrade if the existing service cannot support the charger. Enter zero if the panel has capacity.
  • Permit and inspection cost ($): the fixed permit and inspection amount.
  • Mounting, trenching or repairs ($): the fixed amount for mounting, trenching or repairs, such as for a detached garage run, which the limitations note flags.
  • Utility, state or federal incentives ($): the incentive amount you have confirmed you qualify for, from the program's own documents. Eligibility rules apply, as the limitations note says.

Method and assumptions

The model prices wiring per foot of run, adds the charger, base labor, panel upgrade, permit and mounting amounts for a gross installed cost, then subtracts confirmed incentives. The result is floored at zero.

With the starting inputs: 45 feet at $18 gives $810.00 for wiring, charger $650, labor $900, panel upgrade $2,500, permit $250 and mounting $500 give a gross of $5,610.00, and subtracting $1,000.00 in incentives gives a net of $4,610.00.

Reading the result

  • Wiring and conduit scales with the run length; a longer panel-to-charger distance adds cost per foot.
  • Panel and other upgrades combines the panel field and the mounting field; a service upgrade dominates it.
  • Incentives applied subtracts only what you entered; confirm eligibility in the program documents before trusting the net.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring straight-line distance instead of the actual wiring route, which is usually longer.
  • Assuming the panel has capacity without an electrician confirming it; the upgrade field exists for that answer.
  • Entering unconfirmed incentives; the limitations note lists eligibility as a factor.
  • Forgetting trenching for a detached garage, then seeing it on the real quote.

Frequently asked questions

What if my incentives exceed the gross cost?

The result floors at zero; the model never shows a negative cost. A zero result means the entered incentives exceed the entered project cost.

How do I model a charger on a detached garage?

Use the real wiring route length, add the trenching quote to the mounting and trenching field, and check the limitations note: detached garages are called out as a cost factor.

Where does the carmaker's charger discount go?

In the incentives field, but only the amount confirmed in the offer's own terms.

Important limitations

Electrical capacity, charger amperage, trenching, detached garages, local code, utility programs and incentive eligibility can materially change cost.

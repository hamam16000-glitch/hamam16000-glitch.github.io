Quick summary: Calculate return on ad spend from attributed revenue and advertising cost.

Formula, worked scenarios and limitations

Use revenue that belongs to the same advertising period

Enter revenue attributed to the ads and advertising spend measured on a consistent basis. The form cannot verify attribution, reconcile different platforms or identify purchases credited by more than one channel.

Return on ad spend is revenue divided by ad spend. The ratio is displayed as a multiple and as a percentage: 5.00x means 5 of attributed revenue per 1 spent, or 500%. It does not mean a 500% profit.

Reconcile the default numbers

Revenue of 50,000 and spend of 10,000 give 5.00x ROAS. Subtracting the ad spend leaves 40,000 before product, fulfilment, payment, return and overhead costs.

The required contribution margin shown is spend divided by revenue, or 20% in this example. If the contribution available before advertising were exactly 20% of 50,000, it would equal 10,000 and be fully consumed by the ad spend. Other excluded costs could still leave a loss.

Two campaigns can have the same ROAS and different outcomes

At 50,000 revenue and 10,000 ad spend, a 30% contribution before advertising gives 15,000 to cover ads, leaving 5,000 before other excluded costs. A 15% contribution gives only 7,500, leaving a 2,500 shortfall after ads. Both cases have the same 5.00x revenue ratio.

Refunds, repeat orders, conversion lag and a changed product mix can alter the comparison. Document whether entered revenue includes tax, shipping and refunds before comparing a platform report with business receipts.

Zero values and related measures

Positive spend with zero revenue produces zero ROAS and a shortfall equal to spend before other costs. A break-even margin based on zero revenue is undefined. Zero ad spend also leaves the ROAS division undefined.

Use CPA to examine cost per attributed acquisition or profit margin to compare a consistently defined cost with revenue. This form does not forecast future campaign performance or recommend a budget.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the return multiple: 5.00x on the starting inputs, 50,000 of attributed revenue against 10,000 of spend.
  • The profit line ($40,000.00) subtracts only the ad spend; product, delivery and overhead costs still come out of it.
  • The break-even margin (20.00%) is the gross margin at which the attributed revenue exactly covers the spend; below it, the campaign loses money even before other costs.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the multiple as profit; 5.00x describes revenue over spend, and the $40,000.00 line still carries every non-advertising cost.
  • Attributing all revenue to ads; the model divides whatever revenue you enter, so a loose attribution flatters the return.
  • Comparing the percentage with ROI; 500.00% restates the same revenue-over-spend ratio, while a return on investment nets out more costs.

Frequently asked questions

What does the break-even margin mean?

It is the gross margin that exactly covers the spend from the attributed revenue: 20.00% on the starting inputs. Above it the campaign clears its ad cost; below it, it does not.

What if the spend is zero?

The model needs a positive spend to divide, so enter the actual advertising cost.

Is a higher multiple always better?

Not by itself. The model only computes the ratio from your figures; a high multiple on low-margin revenue can still lose money after other costs.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate annual home-warranty cost and compare it with expected repair spending using plan premiums, service fees, coverage percentage and exclusions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual warranty plan premium ($): the yearly plan price from the actual contract's terms.
  • Optional coverage add-ons ($): the yearly price of any add-on coverage you are considering.
  • Service-call fee ($): the fee paid per service claim, from the contract.
  • Expected service claims per year: how many claims you expect in a year. This is your assumption; the limits section notes actual repair frequency determines value.
  • Average repair or replacement cost per claim ($): your assumption for what an average claim would cost out of pocket.
  • Estimated covered percentage of repair cost (%): your assumption for how much of a repair the plan would pay, after caps and exclusions. The contract's coverage caps and exclusions determine the real figure; the limits section lists them.
  • Expected excluded or uncovered costs ($): your assumption for yearly costs the plan would not cover.

Method and assumptions

The model builds two expectation figures from your assumptions: the yearly cost with the warranty (premium, add-ons, service-call fees, the uncovered share of repairs, plus excluded costs) and without it (all repairs plus excluded costs), then subtracts one from the other for an expected savings or loss. Every figure depends on the assumptions you enter; the contract's caps, waiting periods and exclusions determine what actually happens, as the limits section says.

With the starting inputs: 3 claims at $850 total $2,550 in repairs, of which 75 percent, $1,912.50, is assumed covered. The with-warranty cost is premium $720, add-ons $180, three service fees at $100, the uncovered $637.50 and $300 excluded, totaling $2,137.50, against $2,850.00 without, an expected saving of $712.50 per year.

Reading the result

  • Expected cost without warranty is the repair assumptions plus excluded costs; it rises one-to-one with your claims assumptions.
  • Expected covered repair value is what the plan is assumed to pay; the covered percentage drives it entirely.
  • Estimated annual savings or loss is positive when the assumed coverage beats the premiums and fees; flip any assumption and it can go negative, which is the honest point of the model.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the expected savings as a prediction; it is arithmetic on your own assumptions, and the contract terms determine real outcomes.
  • Assuming 100 percent coverage; caps and exclusions in the contract limit what claims actually pay.
  • Forgetting the service-call fee applies per claim, so more assumed claims cost more fees.
  • Entering optimistic claims assumptions to justify the plan, or pessimistic ones to reject it; run both ends and look at the spread.

Frequently asked questions

Is a positive savings a recommendation to buy?

No. The result is arithmetic on assumptions you entered. Coverage caps, waiting periods, denied claims and exclusions, listed in the limits section, are in the contract documents, and they determine the real value.

Why does the model need a covered percentage?

Because plans rarely pay the full repair cost. The percentage stands in for caps and exclusions; estimate it from the contract's actual terms, not from marketing.

What if I expect no claims?

Enter zero claims and the model shows the premium plus add-ons against zero repair value: the expected loss of a claim-free year. That is a legitimate scenario to look at alongside claim-heavy ones.

Important limitations

Coverage caps, waiting periods, denied claims, appliance age, contractor availability, exclusions and actual repair frequency determine value.

What this calculator estimates

Student Loan Refinance Calculator

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Student loan balance ($): what you owe today. It must be above 0.
  • Current interest rate (%): the annual rate on the existing loan. It can be 0.
  • Current remaining term (years): the years left on the existing schedule. It must be above 0.
  • New refinance rate (%): the annual rate on the new loan. It can be 0.
  • New refinance term (years): the repayment period of the new loan. It must be above 0.
  • Refinance fees ($): fees for the new loan, which the model finances by adding them to the balance. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator amortizes the current balance over the remaining term at the current rate, then amortizes the balance plus the fees over the new term at the new rate. It compares the two monthly payments and the two total costs, and notes that entered fees are financed rather than paid up front.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the current payment is $696.65 and the new payment is $643.75, a saving of $52.90 a month. Total cost falls by $6,348.08 over the life of the loans.

Worked example

For a 12,000 balance at 0% over two years, the payment is 500. Financing 1,200 in fees at the same rate and term raises the payment to 550 and total payments by 1,200.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the monthly difference: $52.90 saved on the starting inputs.
  • The lifetime comparison ($6,348.08 saved) is the fuller view, because a longer new term can cut the payment while raising the total cost.
  • The model finances entered fees by adding them to the new balance, and says so on the break-even line; there is no separate up-front fee case.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing only monthly payments; extending the term can lower the payment while raising lifetime cost, which the interest line exposes.
  • Entering fees as cash paid; the model adds them to the new balance, so they accrue interest.
  • Entering the original term instead of the remaining term; the current schedule starts from today's balance and remaining years.
  • Reading the saving as a lender offer; the model amortizes your figures at flat rates, and actual refinance pricing comes from the lender's own quote, so compare against real offers.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the $52.90 come from?

The difference between the two amortized payments: $696.65 on the current loan against $643.75 on the new one, at the starting inputs.

Why compare lifetime cost too?

A lower payment can hide a longer schedule. On the starting inputs both terms are 10 years, so the lower rate saves $6,348.08 overall; with a longer new term that figure could shrink or reverse.

How are fees handled?

They are added to the new balance and financed, and the model says exactly that on the break-even line. With 0 entered on the starting inputs there is nothing to recover.

Limitations

Different terms can lower the payment while increasing total cost. Federal repayment protections, forgiveness, variable rates, discounts and tax effects are excluded. Total-payment savings are not necessarily interest-only savings.

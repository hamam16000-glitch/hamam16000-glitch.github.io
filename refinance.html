What this calculator does

The Mortgage Refinance Calculator compares an existing mortgage with a proposed replacement loan. It can estimate changes in monthly payment, interest cost, and the time required to recover refinance closing costs.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current loan balance ($): the principal still owed. It must be greater than 0.
  • Current interest rate (%): the existing yearly rate. It can be 0.
  • Remaining term (years): the years left on the current loan, at least 0.1.
  • New interest rate (%): the yearly rate on the replacement loan. It can be 0.
  • New loan term (years): the replacement loan's length, at least 0.1.
  • Closing costs ($): the one-time cost of refinancing. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator amortizes the balance twice, over the remaining term at the current rate and over the new term at the new rate, compares the two payments, and totals each path's payments over its full schedule, adding the closing costs to the new path.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 250000 at 7 with 25 years left costs $1,766.95 a month, while 6 over 20 years costs $1,791.08, so the monthly change is -$24.13 and the total comparison favors the new loan by $95,225.76.

Worked example and scenario testing

If refinancing costs 4,000 and lowers the monthly payment by 160, the simple break-even period is 25 months. Selling or refinancing again before then may prevent those upfront costs from being recovered.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the monthly change: -$24.13 on the starting inputs, meaning the new payment is higher.
  • The monthly line and the total line can disagree, and do here: the payment rises while the total falls $95,225.76, on the combined effect of the lower entered rate, the schedule clearing the balance 5 years sooner, and the closing costs charged to the new path; the model weighs all three, not the term alone.
  • The break-even line says no monthly break-even on these inputs, because it only divides costs by a monthly saving, and a higher payment has none; it does not weigh the shorter schedule.

Accuracy, assumptions, and limitations

The estimate may not include taxes, escrow changes, prepaid interest, mortgage insurance, lender credits, opportunity cost, variable rates, or tax consequences.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the monthly change as the whole verdict; the starting inputs show a higher payment alongside a much lower total, so both lines need reading.
  • Entering the original loan term instead of the years remaining; the current path amortizes over the remaining term only.
  • Reading the total savings as cash received; it is the difference between two full payment schedules, $95,225.76 on the starting inputs, not a payout.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the monthly change negative?

The new payment is higher: $1,791.08 against $1,766.95 on the starting inputs, so the change shows as -$24.13.

How can the total favor a higher payment?

The lower entered rate and the shorter entered term together outweigh the higher monthly amount over the full schedules, even after closing costs: $95,225.76 on the starting inputs.

How is each payment computed?

The balance is amortized at each rate over its own term: 250000 at 7 over 25 years is $1,766.95 and at 6 over 20 years is $1,791.08 on the starting inputs.

Privacy and browser-based calculations

Only mortgage scenario values are needed. Do not enter account numbers, Social Security numbers, or lender credentials.

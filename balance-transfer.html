What this calculator estimates

Estimate payoff time and savings from a credit card balance-transfer offer.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Balance to transfer ($): the card balance you would move. It must be greater than zero.
  • Current card APR (%): the annual rate on the existing card.
  • Monthly payment ($): the fixed monthly amount you would pay on either path. It must be greater than zero, and large enough to cover monthly interest, or the page says the payment is too low.
  • Balance-transfer fee (%): the one-time percentage added to the transferred balance.
  • Promotional APR (%): the rate during the promotional period, often 0.
  • Promotional period (months): how long the promotional rate lasts.
  • APR after promotion (%): the rate on any balance left when the promotion ends.

Method and assumptions

The calculator simulates both paths month by month at the same monthly payment: the current card at its APR until payoff, and the transferred balance (balance plus the transfer fee) at the promotional rate for the promotional months and the post-promotion rate afterward. It reports each payoff time, the current interest against the transfer cost (fee plus interest), the balance left when the promotion ends, and the net saving.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the transfer fee is 3% of 10,000, which is $300.00, so the transferred balance starts at 10,300. After 18 promotional months of 400 payments at 0%, $3,100.00 remains, which then accrues at 20%. Staying put costs $4,001.13 of interest over 36 months; transferring costs $547.67 (the $300.00 fee plus $247.67 of interest) over 27 months, so the headline reads $3,453.46 saved.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the net saving: $3,453.46 saved on the starting inputs. If the inputs ever favor staying put, the line shows the difference as a cost instead.
  • Payoff time (36 months vs 27 months here) is the second comparison that matters: the transfer wins on both speed and cost on these inputs.
  • Balance after promotional period ($3,100.00) is the risk line: whatever remains when the promotion ends starts accruing at the post-promotion APR, so a plan that clears the balance within the promotion is safer than this path, as the limitations note suggests.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the transfer fee; it is added to the balance you repay, not paid separately, which is why the transfer path starts at 10,300 here.
  • Assuming the promotional rate lasts the whole payoff; the model switches to the post-promotion APR on schedule, so enter the real promotional length.
  • Entering a payment below the monthly interest; the page stops and says the payment is too low because the balance would never fall.
  • Reading the saving as guaranteed; it assumes the fixed payment is actually made every month on both paths, and real terms depend on the card agreements, as the limitations note says.

Frequently asked questions

Why did the transfer save time as well as interest?

Because the same 400 payment goes further at 0%: during the promotion every dollar reduces principal, so the balance falls faster and is gone 27 months in on these inputs.

What if I would pay more than the fixed amount some months?

The model holds the payment constant on both paths for a fair comparison. Extra payments would shorten both paths; enter a higher fixed payment to approximate a more aggressive plan.

Is the fee ever worth paying?

That is exactly the comparison the page runs: the fee is included in the transfer cost, so if the interest saving does not cover it, the headline flips to show the transfer costing more.

Important limitations

Offers may exclude certain balances, require minimum payments, use deferred-interest terms or change the APR after late payments.

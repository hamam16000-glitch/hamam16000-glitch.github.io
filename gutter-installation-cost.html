What this calculator estimates

Estimate gutter installation cost from linear footage, material rate, complexity, downspouts, removal, gutter guards and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Gutter length (linear ft): the total gutter run in linear feet, measured along the roof edges getting gutters.
  • Installed gutter cost per linear ft ($): the per-foot installed rate for the gutter material and profile you plan, from a written quote.
  • Roofline complexity factor: a multiplier for corners, angles and height. The starting 1.15 is an example; simple straight runs are closer to 1.0, complex rooflines higher.
  • Number of downspouts: how many downspouts the plan needs.
  • Cost per downspout ($): the installed price per downspout.
  • Old gutter removal ($): the fixed amount to remove and dispose of existing gutters. Enter zero on new construction.
  • Gutter guard cost per linear ft ($): the per-foot rate for guards over the gutter run. Enter zero if you skip guards.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for fascia repairs found after removal.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the gutter length by the per-foot rate and the complexity factor, adds downspouts, removal and guards, then applies contingency. Guards are priced per foot of gutter run, like the gutters themselves.

With the starting inputs: 220 feet at $18 with a 1.15 factor gives $4,554.00 for gutters, 6 downspouts at $180 give $1,080.00, removal plus 220 feet of guards at $9 adds $2,630.00, and the $8,264 subtotal at 8 percent contingency adds $661.12, for a total of $8,925.12.

Reading the result

  • Gutters and roofline complexity is the line the length, rate and factor all move; a higher factor inflates it multiplicatively.
  • Removal and gutter guards combines two fields; zero the guard rate to see the gutters-only figure.
  • Contingency is the buffer for fascia surprises; old gutters often hide them.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the complexity factor at 1.15 for a straight single-story run, which inflates the gutter line by 15 percent.
  • Measuring the whole roofline perimeter instead of only the edges getting gutters.
  • Entering a guard quote as a lump sum in removal and also per foot in the guard field.
  • Leaving removal at the default on a new build with no old gutters.

Frequently asked questions

What complexity factor should I use?

The factor is a simplified stand-in for corners, height and access. Start at 1.0 for a simple straight single-story run and raise it toward the example 1.15 or beyond as corners and stories add work. It is an example input; your installer's quote reflects the real complexity.

Why price guards per foot?

Because guard material and installation scale with the gutter run, the same basis as the gutters themselves. If your guard quote is a lump sum, divide by the gutter length to get the per-foot rate.

Where does fascia repair go?

There is no separate field. Add the quoted amount to the removal field as a fixed cost; the limitations note flags fascia condition as a pricing factor.

Important limitations

Material, stories, fascia condition, roof access, drainage design, permits and regional labor pricing affect actual bids.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate a business income coverage need for a potential restoration period.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Monthly continuing expenses ($): the ongoing monthly costs the business would still owe during a shutdown.
  • Monthly net profit to protect ($): the monthly profit the coverage would need to replace.
  • Estimated restoration period in months: how long a shutdown might last. It must be greater than zero.
  • Extra expense allowance ($): a flat amount for extraordinary costs of keeping the business running during restoration.
  • Contingency buffer (%): a margin added on top of the base estimate, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds monthly expenses and monthly profit, multiplies by the restoration months, adds the extra expense allowance for the base estimate, then applies the contingency buffer for the recommended coverage amount.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 40,000 plus 15,000 is 55,000 per month, times 6 months is 330,000, plus the 25,000 allowance gives a base of $355,000.00. The 15% buffer lifts the recommended amount to $408,250.00, about $68,041.67 per month of the restoration period.

Reading the result

  • Recommended coverage amount is the headline, including the buffer; the base estimate without it is shown separately.
  • Monthly equivalent divides the recommended amount by the restoration months, useful for comparing against monthly cash flow.
  • The limitations note warns that policy definitions, waiting periods, coinsurance and exclusions materially change real coverage, so treat this as a planning figure and review it against actual policy terms with the insurer.

Common mistakes

  • Entering annual figures in the monthly fields; every currency field here is monthly except the flat allowance.
  • Setting the buffer to zero without a reason; the buffer exists because shutdown costs routinely overrun early estimates, though the right margin is a judgment for you and your insurer.
  • Reading the recommended amount as what a policy would pay; actual payment follows policy terms, waiting periods and loss calculations, as the limitations note says.
  • Forgetting the extra expense allowance is a flat total, not a monthly figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is this how an insurer calculates my coverage?

No. Policy definitions, waiting periods, restoration limits, coinsurance and actual loss calculations govern real claims, per the limitations note; this is a planning estimate to discuss with the insurer.

Why add a contingency buffer?

Because restoration often takes longer or costs more than planned; the buffer keeps the estimate from sitting exactly on the base figure. The 15% starting value is illustrative, not a standard.

Should profit be gross or net?

The field asks for monthly net profit to protect, matching how business-interruption coverage thinks about lost income; enter the figure your own accounts support.

Important limitations

Policy definitions, waiting periods, restoration limits, coinsurance, exclusions and actual loss calculations can materially change coverage.

What this calculator estimates

Project future long-term care costs and estimate the funding gap after savings and existing coverage.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current monthly care cost ($): what the care would cost per month at today's prices.
  • Years until care may begin: the years before the care is expected to start.
  • Expected years of care: how long the care is expected to last. It must be above 0.
  • Annual care-cost inflation (%): the yearly growth rate to apply to the monthly cost.
  • Savings reserved for care ($): savings you have set aside for this.
  • Existing monthly insurance benefit ($): the monthly benefit an existing policy pays.

Method and assumptions

The calculator projects the monthly cost forward at the inflation rate for the years until care begins, multiplies that future monthly cost by the months of care for the total, totals the insurance benefit over the same months, and reports the gap after the benefit and the reserved savings.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the future monthly cost is 7,500 grown at 3% for 20 years, which is $13,545.83, so 3 years of care totals $487,650.03. The benefit of 2,000 a month covers $72,000.00, the savings cover $100,000.00, and the remaining gap is $315,650.03.

Reading the result

  • The funding gap is the headline: $315,650.03 on the starting inputs after the entered benefit and savings.
  • The projected monthly cost ($13,545.83) is today's 7,500 grown at 3% for 20 years; the projection horizon drives it more than any other field.
  • The total care cost ($487,650.03) assumes the projected monthly cost stays flat through the care years; the model does not inflate within the care period itself.

Common mistakes

  • Entering today's monthly cost and reading the gap as today's money; the model projects the cost forward at the entered inflation rate.
  • Entering an annual benefit in the monthly benefit field; the field multiplies by the care months.
  • Counting the same money in savings and in the insurance benefit; the model subtracts both, so enter each once.
  • Assuming the benefit pays as entered; what a policy pays depends on its terms, so confirm with the insurer or plan documents.

Frequently asked questions

Does the cost keep inflating during the care years?

No. The model projects the monthly cost to the start of care and holds it flat across the care months; that is how this projection is built.

What if savings and benefits cover everything?

The gap floors at zero, so the headline never goes negative.

Where does the inflation rate come from?

You set it as a planning assumption. The model compounds whatever rate you enter over the years you enter.

Important limitations

Care type, location, benefit periods, elimination periods, taxes and investment returns can substantially change the outcome.

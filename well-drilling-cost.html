What this calculator estimates

Estimate a private-water-well project cost from drilling depth, casing, pump, testing, permits, access and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Estimated well depth (ft): the depth the driller estimates from local geology. The limitations note is blunt: required depth causes wide cost differences.
  • Drilling cost per foot ($): the per-foot drilling rate from a written quote.
  • Casing cost per foot ($): the per-foot casing rate. The model prices drilling and casing per foot on the same depth.
  • Pump, pressure tank and controls ($): the fixed total for the pump, pressure tank and controls.
  • Water testing and treatment setup ($): the fixed amount for water testing and any treatment setup.
  • Permits and professional services ($): the fixed permit and professional services total.
  • Mobilization and site access ($): the fixed amount for mobilizing the rig and site access.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal. Abandoned attempts are listed in the limitations note; deeper-than-planned drilling is the classic well surprise.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the depth by the combined drilling and casing rates, adds the pump system, testing, permits and mobilization as fixed costs, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the depth for an all-in cost per foot.

With the starting inputs: 300 feet at $40 drilling plus $15 casing gives $16,500.00, the pump system adds $2,500.00, testing, permits and access add $2,000.00, and the $21,000 subtotal at 10 percent contingency adds $2,100.00, for a total of $23,100.00, or $77.00 per foot.

Reading the result

  • Drilling and casing is the line the depth moves; every added foot adds both rates.
  • Testing, permits and access combines three fixed fields; mobilization alone can be significant on remote sites.
  • Estimated cost per foot bundles the pump and fixed costs, so shallow wells show a higher per-foot figure.

Common mistakes

  • Using a hopeful depth instead of the driller's geology-based estimate.
  • Quoting drilling per foot but forgetting casing is priced per foot too; the model adds both.
  • Leaving mobilization out on a remote or difficult-access site.
  • Comparing the all-in per-foot result to a bare drilling rate.

Frequently asked questions

What if the well comes in deeper than estimated?

Every extra foot adds the drilling plus casing rates, and contingency covers some overrun. The limitations note warns that required depth and abandoned attempts cause wide differences; discuss depth risk with the driller before relying on any total.

Where does water treatment equipment go?

In the testing and treatment field, as a fixed amount based on what the water test shows is needed.

Is the per-foot result a market rate?

No. It divides the total, including pump, testing, permits, mobilization and contingency, by your depth. The bare drilling and casing rates are the comparable figures.

Important limitations

Geology, required depth, diameter, casing, yield, access, abandoned attempts, treatment needs and local permits can cause wide cost differences.

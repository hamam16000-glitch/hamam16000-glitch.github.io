What this calculator estimates

Estimate how quickly an auto loan can be paid off and how much interest may be saved by adding an extra monthly payment.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current auto-loan balance ($): what you still owe on the loan today. It must be above 0.
  • Annual interest rate (%): the yearly rate on the loan. It can be 0.
  • Current monthly payment ($): the payment you make now. It must be above 0 and large enough to cover the monthly interest.
  • Extra monthly payment ($): the additional amount you plan to pay each month on top of the current payment. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator amortizes the balance month by month at the entered rate twice: once at the current payment plus the extra, and once at the current payment alone. It reports the accelerated payoff time in months, the total interest on that faster path, the base-case interest, and the interest saved.

With the illustrative starting inputs: paying 525 plus 100, which is $625 a month, retires the 22,000 balance in 40 months with $2,923.91 of interest, while $525 alone costs $3,589.27 of interest, so the extra payment saves $665.36.

Worked example

A 1,000 balance at 0% with 100 monthly is repaid in 10 months with no interest. If a 5 payment on a 1,000 balance at 12% cannot amortize the loan, adding 100 can still produce an 11-month payoff; original-plan savings are then unavailable.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the accelerated payoff: 40 months on the starting inputs, the point the balance reaches zero with the extra payment added.
  • The interest comparison is the money view: $2,923.91 on the faster path against $3,589.27 on the current payment alone, a saving of $665.36.
  • If the payment does not cover the first month's interest, or the loan would outlast 100 years, the calculator says so instead of showing a payoff.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the original loan amount instead of today's balance; the simulation starts from what you owe now.
  • Entering a payment that only covers interest; the model reports that the loan is not repaid within 100 years rather than showing a date.
  • Treating the saved figure as guaranteed; it holds the rate and both payments flat for the whole simulation, so it changes if the actual loan terms change.
  • Entering the extra as a one-time amount; the model adds it every month, so a single lump sum belongs in the balance field instead.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the saving come from?

Every extra dollar goes to principal after that month's interest, so later months charge interest on a smaller balance. On the starting inputs that adds up to $665.36.

Why does it ask for my current payment?

The base case amortizes the balance at the current payment alone, so the comparison shows exactly what the extra payment changes: $3,589.27 versus $2,923.91 of interest on the starting inputs.

Does the model include prepayment penalties?

No. It applies the full extra payment to principal every month. Check your own loan agreement for any prepayment terms before acting on the saving.

Limitations

Payments are assumed monthly after interest accrual, with no new charges or fees. Unfinished schedules are explicitly reported after the 1,200-month simulation limit; the limit is not a payoff date.

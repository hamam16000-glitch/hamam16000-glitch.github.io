What this calculator estimates

Estimate cash required to exercise stock options and pay tax on the spread.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Options to exercise: the number of options exercised, at least 1.
  • Strike price per share ($): the exercise price. It can be 0.
  • Current fair market value per share ($): the share value now. It can be 0.
  • Estimated tax rate on bargain element (%): your estimated rate on the spread. From 0 to 100.
  • Broker, legal and administrative fees ($): the fees on the exercise. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator prices the exercise as the options times the strike, the bargain element as the options times the amount the fair value exceeds the strike, the estimated tax as the entered rate on that element, and totals the three for the cash cost. It also shows the value of the shares minus the total cost.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the exercise cost is $25,000.00, the bargain element is $55,000.00, the estimated tax at 35% is $19,250.00, the fees add 500, the total cost is $44,750.00, and the shares are worth $35,250.00 more than the total cost.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the total cash cost: $44,750.00 on the starting inputs, the $25,000.00 exercise plus the $19,250.00 estimated tax plus the fees.
  • The bargain element ($55,000.00) is the paper gain at exercise; the estimated tax line applies your own entered rate to it, so the total moves with that estimate.
  • The final line ($35,250.00) compares the shares' current value against the total cost; it is not a profit figure, since the shares' value can move after exercise.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the total exercise cost in the strike field; the strike is per share, and the model multiplies by the option count.
  • Entering a marginal rate without thought; the tax line is the entered rate applied to the bargain element, and the actual treatment depends on the option type and the filer's situation, so confirm with a qualified tax professional.
  • Reading the value-minus-cost line as locked-in gain; the shares' value at exercise is a snapshot, and later price moves change any real gain.

Frequently asked questions

What is the bargain element?

The amount the fair value exceeds the strike across all options: (8 − 2.5) times 10,000, which is $55,000.00 on the starting inputs.

How is the tax line computed?

The entered rate applied to the bargain element: 35% of $55,000.00 is $19,250.00. The rate is your estimate; actual treatment follows the option type and the applicable tax rules.

What if the fair value is below the strike?

The bargain element floors at zero, so the tax line drops to zero and the total is the exercise cost plus fees.

Important limitations

ISO and NSO tax treatment, AMT, holding periods, liquidity, valuation and company restrictions require professional advice.

Cash exercise and paper spread

Strike price times option count is the exercise cash. Fair market value minus strike price creates the modeled bargain element when positive; applying the entered tax rate is a separate estimate. A paper spread is not sale proceeds, and exercising options does not guarantee liquidity. The form does not decide ISO, NSO or alternative minimum tax treatment.

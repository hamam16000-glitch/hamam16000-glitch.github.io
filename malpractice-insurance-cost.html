What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual medical-malpractice insurance premium from base pricing, specialty risk, coverage limits, claims history, deductible and policy fees.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Base annual premium before adjustments ($): the starting yearly premium for the specialty and setting.
  • Specialty risk factor: a multiplier for the specialty's risk, where 1 means no adjustment. It must be greater than zero.
  • Coverage-limit factor: a multiplier for the chosen coverage limits, where 1 means no adjustment.
  • Claims-history surcharge (%): a percentage surcharge for past claims.
  • Deductible discount (%): a percentage credit, from 0 to 100.
  • Policy and association fees ($): flat fees added at the end.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the specialty and coverage-limit factors to the base premium, then the claims surcharge and the deductible discount in order, and adds fees. It also shows the monthly equivalent, the premium after the two factors, the net effect of the surcharge and discount, and the fees.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the factors bring the 5,000 base to $8,400.00. The 15% surcharge and 5% discount net to an adjustment of $777.00, and adding 350 of fees gives a total of $9,527.00, about $793.92 per month.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual premium is the headline; Monthly equivalent divides it by 12.
  • Premium after factors shows the specialty and limit choices before claims and deductible effects.
  • The limitations note lists specialty, state, retroactive date and underwriting as real pricing inputs, so treat this as a budgeting model and get carrier or broker quotes for actual figures.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the surcharge and discount as offsetting evenly; 15% up then 5% down nets to 9.25% up, because they compound on different bases, which is why the adjustment is $777.00 here.
  • Entering a monthly premium as the base; the base field is annual.
  • Assuming the factors come from an insurer table; they are your editable multipliers, and actual premiums vary by specialty, state, procedures and claims history, per the limitations note.
  • Forgetting fees are added after the percentage math; the 350 is not discounted.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a real malpractice quote?

No. Actual premiums vary by specialty, state, limits, retroactive date, practice setting, procedures, claims history and underwriting, as the limitations note says; this page is a budgeting model with editable rates.

Why do the surcharge and discount compound?

The model applies them in sequence to the factored premium: first the claims surcharge percentage, then the deductible discount percentage on the result.

What is the coverage-limit factor for?

It scales the premium for higher or lower policy limits relative to the base; the right figure comes from comparing actual quotes at different limits.

Important limitations

Actual malpractice premiums vary by specialty, state, limits, retroactive date, practice setting, procedures, claims history, carrier underwriting and risk-management credits.

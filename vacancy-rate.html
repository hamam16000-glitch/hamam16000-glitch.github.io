What this calculator estimates

Estimate physical vacancy rate, occupied rate, potential rent and lost rent from vacant unit-days.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Total rentable units: the units in the property or portfolio. It must be at least 1.
  • Days in analysis period: the span you are measuring, typically 365 for a year.
  • Total vacant unit-days: the sum of days each unit sat empty over the period; one unit empty for 30 days is 30 unit-days.
  • Average monthly rent per unit ($): the typical monthly rent, used to value the vacancy.

Method and assumptions

The calculator divides vacant unit-days by total available unit-days (units times period days) for the vacancy rate, reports the occupied complement, values the period's full occupancy at the average rent, applies the rate to estimate the rent lost, and converts vacant unit-days into an average number of empty units.

With the illustrative starting inputs: available unit-days are 12 times 365, which is 4,380, so the vacancy rate is 420 over 4,380, which is 9.59%, and occupancy is 90.41%. Full-period rent potential is 12 times 1,650 times 12, which is $237,600.00, the lost rent is $22,783.56, and the average empty count is 420 over 365, which is 1.15 units.

Reading the result

  • Vacancy rate is the headline: 9.59% on the starting inputs, the share of available unit-days that sat empty.
  • The lost-rent line ($22,783.56) values the vacancy at the average rent, the money left uncollected against the $237,600.00 full-occupancy potential.
  • The average-empty line (1.15 units) translates unit-days into something visual: about one unit empty at any time on these inputs.

Common mistakes

  • Counting units instead of unit-days; a unit empty all year contributes 365 unit-days, not 1, so the count must be days-weighted.
  • Using a period shorter than the vacancy data; the vacant unit-days must cover exactly the entered period or the rate is distorted.
  • Entering vacant days above the available unit-days; the page rejects it because the vacancy cannot exceed 100%.
  • Reading lost rent as recoverable income; it is the value of the empty time at the entered rent, not a debt anyone owes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I collect vacant unit-days?

For each unit, count the days it sat empty during the period and add them up. A 12-unit property with two units empty for three months each totals about 180 unit-days.

Does a lower vacancy rate always mean more income?

Only at the same rent. The lost-rent line values vacancy at the average rent you enter, so the income effect depends on both figures together.

Is this the same as the occupancy rate?

They are complements: occupancy is 100% minus the vacancy rate, 90.41% on the starting inputs.

Important limitations

Economic vacancy can also include concessions, nonpayment, offline units and below-market rent. This calculator focuses on physical vacancy.

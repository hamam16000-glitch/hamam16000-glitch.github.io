What this calculator estimates

Estimate the multi-year cost of owning a vehicle after depreciation and operating expenses.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Purchase price ($): what you pay for the vehicle. It cannot be negative.
  • Years owned: how long you keep it. It must be at least 1.
  • Total depreciation over period (%): the share of value lost over the whole period, from 0 to 100.
  • Annual fuel or charging cost ($): the yearly energy cost. It cannot be negative.
  • Annual insurance cost ($): the yearly premium. It cannot be negative.
  • Annual maintenance and repairs ($): the yearly upkeep figure. It cannot be negative.
  • Total financing interest ($): all interest paid over the period. It cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the depreciation percentage to the price for the end value, totals the annual running costs over the years owned, and adds the lost value, running costs and financing interest for the total cost of ownership, also shown per year.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the end value is 38,000 less 45%, which is $20,900.00, so $17,100.00 of value is lost. Running costs are 1,800 plus 1,800 plus 1,200 per year, which is $4,800.00, or $24,000.00 over 5 years. The total is $17,100.00 plus $24,000.00 plus 4,500, which is $45,600.00, or $9,120.00 a year.

Reading the result

  • The total cost is the headline: $45,600.00 on the starting inputs, everything the vehicle costs beyond what you recover at the end.
  • Depreciation ($17,100.00) is the largest single component here, larger than the five years of running costs combined minus fuel figures aside.
  • The per-year figure ($9,120.00) is the one to compare against other vehicles or transport options on the same horizon.

Common mistakes

  • Entering annual depreciation in the period field; the field takes the total percentage lost over the whole ownership period.
  • Entering monthly running costs; the fuel, insurance and maintenance fields are annual.
  • Leaving financing interest out; on the starting inputs it adds $4,500.00, and omitting it understates the total.
  • Comparing totals across different ownership periods; use the per-year line, $9,120.00 on the starting inputs, when the horizons differ.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest cost here?

Depreciation on the starting inputs: $17,100.00 of lost value against $24,000.00 of running costs and $4,500.00 of interest over the 5 years.

Does the model include the loan principal?

Only through depreciation and interest. The price you pay is recovered through the end value; what you lose on it, $17,100.00 here, is the cost, plus the $4,500.00 of financing interest.

How do I compare two cars?

Enter each with the same years owned and compare the per-year figure. On the starting inputs that is $9,120.00.

Important limitations

Registration, taxes, unexpected repairs, resale conditions and opportunity cost are excluded unless reflected in the inputs.

Keep financing and depreciation separate

Purchase price minus resale value measures depreciation over the ownership window. Financing interest is an additional cost; paying back loan principal is not another full purchase price. Match annual mileage, fuel economy and ownership years to one scenario, and keep a resale assumption separate from any trade-in credit used when buying.

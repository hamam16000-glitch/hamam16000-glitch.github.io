What this calculator estimates

Car Repair vs Replace Calculator

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Major repair cost ($): the quoted cost of the repair. It cannot be negative.
  • Current vehicle trade-in value before repair ($): what the current vehicle is worth as a trade-in now. It cannot be negative.
  • Repaired vehicle resale value at the comparison end ($): what the repaired vehicle would sell for at the end of the comparison. It cannot be negative.
  • Expected additional years after repair: how long the repaired vehicle is expected to last. It must be at least 1.
  • Replacement cash down payment and fees, excluding trade credit ($): the cash and fees a replacement needs up front, not counting the trade-in. It cannot be negative.
  • Replacement monthly payment ($): the payment on the replacement vehicle. It cannot be negative.
  • Comparison months: the comparison window as a whole number of months, at least 1 and within the repaired vehicle's expected life.
  • Replacement equity at period end, resale value minus remaining debt ($): what the replacement would be worth net of its loan at the end of the comparison. It cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator costs the keep path as the repair plus the current vehicle's forgone trade-in value minus its repaired resale value, costs the replace path as the forgone trade-in plus the down payment and fees plus the monthly payments minus the replacement's end equity, and shows which path is lower with the annualized keep cost and the gap.

With the illustrative starting inputs: keeping costs 4,500 plus 9,000 minus 3,000, which is $10,500.00, or $3,500.00 a year over 3 years. Replacing costs 9,000 plus 5,000 plus 650 times 36 minus 8,000, which is $29,400.00. Repair is lower by $18,900.00.

Worked example

Repair 4,500, current value 9,000 and ending repaired value 3,000 give keeping cost 10,500 over three years. With cash down 5,000, 650 monthly for 36 months and ending replacement equity 8,000, replacement cost is 29,400.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the verdict: repair is lower by $18,900.00 on the starting inputs.
  • Both paths count the current vehicle's 9,000 trade-in value, because spending it as a trade-in is a cost of replacing and a benefit forgone by keeping.
  • The annualized keep cost ($3,500.00) is the figure to compare against the replacement's ongoing payment when the windows differ.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the trade-in value in the keep path; keeping the car means not collecting its 9,000 trade-in, which the model counts on both sides.
  • Setting the comparison months beyond the repaired vehicle's expected life; the model rejects that combination.
  • Leaving the end resale values at zero; both paths credit an end value, and zeroing them overstates the cost of whichever side is understated.
  • Reading the verdict as a recommendation; the model compares the entered costs only, and reliability, safety and financing terms are outside it.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the trade-in value appear in both paths?

Keeping the car forgoes the 9,000 you could trade it in for; replacing spends that same value. Counting it on both sides keeps the comparison honest on the starting inputs.

What does the $3,500.00 figure mean?

The keep path annualized: $10,500.00 over 3 years on the starting inputs. Compare it with the replacement's monthly payment when the time windows feel different.

What if the repair does not last as long as hoped?

Shorten the expected years and the annualized keep cost rises; the verdict can flip. The model reruns instantly, so test the pessimistic case too.

Limitations

The existing vehicle is assumed fully owned and its entered value applied as trade credit. Replacement cash down excludes that trade credit. Ending replacement equity must be resale value minus remaining debt. Compare within the repaired vehicle's expected life. Running costs and other repairs are excluded.

Compare the same ownership window

The repaired-car life and the replacement comparison period must be compatible. The replacement scenario includes the entered payments and terminal resale assumption; a cheaper monthly payment by itself does not establish a cheaper vehicle. Use the same treatment of fuel, insurance and maintenance on both sides, or compare those omitted costs separately.

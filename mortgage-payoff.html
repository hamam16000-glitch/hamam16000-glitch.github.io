What this calculator estimates

Estimate payoff time and total interest from a current balance and monthly payment.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current mortgage balance ($): the principal owed today, from the latest statement. It must be greater than zero.
  • Interest rate (%): the annual rate on the mortgage.
  • Monthly principal & interest payment ($): the scheduled principal-and-interest part of the payment, excluding any tax and insurance escrow.
  • Extra monthly payment ($): the additional amount paid toward principal each month; it cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator simulates the loan month by month at the entered rate: each month adds that month's interest to the balance and subtracts the scheduled payment plus the extra payment, until the balance reaches zero. It reports the payoff time in years and months, the month count, the total interest paid, and the total paid overall.

With the illustrative starting inputs: paying 1,800 plus 200, which is 2,000 a month, against 250,000 at 6% retires the loan in 197 months, which reads as 16 yrs 5 mo. Total interest is $143,312.84 and the total paid is $393,312.84.

Reading the result

  • The payoff time is the headline: 16 yrs 5 mo on the starting inputs, how long until the balance reaches zero at the entered payment plus extra.
  • The interest line ($143,312.84) is the carrying cost over that whole period; extra payments shorten the period and reduce this figure because every extra dollar cuts principal immediately.
  • The total line ($393,312.84) is principal plus interest, the full outlay at the entered payment pattern.

Common mistakes

  • Including escrow in the payment field; the simulation needs only principal and interest, or the payoff comes out too fast.
  • Entering a payment at or below the monthly interest; the page reports the payment is too low because the balance would never fall. On the starting inputs the balance's monthly interest is 1,250, so the combined payment must exceed that.
  • Assuming the extra payment is applied automatically; real servicers need the extra directed to principal, per the loan documents.
  • Reading the interest figure as the whole cost of the loan; closing costs and any rate adjustments are outside the simulation, as the limitations note says.

Frequently asked questions

What happens with no extra payment?

Set the extra to 0 and the simulation runs at the scheduled payment alone: on the starting inputs that is 238 months, so the entered 200 extra cuts about 3.4 years and about $34,578.76 in interest.

Does the model handle rate changes?

No. It holds the entered rate constant, so adjustable-rate loans only match the simulation while the rate stays put.

Why does the last month differ?

The final payment is only what remains plus that month's interest, so it is smaller than the regular 2,000 on the starting inputs.

Important limitations

The entered payment excludes escrow for taxes and insurance. The combined payment must exceed the first month's interest. The calculation has a 100-year simulation limit and excludes rate changes, lender rounding, fees, new borrowing and prepayment penalties.

The final month and supported horizon

Interest is added monthly before the entered payment and extra payment are applied. The final payment is limited to the balance plus that month’s interest, so it can be smaller than a regular payment. Completion in exactly 1,200 months is supported; a balance still outstanding after that month exceeds the 100-year simulation window.

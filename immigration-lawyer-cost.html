What this calculator estimates

Estimate immigration legal-service costs using editable attorney hours, package fees, filing, translation and specialist assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Estimated attorney hours: the hours the attorney expects the matter to take.
  • Attorney hourly rate ($): the hourly rate for the attorney's time.
  • Flat, package or minimum legal fee ($): the flat, package or minimum fee for the matter.
  • Court, filing and government fees ($): filing and government charges for the matter.
  • Expert, translation or professional fees ($): outside professional fees the matter needs, such as translation.
  • Other case-related costs ($): any other costs tied to the matter.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the hours by the rate for the hourly basis, takes the larger of that and the flat or minimum fee as the legal fee, adds the filing, expert and other costs as external fees, and reports the total plus how far it runs above the flat figure.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the hourly basis is 18 times 325, which is $5,850.00, above the 4,200 flat fee, so the legal line is $5,850.00. The external fees are 725 plus 900 plus 300, which is $1,925.00, the total is $7,775.00, and the amount above the flat fee is $3,575.00.

Reading the result

  • The matter total is the headline: $7,775.00 on the starting inputs.
  • The hourly basis ($5,850.00) exceeds the 4,200 flat fee, so the hourly figure governs the legal line; the model never adds the two bases together.
  • Government filing charges ($725 of the $1,925.00 external line) sit outside the attorney's fee, and they change on their own schedule, so confirm current amounts from the official source.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the flat field as a fee added on top of the hours; the model takes the larger of the two bases as the legal fee.
  • Assuming translation is inside the attorney's rate; the model prices it as a separate external line.
  • Leaving government filing charges out of the external fields; the model only adds what you enter.
  • Reading the total as the fee you will pay; engagement letters differ, so confirm the basis, hourly or flat, in your own agreement.

Frequently asked questions

What if the matter finishes in fewer hours?

The flat or minimum governs below the crossover. At 10 hours the hourly basis would be $3,250.00, so the legal line would read the 4,200 figure and the total would be $6,125.00.

Are government filing charges included?

Yes, in the external line as entered: $725.00 on the starting inputs. Confirm the current amounts from the official source, since they change on their own schedule.

Is this a quote?

No. It is planning arithmetic from the figures you enter; the actual fee comes from your attorney's own agreement.

Important limitations

Legal fees vary widely by state, attorney, complexity, urgency, hearings, negotiations, appeals and outcome. This is a budgeting estimate, not a fee quote or legal advice.

How a flat or minimum fee is handled

The legal fee is max(estimated hours × hourly rate, entered flat/minimum fee). External charges are then added. This is a minimum-fee model, not a selector between two independent quotes: entering both a complete hourly quote and a separate alternative fixed quote will intentionally use the larger amount.

For 10 hours at $200 with a $1,500 minimum and $300 of external charges, the total is $2,300. If the minimum rises to $2,500 with the same work, the total becomes $2,800. A retainer credited against billed work should not also be entered as a new external expense.

Keep attorney fees separate from government filing, translation, medical and courier charges. Confirm which application stages the engagement includes; the form does not determine eligibility, current filing fees or the outcome of an application.

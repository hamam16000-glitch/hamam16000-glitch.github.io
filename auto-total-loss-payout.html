What this calculator estimates

Estimate an auto total-loss payout, loan payoff and remaining equity or deficiency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Vehicle actual cash value ($): the insurer's valuation of the vehicle. It cannot be negative.
  • Sales tax and eligible fees (%): the percentage added to the valuation for tax and eligible fees, from 0 to 30.
  • Collision or comprehensive deductible ($): the deductible subtracted from the settlement. It cannot be negative.
  • Outstanding auto loan payoff ($): what you still owe on the vehicle. It cannot be negative.
  • GAP coverage toward deficiency ($): any GAP coverage available toward a shortfall. It cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds the tax percentage to the cash value and subtracts the deductible for the settlement, then compares it with the loan: a positive difference is cash to the owner, a shortfall becomes a deficiency after any GAP coverage, floored at zero.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the settlement is 28,000 plus 7% minus 1,000, which is $28,960.00. Clearing the 31,000 loan and the deductible takes $32,000.00, so the settlement falls short, but the 5,000 of GAP coverage absorbs the gap and the deficiency is $0.00.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the outcome: a deficiency of $0.00 on the starting inputs, because GAP coverage absorbs the shortfall.
  • The settlement ($28,960.00) is what the model pays out: cash value plus the tax allowance minus the deductible.
  • Without the entered GAP coverage the shortfall would be real: the settlement is $2,040.00 below the loan on these inputs.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the replacement cost instead of the actual cash value; the model starts from the cash value, and insurers settle on their own valuations.
  • Forgetting the deductible; it comes straight off the settlement, $1,000.00 on the starting inputs.
  • Assuming GAP covers everything; the model subtracts the entered coverage from the shortfall and floors at zero, so a shortfall beyond the coverage remains yours.
  • Reading the result as a claims decision; the model applies your figures only, and actual settlements follow the policy and the insurer's own valuation, so confirm against the policy documents and the insurer's offer.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no cash to the owner here?

The settlement of $28,960.00 goes to the loan first, and the 31,000 payoff exceeds it. The model shows the $2,040.00 shortfall absorbed by the entered GAP coverage, leaving a $0.00 deficiency.

What does the $32,000.00 line mean?

The loan plus the deductible: what clearing both takes on the starting inputs, set against the settlement.

Is the tax allowance guaranteed?

The model adds the percentage you enter, 7% here. Whether tax and fees are included in a real settlement depends on the policy and local rules, so confirm with the insurer.

Important limitations

Valuation disputes, taxes, salvage retention and GAP exclusions can change the result.

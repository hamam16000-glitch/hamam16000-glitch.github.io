What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual cybersecurity-services budget from users, endpoints, monitoring, assessments, incident response and training.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Employees and users: the people covered. It must be above 0.
  • Annual identity, email and training cost per user ($): the yearly per-user price for identity, email security and training.
  • Protected endpoints: the devices protected.
  • Annual endpoint protection cost ($): the yearly protection price for each endpoint.
  • Managed monitoring and SOC services ($): the yearly fee for monitoring and security-operations services.
  • Risk assessment and penetration testing ($): the yearly assessment and testing spend.
  • Incident-response retainer ($): the yearly retainer for incident response.
  • Additional awareness and tabletop exercises ($): the yearly spend on extra awareness work and exercises.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies employees by the per-user annual cost and endpoints by the per-endpoint annual cost, adds the four service lines, and reports the yearly total plus a monthly average.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the user line is 100 times 200, which is $20,000.00, the endpoint line is 150 times 120, which is $18,000.00, and the services are 15,000 plus 10,000 plus 12,000 plus 5,000, which is $42,000.00. The yearly total is $80,000.00, and the monthly average is $6,666.67.

Reading the result

  • The yearly total is the headline: $80,000.00 on the starting inputs, or $6,666.67 a month on average.
  • The user ($20,000.00) and endpoint ($18,000.00) lines scale with headcount and device count; the services block ($42,000.00) stays flat as entered.
  • The monthly average divides the yearly total by 12; the model treats all fields as annual figures.

Common mistakes

  • Entering monthly per-user prices in the annual fields; both per-unit fields are yearly figures.
  • Counting each employee's laptop as both a user and an endpoint in the same line; the model prices the two lines separately, so enter each count in its own field.
  • Reading the retainer as the cost of an incident itself; it is the yearly fee you enter, and response costs are a separate matter.
  • Leaving the assessment line at zero when testing is planned; the model only adds what you enter.

Frequently asked questions

What does the incident-response retainer buy?

Whatever the provider's agreement says. The model adds the yearly fee you enter; the covered scope comes from the provider's own terms.

Can I price part of a year?

The fields are annual, so prorate the figures before entering them.

Is compliance work included?

Only if you fold it into one of the service fields; the model has no separate compliance line.

Important limitations

Industry, data sensitivity, compliance, current controls, cloud footprint, attack surface, service hours, tools, insurance requirements and incident history affect cost.

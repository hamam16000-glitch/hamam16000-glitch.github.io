What this calculator estimates

Estimate the cost to convert a bathtub to a shower. Starting figures are illustrative example inputs, not market prices or recommendations; enter your own measurements and written quotes.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Number of conversions: how many bathtubs you are converting to showers, normally 1.
  • Low cost per conversion ($): the lowest written quote for a basic conversion, for example keeping the drain location with a prefab base and surround.
  • Typical cost per conversion ($): the quote that best matches the finish level you actually expect to choose.
  • High cost per conversion ($): the highest quote you would still consider, such as custom tile, a moved drain and frameless glass.
  • Plumbing and repair costs ($): the fixed amount for drain or valve moves and wall or floor repair after the tub comes out, but only when your conversion quotes exclude them. If a quote already includes them, leave this lower or at zero.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer applied to everything. Use it for unknowns like water damage found under the old tub; it is not a price source on its own.

Method and assumptions

For each of your three rates the calculator adds the fixed work once and then applies the contingency percentage to the whole amount: scenario = (conversions x rate + fixed) x (1 + contingency/100). The headline number is the scenario built from your typical rate. The breakdown line labelled total excluding base fixed charges subtracts the fixed work itself but keeps its contingency share.

With the starting figures (1 conversion, $3,500 / $7,000 / $14,000 rates, $750 fixed, 12% contingency): low = ($3,500 + $750) x 1.12 = $4,760.00, typical = ($7,000 + $750) x 1.12 = $8,680.00, high = ($14,000 + $750) x 1.12 = $16,520.00, and the variable part of the typical scenario is $7,000.00.

How to compare quantities, quote scope and contingency

Reading the result

  • A wide range usually reflects scope differences such as drain moves and custom tile, not just price shopping. Align what each quote includes before comparing totals.
  • The typical scenario is the arithmetic on your typical rate, not a predicted invoice.
  • The variable-cost line isolates the conversion work itself, so you can see what the fixed plumbing and repair allowance adds.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the drain move twice. If the conversion quote already includes plumbing relocation, entering it again in the fixed field inflates every scenario.
  • Ignoring repair work after removal. Removing a tub exposes wall and floor that often need repair; if quotes exclude that, it belongs in the fixed field, not at zero.
  • Comparing unlike quotes. A keep-the-drain prefab conversion and a custom tiled conversion are different scopes; enter them as separate scenarios.
  • Skipping the contingency. Hidden water damage under an old tub is common enough that a zero buffer is a deliberate choice, not a neutral one.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my range so wide?

Because the low and high rates you entered are far apart, usually because the quotes assume different scopes: keeping the drain with a prefab surround versus moving plumbing and custom tile. The calculator only reflects the spread you enter.

Where does the old tub disposal go?

Include it exactly once. If the conversion quote covers demolition and disposal, do not add them again. If they are billed separately, put them in the fixed field.

Should I budget the typical scenario?

Budget with the scenario whose quote you actually expect to accept. The typical line applies the arithmetic to the rate you marked typical; it is not a market average.

Limitations

Prefilled prices for tub to shower conversion cost are illustrative budgeting assumptions. Replace them with local written quotes and avoid including 'Plumbing and repair costs ($)' both in a unit quote and again as a fixed charge. A linear unit-cost model does not capture every scope, access, size, materials or regulatory requirement.

Match the quantity to the quoted scope

Count complete conversions. Confirm whether demolition, waterproofing, shower fittings, enclosure and plumbing changes are inside the per-conversion quote before adding repair costs.

Each contingency point adds $77.50 to the typical scenario at the starting figures, while the $10,500 gap between your low and high rates before contingency drives the range; keeping the drain in place versus moving it is usually what separates a low quote from a high one.

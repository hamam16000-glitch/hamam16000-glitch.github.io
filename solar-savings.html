What this calculator estimates

Estimate solar bill savings, net system cost and simple payback period.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Average monthly electric bill ($): the typical monthly bill. It can be 0.
  • Bill offset from solar (%): the share of the bill solar covers. From 0 to 100.
  • Annual utility-rate increase (%): the assumed yearly growth in utility rates. It can be 0.
  • Installed system cost ($): the full installed cost. It can be 0.
  • Tax credits and incentives ($): the credits and incentives applied against the cost. It can be 0.
  • Analysis period (years): the horizon, whole years from 1 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator nets the incentives out of the system cost, prices the first-year savings as the annual bill at the offset share, then grows that saving at the entered rate increase each year and totals it over the analysis period. The payback line reports the year the cumulative savings first cover the net cost.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the net cost is $16,800.00, the first-year saving is $2,244.00, and growing it at 3% a year for 25 years totals $81,814.59, with the cumulative savings covering the net cost in about 7 years.

Worked example

A 100 monthly bill offset by 50% produces 600 first-year savings. At zero annual growth, ten whole years produce 6,000. A zero net upfront cost is reported directly.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the total savings over the period: $81,814.59 on the starting inputs, twenty-five years of bill savings growing at the entered rate.
  • The payback line (About 7 years) is the cash-flow view: the year the running total of savings first passes the $16,800.00 net cost.
  • Both lines rest on the entered offset and rate increase staying constant, which are assumptions; actual production, rates and incentives differ, so treat the figures as the arithmetic of your scenario and confirm incentives with the applicable programs.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the system cost after incentives in the cost field and again in the incentives field; the model nets the incentives itself.
  • Entering the offset as the share of the bill you still pay; the field is the share solar covers, not the remainder.
  • Reading the total as a guaranteed return; it is the model's arithmetic on the entered bill, offset and rate growth, not a production guarantee.

Frequently asked questions

How is the payback computed?

The first year the cumulative savings reach the net cost: the $2,244.00 first-year saving growing at 3% passes the $16,800.00 net cost in about 7 years on the starting inputs.

What if incentives cover the whole cost?

The net cost becomes zero and the model reports that there is no net upfront cost instead of a payback year.

Does the model include maintenance or degradation?

No. It grows the bill saving at the entered rate only; equipment performance over time sits outside the model.

Limitations

The estimate does not separately model degradation, maintenance, financing, export tariffs, equipment replacement or incentive eligibility. Whole analysis years must be from 1 to 100. Entered incentives and costs are assumptions, not verified offers.

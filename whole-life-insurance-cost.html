What this calculator estimates

Estimate whole life insurance premiums and a simplified projected cash value using editable premium and accumulation assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Coverage amount ($): the death benefit. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual premium rate per $1,000 ($): the yearly premium per thousand dollars of coverage.
  • Annual policy fee ($): a flat yearly fee added to the rated premium.
  • Projection period (years): how many years to project. It must be a whole number of at least 1; the calculator rejects decimals.
  • Premium portion accumulating cash value (%): the share of each annual premium treated as accumulating toward cash value, from 0 to 100. This is a simplified modeling assumption, as the limitations note implies; real policies follow their own illustration.
  • Estimated annual cash-value growth (%): the yearly growth rate applied to the accumulating portion.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the rate per $1,000 of coverage plus the flat fee for the annual premium, then projects a simple cash-value estimate: each year's accumulating portion grows at the entered rate, compounded year over year like an annuity. It also shows the monthly premium, the total premiums paid, and the coverage amount.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 250,000 over 1,000 times 8.5 plus the 120 fee gives an annual premium of $2,245.00, about $187.08 per month, and $44,900.00 paid over 20 years. With 55% of each premium accumulating at 3.5% growth, the projected cash value after 20 years is $34,918.34.

Reading the result

  • Estimated monthly premium is the headline; the annual premium and the 20-year total paid sit beside it.
  • Projected cash value is the model's simplified accumulation of the entered premium portion at the entered growth rate; it is not a policy illustration.
  • The limitations note is direct: whole life policies include guarantees, expenses, dividends, surrender charges and underwriting features not captured here, and you should use an official policy illustration before making a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the projected cash value as a policy value; the limitations note says real whole life includes guarantees, expenses, dividends and surrender charges this model omits, and directs you to an official policy illustration before deciding.
  • Entering a decimal projection period like 20.5; the calculator requires a whole number of years.
  • Reading the accumulating portion as an insurer's figure; 55% is an illustrative starting input, and actual cash-value mechanics come from the policy illustration.
  • Comparing the cash value to premiums paid without noting the gap is the model's cost of insurance; here $44,900.00 paid against $34,918.34 projected, on simplified assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an actual whole-life illustration?

No. The limitations note says whole life policies include guarantees, expenses, dividends, surrender charges and underwriting features not captured here, and to use an official policy illustration before making a decision; this page is a simplified budgeting model.

How does the cash-value projection work?

Each year the accumulating share of that year's premium is added and everything already accumulated grows at the entered rate, compounded annually for the entered whole number of years.

Why does the calculator reject decimal years?

The projection compounds whole years, so the period must be a whole number of at least 1; decimals are rejected as invalid input.

Important limitations

Whole life policies include guarantees, expenses, dividends, surrender charges and underwriting features not captured here. Use an official policy illustration before making a decision.

Premium budgeting and cash-value illustrations are different

Use an insurer’s quoted annual rate per $1,000 of coverage; the starting rate is a demonstration, not an underwriting result. The accumulation output is the future value of a hypothetical share of annual premiums deposited at each year end. It is not an insurer’s guaranteed cash value, surrender value or dividend illustration. The model now requires whole projection years to match those annual deposits. If the assumed growth is zero, allocated contributions simply add up; a tiny positive rate must not make them disappear.

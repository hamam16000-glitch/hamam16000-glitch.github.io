What this calculator estimates

Estimate annual straight-line depreciation for residential rental property.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Property purchase price ($): the price paid. It can be 0.
  • Land value excluded from depreciation ($): the land's share of the price, which the model excludes. It can be 0 but cannot exceed the price plus improvements.
  • Capital improvements added to basis ($): improvements that add to the basis. It can be 0.
  • Recovery period (years): the recovery period in years. It must be above 0.
  • Rental-use percentage (%): the share of the property in rental use. From 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator removes the land value from the price, adds the improvements, scales by the rental-use percentage for the depreciable basis, then divides by the recovery period for the annual depreciation and shows the monthly figure alongside.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the basis is 450,000 minus 90,000 plus 30,000 at full rental use, which is $390,000.00, the annual depreciation over 27.5 years is $14,181.82, and the monthly figure is $1,181.82.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the annual depreciation: $14,181.82 on the starting inputs, the $390,000.00 basis spread evenly over the recovery period.
  • The land line ($90,000.00) is the part of the price the model never depreciates; the split between land and building is your entry and drives everything else.
  • A partial rental-use percentage scales the whole basis down in proportion, so a home rented at half use depreciates at half the figure.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the full price without removing land; the model excludes only the land field, and leaving it at zero overstates the basis.
  • Entering the recovery period in months; the field is years, and fractional years are accepted.
  • Reading the figure as an allowable deduction; the model applies straight-line arithmetic to your entries, while the correct basis, recovery period and method follow the applicable tax rules, so confirm with a qualified tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

How is the basis computed?

The price minus the land plus the improvements, scaled by rental use: (450,000 − 90,000 + 30,000) at 100%, which is $390,000.00 on the starting inputs.

Why is land excluded?

The model treats the land value as non-depreciable and subtracts it before spreading the rest over the recovery period.

Does the model handle mid-year starts or method changes?

No. It spreads the basis evenly over the full period; placed-in-service conventions and method rules follow the applicable tax authority's own guidance.

Important limitations

Closing costs, placed-in-service dates, personal use and component lives require tax-specific treatment.

A full-year straight-line planning amount

The recovery period is a user input, not a classification decision. Dividing basis by that period gives a simple full-year amount; dividing again by 12 does not apply the US mid-month convention or a placed-in-service date. Land is excluded, and personal use reduces the entered basis by the rental-use percentage. Separate components or later improvements can need separate schedules.

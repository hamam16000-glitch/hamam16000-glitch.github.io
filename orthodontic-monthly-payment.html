What this calculator estimates

Estimate monthly orthodontic payments after insurance and down payment.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Total orthodontic treatment fee ($): the provider's full quoted fee for the treatment plan, from the treatment contract.
  • Insurance lifetime orthodontic benefit ($): the lifetime orthodontic maximum your plan documents state; coverage, waiting periods and eligibility rules are set by the insurer and plan documents.
  • Down payment ($): the upfront payment the provider requires before the plan starts.
  • Annual financing rate (%): the rate on the payment plan, from 0 to 100; many in-house plans run at 0, which is the starting value here.
  • Payment-plan months: the plan length, from 1 to 120.
  • Records, retainers and financing fees ($): extra charges added to the amount being financed.

Method and assumptions

The calculator nets the treatment fee against the insurance benefit and adds the fees, subtracts the down payment for the financed amount, and divides or amortizes that over the plan months at the entered rate. The plan-total line adds the down payment back, and the financing-charge line shows what the plan costs beyond the net fee: $0.00 when the rate is 0.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the net fee is 7,000 minus 1,800 plus 450, which is $5,650.00, and after the 1,000 down payment the financed amount is $4,650.00. At 0% over 24 months the payment is $193.75, the plan total is $5,650.00, and the financing charge is $0.00.

Reading the result

  • The monthly payment is the headline: $193.75 on the starting inputs, the plan installment at the entered rate.
  • The financed line ($4,650.00) is the amount the plan actually covers after insurance and the down payment.
  • The financing-charge line tells you whether the plan costs anything extra: $0.00 at the entered 0% rate, and positive the moment a rate applies.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the insurance benefit is paid out; the page nets the benefit you enter, but whether the plan pays that amount depends on the insurer's rules, waiting periods and remaining lifetime maximum, per the plan documents.
  • Entering the monthly premium cost in the benefit field; the field is the lifetime orthodontic benefit only.
  • Forgetting records or retainer fees; on the starting inputs they add 450 to the financed base, which is 24 a month in the 0% case.
  • Comparing a 0% plan to a loan by payment alone; at 0% the financing charge is $0.00 here, and a rate above zero adds cost the limitations note asks you to weigh.

Frequently asked questions

What if insurance pays more or less than expected?

Change the benefit field: every dollar of benefit removes a dollar from the financed base, so at the starting figures each 240 of benefit is about 10 off the monthly payment on the 0% plan.

Is the down payment part of the plan total?

Yes. The plan-total line adds it back: 24 payments of $193.75 plus 1,000, which is $5,650.00 on the starting inputs.

Does the provider's actual plan match this?

Only if its rate, months and fees match what you enter. The treatment contract states the real schedule; this page sizes the arithmetic.

Important limitations

Lifetime orthodontic maximums, age limits, treatment changes and missed-payment fees vary.

Insurance reductions and borrowing charges

Price less entered insurance plus fees is the net treatment cost; the down payment reduces the amount borrowed. The fixed-rate installment covers only that financed balance. Confirm whether an insurance amount will be paid to the provider before the financing starts and whether the quoted treatment price already includes the same administrative fee.

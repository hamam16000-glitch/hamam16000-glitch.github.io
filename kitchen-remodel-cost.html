What this calculator estimates

Estimate kitchen remodeling costs for cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, labor and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Kitchen area (square feet): the kitchen's floor area, length times width. The model uses it for flooring and for the per-square-foot result.
  • Cabinets ($): the quoted total for cabinets, including installation if the quote bundles it. If installation is separate, keep it in the labor percentage instead, so it is counted exactly once.
  • Countertops ($): the quoted countertop total, including fabrication and installation when bundled.
  • Appliances ($): the total for appliances you are buying for the remodel. Leave at zero if you are keeping existing appliances.
  • Flooring per square foot ($): the installed flooring rate per square foot. The model multiplies it by the kitchen area.
  • Labor as % of materials: labor as a percentage of the materials and appliance total. Convert any fixed labor quote to the matching percentage before entering.
  • Permits and design fees ($): the fixed permit and design amount, only when the other figures exclude it.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for unknowns like subfloor repair or electrical surprises.

Method and assumptions

The model adds cabinets, countertops, appliances and area-based flooring into a materials and appliances total, applies the labor percentage to it, adds permits and design fees, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the area for a per-square-foot figure.

With the starting inputs: cabinets $18,000, countertops $6,000, appliances $8,000 and 180 square feet at $15 give $34,700.00 in materials and appliances. Labor at 35 percent adds $12,145.00, permits add $1,200, and contingency at 10 percent adds $4,804.50, for a total of $52,849.50, or $293.61 per square foot.

Reading the result

  • Materials and appliances is the line your cabinet, countertop and appliance choices move directly.
  • Estimated labor follows the materials total because the model prices labor as a percentage of it.
  • Estimated cost per square foot bundles everything, so it reads far above a cabinets-only or flooring-only rate.

Common mistakes

  • Counting cabinet installation both inside a bundled cabinet quote and in the labor percentage.
  • Entering a fixed labor quote as a percentage without converting it against the materials total.
  • Using the whole home's area instead of the kitchen's floor area.
  • Leaving appliances at the default when keeping existing ones, which inflates the total.

Frequently asked questions

Where do layout or plumbing changes go?

The model has no separate field for layout changes, plumbing relocation or structural work. Add their quoted amount to permits and design fees only if it is a fixed price, and remember the limitations note: those changes can materially increase cost beyond this estimate.

Why did my labor estimate jump when I upgraded cabinets?

Labor is modeled as a percentage of the materials and appliances total, so more expensive cabinets raise the labor line. If your contractor quoted fixed labor, convert it to a percentage of your materials total and enter that.

Can I compare the per-square-foot result to an advertised remodel rate?

Only if the advertised rate bundles the same items. This result includes cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, labor, permits and contingency.

Important limitations

Layout changes, electrical upgrades, plumbing relocation, structural changes and premium finishes can materially increase cost.

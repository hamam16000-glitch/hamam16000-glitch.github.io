What this calculator estimates

Estimate furnace replacement cost including equipment, labor, venting, gas or electrical upgrades, permits, removal and rebates.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Furnace equipment cost ($): the price of the furnace unit itself, before installation. Use the equipment line from a written quote.
  • Installation labor and materials ($): the labor and install-materials amount. If the quote bundles equipment and labor, split it so each is entered exactly once.
  • Venting and duct modifications ($): the quoted amount for venting or duct changes the new unit needs. Enter zero if none.
  • Gas or electrical upgrades ($): the quoted amount for gas line or electrical work the installation requires.
  • Permits and old unit removal ($): the fixed permit, haul-away and disposal amount, only when not already inside the labor quote.
  • Rebates and incentives ($): the total rebate amount you have confirmed you qualify for, from the utility or program documents. Enter only confirmed amounts.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the project subtotal for surprises found during installation.

Method and assumptions

The model adds equipment, labor, venting, upgrades and permit or removal costs into an installed base, applies contingency for a gross project cost, then subtracts the confirmed rebates. The result is floored at zero.

With the starting inputs: equipment $4,800, labor $2,800, venting $900, upgrades $600 and permits or removal $450 give an installed base of $9,550.00. Contingency at 8 percent adds $764.00 for a gross of $10,314.00, and subtracting $500 in rebates gives a net of $9,814.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated installed cost before rebate is the subtotal before contingency; the rebate subtracts from the gross, after contingency.
  • Estimated contingency is a percentage of the installed base, so higher-efficiency equipment raises it too.
  • The net result depends on the rebate figure; unconfirmed incentives make the net meaningless.

Common mistakes

  • Entering an advertised rebate without confirming eligibility in the program's own documents.
  • Splitting a bundled installed quote into equipment and labor incorrectly, counting labor twice.
  • Leaving venting at the default when the new unit reuses existing venting.
  • Treating the net as a final price; the limitations note lists fuel type, capacity and vent condition as real cost drivers.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the rebate subtracted after contingency?

That is the model's arithmetic: contingency builds a buffer into the gross cost, and the confirmed rebate comes off that gross. The rebate does not shrink the buffer the project may need.

What if rebates exceed the gross cost?

The result floors at zero; the model never shows a negative cost. Confirm the actual incentive stack with the program documents.

How do I compare two efficiency tiers?

Run the calculator once per tier with that tier's equipment price and its confirmed rebate, then compare nets. The fuel-cost difference is outside this model.

Important limitations

Fuel type, efficiency, capacity, chimney or vent condition, ductwork, code upgrades and local contractor pricing affect actual cost.

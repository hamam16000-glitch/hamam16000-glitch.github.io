What this calculator estimates

Calculate total weeks, full weeks and remaining days between two calendar dates.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Start year: the start year, a whole value.
  • Start month: the start month, from 1 to 12.
  • Start day: the start day; the date must exist on the calendar.
  • End year: the end year, a whole value.
  • End month: the end month, from 1 to 12.
  • End day: the end day; the date must exist on the calendar.

Method and assumptions

The calculator counts the calendar days between the two dates and divides by seven, reporting the raw quotient, the total days, the full weeks, the leftover days, and which date is later.

With the illustrative starting inputs: from January 1, 2026 to July 25, 2026 is 205 days, the raw quotient is 29.28571429 weeks, which is 29 weeks and 2 days, and the end date is later.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the raw week quotient: 29.28571429 on the starting inputs, shown to many decimals rather than rounded.
  • The full-weeks line (29 weeks) with the remaining line (2 days) is usually the more useful reading; the two always recombine into the 205 days.
  • The order line (End date is later) lets either date come first; the day count uses the absolute span either way.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the headline as rounded weeks; it is the raw quotient to many decimals, so read the full-weeks and remaining lines for the clean count.
  • Entering a date that does not exist; the form rejects invalid calendar dates.
  • Expecting business weeks; the model counts calendar days, weekends included.

Frequently asked questions

How is the week count computed?

The days divided by seven: 205 days from January 1, 2026 to July 25, 2026 gives the raw 29.28571429, which is 29 weeks and 2 days.

Does the date order matter?

No. The day count is absolute and the order line says which date is later.

Why so many decimals?

The model shows the raw quotient instead of rounding; the full-weeks and remaining lines carry the exact answer.

Important limitations

The result counts calendar days and does not account for partial times of day.

Elapsed weeks exclude the starting instant

Seven elapsed days equal one full week. A nine-day interval gives one full week and two remaining days; it does not become ten days by counting both printed dates. The start/end ordering output tells which date comes first. These are calendar weeks of seven days, not ISO week numbers or counts of working weeks.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate solar panel cleaning cost from panel count, per-panel rates and the travel and access charge. Starting figures are example inputs, not market prices or recommendations; enter your own measurements and written quotes.

How to use it

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results.

  • Number of solar panels: the count of panels to be cleaned. Count every panel in the array, including any on a second roof face if the same visit covers them.
  • Low cost per unit ($): the lowest per-panel quote for a straightforward cleaning on an easy-access roof.
  • High cost per unit ($): the highest per-panel quote you would accept, for example for steep roofs, heavy soiling or deionized-water cleaning.
  • Travel, roof access and inspection ($): the fixed visit charge: travel or call-out fee plus any access equipment or inspection billed as a flat amount rather than per panel.

How the calculation works

For each end of the range the calculator multiplies your panel count by one per-panel rate and then adds the fixed visit charge once: low end = panels x low rate + fixed, high end = panels x high rate + fixed. The headline number is the midpoint of those ends, and the per-unit line divides it by your panel count.

With the starting figures (24 panels, $10 and $25 per panel, $100 fixed): low end = 24 x $10 + $100 = $340.00, high end = 24 x $25 + $100 = $700.00, midpoint = $520.00, and midpoint per panel = $21.67.

Reading the result

  • The per-unit line tells you the effective price per panel including the visit charge; compare that, not the bare rate, against flat-per-panel offers.
  • A wide range only shows the spread between the rates you entered; check whether the quotes assume the same access and soiling conditions.
  • For very small arrays, compare any flat minimum visit price you are quoted against the low end of the range.

Common mistakes

  • Counting only one roof face. If the visit covers panels on several faces, count them all; splitting the array across separate visits would duplicate the travel charge.
  • Dropping the visit charge. If your quotes bill travel or access as a flat amount, keep it in the fixed field; setting it to zero removes that amount from both ends, which matters most on small arrays.
  • Comparing bare rates to effective per-panel cost. A provider quoting pure per-panel with no visit fee should be compared against the effective per-unit line, which includes your fixed charge.
  • Treating the midpoint as a quote. It is the average of your two scenarios, not a predicted invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the per-panel price fall when I add panels?

Because the fixed visit charge is divided by more panels in the per-unit line. The per-panel rate itself is unchanged; the trip cost is just spread thinner.

Is inspection included?

Only if you add it, and only once. If a provider bills inspection or roof access as a flat fee, include it in the fixed field so both ends carry it; when your per-panel quotes already include that charge, leave it out of the fixed field.

How often should I budget this?

That depends on local dust, pollen and bird activity, which this page does not assume. Use the result per visit and multiply by the number of visits you actually plan per year.

Important limitations

Actual project pricing varies by location, materials, labor, access, permits, contractor minimums and scope changes. Use local written quotes before committing.

Interpreting the low/high midpoint

Use the quantity and unit named in the form consistently. Access, roof geometry and a contractor’s minimum callout charge can change a quote independently of the surface area; the arithmetic does not predict an energy-yield improvement.

The per-panel rate is the main lever once the array is large: at 24 panels, each extra $5 on a rate moves that end by $120, while the $100 visit charge shifts both ends by the same $100. On small arrays the visit charge dominates the per-unit line, so expect the per-panel figure to fall as panel count grows.

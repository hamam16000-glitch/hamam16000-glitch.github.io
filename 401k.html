Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual salary ($): the gross yearly salary the contribution percentages apply to. It can be 0.
  • Employee contribution (%): the share of salary contributed. From 0 to 100.
  • Employer match (%): the share of the matched contribution the employer adds. It can be 0.
  • Match up to salary (%): the share of salary the match applies to. From 0 to 100.
  • Current 401(k) balance ($): the balance today. It can be 0.
  • Years to invest: the projection period. It can be 0.
  • Expected annual return (%): the assumed annual return. It must stay above -100%.

Method and assumptions

The calculator takes the employee contribution as the entered share of salary, the employer match as the entered match rate applied to the smaller of the contribution and the matched share of salary, then compounds the current balance plus the monthly contribution at the expected annual return over the years entered. It also shows each annual contribution, the total contributed, and the growth over that total.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the employee contribution is $6,000.00 a year, the employer match is $2,250.00 a year (50% of the first 6% of the 75,000 salary), the combined monthly saving compounds at 7% for 30 years to $1,001,060.01, against $267,500.00 contributed in total, leaving $733,560.01 of growth.

Worked example

For salary 12,000, 10% employee contribution, no match and zero return, a 0.1-year horizon rounds to one month and contributes 100.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the projected balance: $1,001,060.01 on the starting inputs, the current 20,000 plus three decades of combined contributions compounded at the entered return.
  • The match line ($2,250.00 a year) is the employer's addition; on these figures it is over a third of the employee's own $6,000.00.
  • The growth line ($733,560.01) against $267,500.00 contributed shows how much of the projection comes from compounding rather than deposits; the growth line and the projected balance move with the entered return, which is an assumption, not a forecast, while the contributed total is fixed by the entered salary, contribution and match.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the employer match as a share of salary; the match field is a share of the matched contribution, and the next field caps how much salary is matched.
  • Entering the monthly contribution as a percentage; the employee field is an annual salary percentage, and the model converts to monthly itself.
  • Reading the projection as an expected balance; the entered return is one constant assumption, while real returns vary year to year, so treat the figure as the arithmetic of that assumption.

Frequently asked questions

How is the employer match computed?

The match rate applied to the smaller of the employee contribution and the matched share of salary: 50% of 6% of the 75,000 salary, which is $2,250.00 a year on the starting inputs.

Does the model include taxes or fees?

No. It compounds the balance and contributions at the entered return only; plan fees and any tax treatment sit outside the model.

Why does the growth line exceed the contributions?

Compounding: $267,500.00 contributed over 30 years at 7% grows by $733,560.01 on the starting inputs. Lower the entered return and the gap shrinks.

Limitations

This projection does not enforce contribution caps, catch-up rules, eligibility or vesting. Employee and match-limit percentages are 0% to 100%; the match percentage is an employer assumption. Return must exceed -100%.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate cash flow and cash-on-cash return for a vacation rental.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual booking revenue ($): the yearly booking revenue. It can be 0.
  • Other annual income ($): other yearly income from the property. It can be 0.
  • Annual operating expenses ($): yearly costs other than the mortgage. It can be 0.
  • Annual mortgage debt service ($): the yearly loan payments. It can be 0.
  • Cash invested ($): the cash put into the property. It must be above 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator totals the booking revenue and other income, subtracts the operating expenses and the debt service for the annual cash flow, then divides by the cash invested for the cash-on-cash return, and shows both totals.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the income totals $90,000.00, the outlay totals $62,000.00, the annual cash flow is $28,000.00, and the return on the 180,000 invested is 15.56%.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the annual cash flow: $28,000.00 on the starting inputs, after both the operating costs and the mortgage.
  • The return line (15.56%) prices that flow against the 180,000 cash invested, the model's cash-on-cash view.
  • The figures are the model's arithmetic on the entered amounts; booking revenue varies with season and occupancy, so treat the result as the scenario's arithmetic, not a forecast.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the property's full price as the cash invested; the return divides by the actual cash put in, not the price.
  • Putting the mortgage in the operating expenses; the model subtracts the two fields separately, and double counting distorts both.
  • Reading the return as a total return; principal paydown, appreciation, taxes and selling costs sit outside the model.

Frequently asked questions

What does the return line measure?

The annual cash flow divided by the cash invested: $28,000.00 on 180,000, which is 15.56% on the starting inputs.

Is the mortgage included?

Yes. The outlay of $62,000.00 on the starting inputs includes the 24,000 of debt service plus the 38,000 of operating expenses.

Can the flow go negative?

Yes. An outlay above the income gives a negative headline and a negative return, which the model shows as-is.

Important limitations

Taxes, appreciation, depreciation and personal use are excluded.

Cash-on-cash return uses cash invested

Annual operating income is booking revenue plus other income less operating expenses. Debt service is then subtracted to obtain the modeled annual cash flow. Dividing that cash flow by cash invested gives the percentage shown. Appreciation, principal paid down, sale proceeds, income taxes and a future refinance are outside this annual cash-return measure.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate the advance you receive up front, the factor's fees, the reserve released later and the net proceeds from selling an invoice.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Invoice amount ($): the face value of the invoice being factored. It must be above 0.
  • Advance rate (%): the share of the invoice paid up front, from 0 to 100.
  • Initial factoring fee (%): the fee percentage charged on the invoice. It can be 0.
  • Weeks until customer payment: how long the customer takes to pay, at least 1.
  • Additional fee per week (%): the extra fee charged for each week after the first. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the advance rate for the upfront payment, builds the fee as the initial percentage plus the weekly percentage for each week after the first, subtracts the advance from the invoice for the reserve, releases the reserve minus the fee, and nets the fee out of the invoice for the total cost.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the advance is 85% of 100,000, which is $85,000.00. The fee is 2% plus 0.5% for each of the 5 weeks after the first, which is 4.5% or $4,500.00. The reserve of $15,000.00 releases as $10,500.00, and the invoice nets $95,500.00.

Worked example

For a 1,000 invoice with a 90% advance and a 20% fee, receive 900 initially. The 200 fee exceeds the 100 reserve, leaving 100 additionally owed and final net proceeds of 800.

Reading the result

  • The advance is the headline: $85,000.00 on the starting inputs, the cash available up front.
  • The total cost line (4.5%) grows with every week the customer takes to pay, which is the real price of speed.
  • The net figure ($95,500.00) is what the invoice ultimately yields after the fee; compare it against waiting for the customer's own payment terms.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the weekly fee as applying from day one; the model charges the initial fee for the first week and adds the weekly rate only for later weeks.
  • Entering the advance as a dollar amount; the field takes a percentage of the invoice.
  • Ignoring how sensitive the cost is to the week count; on the starting inputs each extra week adds 0.5% of the invoice, $500.00, to the fee.
  • Reading the figures as a factor's actual offer; the model applies the percentages you enter, and a factor's real pricing and reserve rules come from its own agreement, so confirm against that document.

Frequently asked questions

How does the fee grow with time?

The model charges the initial fee plus the weekly rate for each week after the first: 2% plus five times 0.5%, which is 4.5% on the starting inputs. Each additional week adds $500.00 here.

What is the reserve release?

The invoice minus the advance, less the fee: $15,000.00 minus $4,500.00 gives $10,500.00 on the starting inputs, paid when the customer settles.

What does the invoice actually net?

The face value minus the fee: $95,500.00 on the starting inputs. The 4.5% cost line shows what the early cash cost you.

Limitations

If fees exceed the reserve, the excess must still be paid; it does not disappear. Collection delays, recourse, additional charges and uneven cash-flow timing are excluded.

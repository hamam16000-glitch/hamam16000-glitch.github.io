What this calculator estimates

Estimate a rental property's monthly cash flow, cap rate and cash-on-cash return from the purchase price, financing, rent and operating expenses.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Purchase price ($): the price paid for the property. It must be above 0.
  • Down payment ($): the cash down. It can be 0 but must stay below the price.
  • Mortgage rate (%): the annual loan rate. It can be 0.
  • Loan term: the loan length in years.
  • Monthly rent ($): the expected monthly rent. It can be 0.
  • Monthly operating expenses ($): monthly costs other than the mortgage. It can be 0.
  • Vacancy rate (%): the share of rent lost to vacancy. From 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator prices the mortgage payment on the price minus the down payment, reduces the rent by the vacancy rate, subtracts the expenses and the payment for the monthly cash flow, then shows the annual net operating income, the cap rate on the price, and the cash-on-cash return on the down payment.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the payment on the 262,500 loan at 6.75% over 30 years is $1,702.57, the rent after 5% vacancy is 2,850, the monthly cash flow is $247.43, the annual net operating income is $23,400.00, the cap rate is 6.69%, and the cash-on-cash return is 3.39%.

Worked example

A zero down payment leaves the cash-on-cash percentage undefined even if cash flow is calculable.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the monthly cash flow: $247.43 on the starting inputs, the rent after vacancy minus the expenses and the $1,702.57 payment.
  • The cap rate (6.69%) divides the net operating income, which is before any loan payment, by the full price, while the cash-on-cash line (3.39%) divides the after-loan cash flow by the 87,500 down payment; different numerators and different bases, so the two lines answer different questions about the same property.
  • The cash flow is the model's arithmetic on the entered rent, vacancy and expenses; it is not a guaranteed return, and reserves for repairs or capital work sit outside the entered expense line unless you add them.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the loan amount as the down payment; the model computes the loan as the price minus the down payment.
  • Entering the rent after your own vacancy adjustment; the model applies the vacancy field itself, so enter the full rent.
  • Reading the cash-on-cash line as a total return; the model counts cash flow only, and principal paydown, appreciation and taxes sit outside it.

Frequently asked questions

Why do the two return lines differ?

The cap rate divides the $23,400.00 net operating income by the full 350,000 price, while the cash-on-cash line divides the annual cash flow by the 87,500 down payment, giving 6.69% and 3.39% on the starting inputs.

What if the down payment is zero?

The cash-on-cash line reports that it is undefined, since there is no entered cash to divide by.

Can the cash flow go negative?

Yes. Expenses and the payment above the adjusted rent give a negative headline, which the model shows as-is.

Limitations

The down payment is the only cash investment entered here. Include acquisition, reserve and rehabilitation cash separately in a full cash-on-cash analysis. Taxes and insurance must be included in operating costs.

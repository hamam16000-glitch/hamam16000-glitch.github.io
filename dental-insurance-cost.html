What this calculator estimates

Compare expected annual dental spending with estimated premiums, deductible, coinsurance and plan maximum benefits.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Monthly dental premium ($): the plan's monthly premium.
  • Expected annual dental charges ($): the dental charges you expect over the year, before any plan payments.
  • Annual deductible ($): the amount you pay before plan coverage starts.
  • Average plan coverage after deductible (%): the share of post-deductible charges the plan pays, from 0 to 100.
  • Annual plan benefit maximum ($): the most the plan pays in a year.

Method and assumptions

The calculator converts the premium to an annual figure, subtracts the deductible from expected charges, applies the coverage percentage capped at the benefit maximum for the plan's benefit, then totals your year: premiums plus charges minus the benefit. It also shows the cost with no insurance and the difference.

With the illustrative starting inputs: premiums total $540.00 a year, charges past the $75 deductible are 1,725, and 60% coverage of that is $1,035.00, under the $1,500 maximum. Your estimated annual cost with insurance is $1,305.00, against $1,800.00 without, a difference of $495.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual cost with insurance is the headline: premiums plus your share of charges.
  • Plan benefit shows what the plan pays on these inputs, $1,035.00, capped by the benefit maximum if charges run high.
  • Estimated savings compares with-insurance against without; a negative figure would mean the plan costs more than it saves on the entered charges.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the annual premium in the monthly field; the calculator multiplies the monthly figure by 12 itself.
  • Ignoring the benefit maximum; on higher charges the plan's payment stops at the cap while your share keeps growing.
  • Treating the coverage percentage as applying to all charges; it applies only after the deductible, and only up to the maximum.
  • Assuming the plan pays every charge; the limitations note lists waiting periods, procedure categories and exclusions, and directs you to the plan contract and a dentist estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does a positive savings figure mean I should buy the plan?

Not by itself; it means the plan saves money on the entered charges. Waiting periods, procedure categories, negotiated rates and exclusions change real outcomes, as the limitations note says, so check the plan contract before deciding.

What happens when charges exceed the benefit maximum?

The plan's benefit stops at the maximum; every dollar above it is yours. Try raising expected charges here and watch the benefit line hold at the cap while your cost climbs.

Why does the calculator need expected charges?

Because the value of a plan depends on how much dental work you actually use; the estimate compares the same charges with and without insurance.

Important limitations

Waiting periods, procedure categories, negotiated rates, exclusions and frequency limits can materially change costs. Review the plan contract and dentist estimate.

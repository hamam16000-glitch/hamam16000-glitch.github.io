What this calculator estimates

Estimate a divorce legal budget from attorney time, hourly rates, filing, mediation, expert and other case costs.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Estimated attorney hours: the hours the attorney expects the case to take.
  • Attorney hourly rate ($): the hourly rate for the attorney's time.
  • Flat, package or minimum legal fee ($): the flat, package or minimum fee for the case.
  • Court, filing and government fees ($): filing and government charges for the case.
  • Expert, translation or professional fees ($): outside professional fees the case needs, such as valuation or forensic work.
  • Other case-related costs ($): any other costs tied to the case.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the hours by the rate for the hourly basis, takes the larger of that and the flat or minimum fee as the legal fee, adds the court, expert and other costs as external fees, and reports the total plus how far it runs above the flat figure.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the hourly basis is 35 times 350, which is $12,250.00, above the 5,000 flat fee, so the legal line is $12,250.00. The external fees are 450 plus 1,800 plus 650, which is $2,900.00, the total is $15,150.00, and the amount above the flat fee is $10,150.00.

Reading the result

  • The case total is the headline: $15,150.00 on the starting inputs.
  • The hourly basis ($12,250.00) exceeds the 5,000 flat fee, so the hourly figure governs the legal line; the model never adds the two bases together.
  • Expert and professional fees ($1,800 of the $2,900.00 external line) are the largest external piece here, and they sit outside the attorney's own fee.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the flat field as a fee added on top of the hours; the model takes the larger of the two bases as the legal fee.
  • Assuming expert fees are inside the attorney's rate; the model prices them as a separate external line.
  • Leaving filing fees out of the external fields; the model only adds what you enter.
  • Reading the total as the fee you will pay; engagement letters differ, so confirm the basis, hourly or flat, in your own agreement.

Reading the fee breakdown for a divorce matter

The starting scenario has 35 hours at $350.00, or $12,250.00, against a $5,000.00 flat fee, so hourly work is 80.9% of the $15,150.00 total. The flat floor would only matter below 14.29 hours. Specialist fees of $1,800.00 are the largest of the non-legal lines.

In the what-if table, 25% more or fewer hours moves the total by $3,062.50 and a 10% rate change by $1,225.00. With no hourly work the page returns $7,900.00, the flat fee plus the entered court, specialist and other costs. Hours are the number to pressure-test on this page, since they set most of the result.

Frequently asked questions

What if the hours come in below the flat fee?

The flat or minimum governs. At 10 hours the hourly basis would be $3,500.00, so the legal line would read the 5,000 figure and the total would be $7,900.00.

What does the external line cover?

Court, filing and government fees plus expert and other case costs: $2,900.00 on the starting inputs.

Is this a quote?

No. It is planning arithmetic from the figures you enter; the actual fee comes from your attorney's own agreement.

Important limitations

Legal fees vary widely by state, attorney, complexity, urgency, hearings, negotiations, appeals and outcome. This is a budgeting estimate, not a fee quote or legal advice.

How a flat or minimum fee is handled

The legal fee is max(estimated hours × hourly rate, entered flat/minimum fee). External charges are then added. This is a minimum-fee model, not a selector between two independent quotes: entering both a complete hourly quote and a separate alternative fixed quote will intentionally use the larger amount.

For 10 hours at $200 with a $1,500 minimum and $300 of external charges, the total is $2,300. If the minimum rises to $2,500 with the same work, the total becomes $2,800. A retainer credited against billed work should not also be entered as a new external expense.

Separate the attorney’s work from mediation, valuation, expert and court charges. A contested hearing or an appeal may be outside a quoted package; the calculator cannot infer those additional hours from the value of the assets in dispute.

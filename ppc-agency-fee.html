What this calculator estimates

Estimate paid-search agency management fees and total campaign budget.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Monthly ad spend ($): the media budget the agency manages each month.
  • Agency fee as percent of spend (%): the percentage-of-spend component of the agency fee, from 0 to 100.
  • Monthly flat management fee ($): any fixed monthly component of the agency fee.
  • One-time setup and tracking cost ($): the onboarding charge, counted once in the contract total.
  • Contract months: the engagement length. It must be at least 1.
  • Monthly landing-page or creative cost ($): creative or landing-page spend included in the comparison.

Method and assumptions

The calculator totals the media spend over the contract, then the fees: the percentage-of-spend component plus the flat fee plus the creative cost, each month, plus the one-time setup. The headline is the all-in contract cost, media plus fees, and the effective management rate is fees as a percentage of media spend.

With the illustrative starting inputs: media over 12 months is 30,000 times 12, which is $360,000.00. Monthly fees are 15% of 30,000, which is 4,500, plus 1,500 flat plus 2,000 creative, which is 8,000, so fees over the contract plus the 3,500 setup are $99,500.00. The all-in total is $459,500.00 and the effective management rate is 27.64% of media.

Reading the result

  • The all-in contract total is the headline: $459,500.00 on the starting inputs, media plus every fee, so it compares cleanly with in-house or other agency quotes on the same basis.
  • Total agency and creative fees ($99,500.00) separate the service cost from the media; that is the figure to negotiate on.
  • The effective management rate (27.64%) restates fees as a share of media; flat and creative components push it above the headline 15% percentage, which is why comparing percentage quotes alone misleads.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing agencies on the percentage-of-spend figure alone; flat fees, creative charges and setup belong in the comparison, which is what the effective rate captures.
  • Forgetting the one-time setup in a short contract; over few months it weighs heavily on the effective rate.
  • Entering the flat fee as part of the percentage or vice versa; the model treats them as separate components.
  • Reading the total as advertising cost per result; no performance or conversion outcome is modeled on this page.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my effective rate higher than the quoted percentage?

Because the quoted percentage usually covers only the spend-based component. Flat management fees, creative charges and setup all add to the real cost of management, and the effective rate divides the full fee total by media spend.

Does a longer contract lower the effective rate?

On these inputs, yes, because the one-time setup spreads over more months while the monthly components stay constant. Try 6 versus 24 months to see the effect.

Should creative be in the comparison?

If you would pay for it anyway, include it so the all-in total is honest; if the agency bundles it, enter it once, not in both the flat fee and the creative field.

Important limitations

Minimum fees, performance incentives, platform charges and production scope vary by agency.

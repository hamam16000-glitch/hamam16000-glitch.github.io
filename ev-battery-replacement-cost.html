What this calculator estimates

Estimate an electric-vehicle battery replacement cost from capacity, battery price, labor, supporting parts, credits, warranty contribution and tax.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Battery capacity (kWh): the pack's capacity in kilowatt-hours. It must be above 0.
  • Battery pack cost per kWh ($): the pack price per kilowatt-hour of capacity.
  • Labor hours: the hours the replacement is expected to take.
  • Labor rate per hour ($): the shop's hourly labor rate.
  • Cooling, hardware and related parts ($): cooling components, hardware and other parts around the pack.
  • Diagnostics and programming ($): diagnosis and programming after installation.
  • Core, recycling or trade-in credit ($): what you get back for the old pack.
  • Warranty or goodwill contribution ($): what the manufacturer or warranty provider contributes.
  • Sales tax on battery and parts (%): the tax rate that applies to the battery and parts.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies capacity by the per-kWh price and adds related parts for the battery line, multiplies labor hours by the rate and adds diagnostics for the labor line, taxes the battery line, adds the core credit and warranty contribution as credits, and subtracts the credits from the sum for the total.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the battery line is 75 times 150 plus 800, which is $12,050.00, labor is 10 times 180 plus 250, which is $2,050.00, and the tax is 8% of $12,050.00, which is $964.00. The credits are 1,000 plus 2,000, which is $3,000.00, so the total is $12,050.00 plus $2,050.00 plus $964.00 minus $3,000.00, which is $12,064.00.

Reading the result

  • The replacement total is the headline: $12,064.00 on the starting inputs after the entered credits.
  • The battery line ($12,050.00) dominates this job at the entered per-kWh price; labor and tax are built around it.
  • The credits line ($3,000.00) subtracts whatever you enter, so enter only amounts confirmed in writing by the manufacturer or warranty provider.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a pack quote that already includes labor in the per-kWh field; the labor line then counts it again.
  • Applying the tax rate to the whole bill; the model taxes the battery and parts line only.
  • Entering hoped-for warranty or goodwill money; the model subtracts what you enter, so use confirmed figures only.
  • Leaving the core credit at zero when the old pack goes back; the model only subtracts what you enter.

Frequently asked questions

Can the credits exceed the cost?

The total floors at zero; the model does not show a negative bill.

Does it handle a refurbished pack?

Yes. Enter its per-kWh price and the arithmetic is the same.

Is a loaner vehicle included?

No. The model covers the entered battery, labor, tax and credits only, as the limitations note says.

Important limitations

Vehicle model, usable capacity, pack availability, module versus full-pack repair, warranty, labor, shipping, recycling and tax treatment affect actual cost.

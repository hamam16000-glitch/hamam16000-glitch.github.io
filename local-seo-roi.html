What this calculator estimates

Estimate local SEO ROI from leads, close rate and customer profit.

How to use it

Enter the one-time website and local SEO setup cost. Monthly gross profit from SEO-sourced customers is the profit you attribute to customers from search, not their revenue; the model compares profit with spend. Monthly SEO agency and content cost is the recurring spend. Evaluation period in months sets how long costs and benefits accumulate. One-time value from improved listings or reviews adds a single non-recurring benefit. Select Calculate to update the results. The starting values are example inputs, not recommendations.

How the calculation works

Gross benefits multiply the monthly profit by the period and add the one-time value: $12,000 x 18 + $5,000 = $221,000. Total spend adds setup to the recurring cost over the period: $7,500 + $3,500 x 18 = $70,500. Net benefit is $221,000 - $70,500 = $150,500, and ROI is $150,500 / $70,500 = 213.48%. Payback divides the setup cost by the net monthly gain: $7,500 / ($12,000 - $3,500) = 0.9 months.

How to interpret the result

ROI is net benefit divided by total modeled spend across the period, so it climbs as the period lengthens while the monthly inputs stay flat; payback, which ignores the period, is the steadier number for comparing scenarios. The model credits every dollar of the entered profit to SEO, so the attribution decision is yours and sits outside the arithmetic. If monthly profit does not exceed the monthly agency cost, the payback line reports no payback.

Important limitations

Results depend on adoption, attribution, labor assumptions, taxes and actual vendor pricing.

ROI is undefined when the total modeled cost is zero. The associated cash-flow amounts can still be calculated; an undefined return percentage is not a zero-percent return.

Common mistakes

Entering revenue instead of gross profit overstates benefits, because the model compares profit with spend. Counting setup work in both the setup cost and the monthly agency fee double-counts it. Extending the period raises ROI mechanically while monthly figures stay flat; keep the period fixed when comparing scenarios. Attributing all sales growth to search when some customers would have come anyway inflates the benefit side.

Frequently asked questions

Should I enter revenue or profit?

Gross profit. The spend side is money out, so comparing it with revenue would count the cost of goods as return. Estimate the profit on SEO-sourced sales and enter that.

Does this prove SEO caused the profit?

No. The calculator applies arithmetic to the profit you attribute; it cannot separate search-driven sales from ones that would have happened anyway.

Why is the payback under one month?

In the starting scenario the monthly profit far exceeds the monthly agency cost, so the $7,500 setup is recovered in about 0.9 months. Your own inputs can produce a very different figure.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate earthquake insurance premium and deductible exposure from dwelling coverage, rates, contents coverage and mitigation discounts.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Dwelling coverage ($): the coverage amount for the structure. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual rate per $1,000 dwelling coverage ($): the yearly rate applied per thousand dollars of dwelling coverage.
  • Contents coverage ($): the coverage amount for belongings.
  • Annual rate per $1,000 contents coverage ($): the yearly rate applied per thousand dollars of contents coverage.
  • Dwelling deductible (%): the deductible as a percentage of dwelling coverage, from 0 to 100. The calculator shows the dollar amount this represents.
  • Seismic mitigation discount (%): a percentage credit for mitigation work, from 0 to 100.
  • Annual policy fees ($): flat yearly fees added before the discount.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies each rate per $1,000 of its coverage, adds the fees for the gross premium, subtracts the mitigation discount for the annual premium, and shows the monthly equivalent. It also converts the percentage deductible into dollars against the dwelling coverage.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the gross premium is $1,990.00, the 8% discount removes $159.20, and the annual premium is $1,830.80, about $152.57 per month. The 15% deductible on 500,000 of dwelling coverage is $75,000.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated monthly premium is the headline; Estimated annual premium sits beside it.
  • Estimated deductible in dollars is the figure that matters at claim time; a percentage deductible on a large coverage amount is a large dollar figure, $75,000.00 here.
  • The limitations note says availability, deductibles and seismic territory vary widely and directs you to a licensed insurer for a binding quote.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the deductible as a small percentage and missing the dollar amount; 15% of 500,000 is $75,000.00 out of pocket before the policy pays on the dwelling.
  • Entering rates per $100; this page's rates apply per $1,000 of coverage, as the field labels say.
  • Applying the discount to the deductible; the mitigation discount applies to the gross premium only.
  • Assuming standard homeowners coverage includes earthquakes; this page prices separate earthquake coverage, and actual availability and terms come from a licensed insurer, per the limitations note.

Frequently asked questions

Is the deductible really that high?

Percentage deductibles are how earthquake policies commonly work, and the calculator converts yours to dollars so the out-of-pocket figure is explicit: $75,000.00 on the illustrative inputs. Actual deductible options come from the insurer.

Is this a binding quote?

No. The limitations note says availability, deductibles, retrofit credits, soil, construction and territory vary widely, and directs you to a licensed insurer for a binding quote.

Why are dwelling and contents rates separate?

Structure and belongings price differently, so the model keeps two rate fields; the gross premium is their sum plus fees.

Important limitations

Actual availability, deductibles, retrofit credits, soil, construction and seismic territory vary widely. Use a licensed insurer for a binding quote.

What this calculator estimates

Find the greatest common factor and least common multiple of two whole numbers.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • First whole number: one of the two numbers to compare. It must be a nonzero whole number, as the limitations note says.
  • Second whole number: the other number. Order does not matter; the greatest common factor and least common multiple are the same either way.

Method and assumptions

The calculator finds the greatest common factor of the two numbers, then computes the least common multiple as the product of the numbers divided by that factor, and reports the product and whether the numbers are coprime, meaning their greatest common factor is 1.

With the starting inputs 84 and 126: the greatest common factor is 42, the product is 10,584, and 10,584 divided by 42 gives the least common multiple 252. The numbers are not coprime, because they share the factor 42.

Reading the result

  • Greatest common factor is the largest whole number dividing both inputs; 84 is 42 x 2 and 126 is 42 x 3.
  • Least common multiple is the smallest value both numbers divide into evenly; 252 is 84 x 3 and 126 x 2.
  • The product line lets you check the relationship: product divided by the factor gives the multiple, here 10,584 and 252.

Common mistakes

  • Entering zero or a decimal; the calculator requires nonzero whole numbers and rejects others.
  • Confusing the two results: the factor is never larger than the smaller input, and the multiple is never smaller than the larger input.
  • Assuming coprime means prime; 84 and 126 are both composite, and coprime would only mean they share no factor beyond 1.
  • Multiplying the two numbers and calling that the least common multiple; that works only when the numbers are coprime.

Frequently asked questions

What does coprime mean here?

The two numbers share no common factor above 1. The Are-the-numbers-coprime line answers it directly; for 84 and 126 the answer is No because 42 divides both.

Why is the product shown?

Because the least common multiple equals the product divided by the greatest common factor. Showing the product makes that arithmetic visible and checkable.

Does order matter?

No. Swapping the inputs changes nothing; both results describe the pair, not the order.

Important limitations

Inputs must be nonzero whole numbers within ordinary browser integer precision.

Exact products and common factors

For 18 and 24, the greatest common factor is 6 and the least common multiple is 72. The identity |a×b| = GCF × LCM checks the result. Inputs must be nonzero safe integers; the LCM and product use integer arithmetic so multiplying two valid components does not silently round the result. Signs do not change the positive common factor or multiple.

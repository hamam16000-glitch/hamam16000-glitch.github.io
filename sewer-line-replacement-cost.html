What this calculator estimates

Estimate a sewer-line replacement budget from pipe length, excavation, access, restoration, permits and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Sewer line length (feet): the length of line being replaced, from the house to the connection point. Use the measurement from the plumber's camera or site sketch.
  • Pipe replacement cost per foot ($): the per-foot rate for the replacement method you are actually getting, trenchless or open trench. Rates differ by method, so match the rate to the method.
  • Excavation and access cost ($): the fixed amount for digging and access, only when the per-foot rate excludes it. Trenchless quotes often bundle this.
  • Driveway, landscaping and interior restoration ($): the fixed amount to restore surfaces disturbed by the work. Enter zero for trenchless work that disturbs nothing.
  • Permits, inspection and cleanout work ($): the fixed total for permits, required inspections and cleanout installation.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for unknowns like rock, groundwater or utility conflicts.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the line length by the per-foot rate, adds the fixed excavation, restoration and permit amounts, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the length for an all-in cost per foot.

With the starting inputs: 90 feet at $190 gives $17,100.00 for the pipe work, fixed costs add $12,700.00, and the $29,800 subtotal at 12 percent contingency adds $3,576.00, for a total of $33,376.00, or $370.84 per foot.

Reading the result

  • Pipe replacement estimate is the only line the length and rate move directly.
  • Access, restoration and permit costs can rival the pipe work itself on jobs with hard digging or surfaces to restore.
  • Estimated total cost per foot bundles the fixed costs, so short replacements show a much higher per-foot figure than the bare rate.

Common mistakes

  • Using an open-trench per-foot rate for a trenchless quote, or the reverse; the methods price differently.
  • Counting excavation inside a bundled per-foot quote and again in the excavation field.
  • Forgetting surface restoration, then facing driveway or landscaping bills after the pipe work is done.
  • Comparing the all-in per-foot result to an advertised trenchless rate that excludes restoration and permits.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my per-foot result double the quoted rate?

The result divides the total, including excavation, restoration, permits and contingency, by the length. The bare quoted rate usually covers only the pipe work. Compare like with like.

Where does street or sidewalk work go?

Add the quoted amount to the restoration field. The limitations note flags street work and municipal requirements as real cost drivers.

Should I model trenchless and open trench separately?

Yes. Run the calculator once per method with that method's rate and its real restoration costs, then compare the totals.

Important limitations

Depth, access, trenchless methods, street work, soil, utility conflicts, pipe material, emergency timing and municipal requirements vary.

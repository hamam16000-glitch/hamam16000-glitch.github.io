What this calculator estimates

Estimate business cash runway from available cash, recurring revenue, operating expenses, debt payments, planned spending and a minimum reserve.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current unrestricted cash ($): cash you can freely spend today. It cannot be negative.
  • Monthly cash revenue ($): cash collected in a typical month.
  • Monthly operating cash expenses ($): recurring monthly cash spending before debt payments.
  • Monthly debt payments ($): required loan and credit payments per month.
  • Planned one-time spending ($): committed one-off spending, such as equipment or a hire package.
  • Minimum cash reserve ($): the cash floor you will not go below; runway stops at this floor rather than at zero.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds operating expenses and debt payments, subtracts revenue for the monthly net burn, subtracts planned spending and the reserve from cash for the spendable amount, and divides that by the burn for the runway to the reserve floor. It also shows the runway to zero cash and the monthly revenue needed to break even.

With the illustrative starting inputs: net burn is 185,000 plus 12,000 minus 120,000, which is $77,000.00 a month. Spendable cash is 750,000 minus 90,000 minus 100,000, which is $560,000.00, so the runway is 560,000 over 77,000, which is 7.3 months. To zero cash it is 660,000 over 77,000, which is 8.6 months, and break-even revenue is $197,000.00 a month.

Reading the result

  • Months of runway is the headline: 7.3 months on the starting inputs, the time until cash reaches your reserve floor at the entered burn.
  • The zero-cash line (8.6 months) is the absolute outer bound without the reserve, so the gap between the two lines is the protection the reserve and planned spending buy.
  • Break-even revenue ($197,000.00) equals the entered expenses plus debt payments; monthly revenue of 120,000 sits $77,000.00 below it, which is exactly the burn.

Common mistakes

  • Entering restricted cash or undeposited checks in the balance; the model assumes the balance is spendable now.
  • Forgetting debt payments in the expense field or double-counting them in both fields; they belong in the debt field only.
  • Leaving the reserve at zero by habit; the headline then measures time to empty, not time to your safety floor.
  • Reading runway as a forecast date; it assumes flat entered rates, and the limitations note says real revenue and costs move.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if revenue covers expenses and debt?

The headline reads Cash-flow positive, because the balance does not fall at the entered rates, so no finite runway exists.

Why subtract planned spending before dividing?

Because that cash is already committed. Runway measured on it would overstate how long the remaining balance lasts.

Does the break-even line include one-time spending?

No. It is the recurring revenue level that covers ongoing expenses plus debt payments, $197,000.00 on the starting inputs. One-time spending is handled through the spendable-cash figure instead.

Important limitations

Collections timing, seasonality, financing, taxes, payroll cycles, restricted cash and sudden cost changes can make actual runway shorter or longer.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate cash down payment needed to reach a target monthly car payment.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Vehicle price plus fees and tax ($): the full on-the-road price, including dealer fees and sales tax, so the down payment covers everything being financed.
  • Trade-in equity ($): what your trade-in is worth after any loan on it is paid off. It acts like a first chunk of the down payment.
  • Target monthly payment ($): the most you want to pay per month on the loan.
  • Loan APR (%): the annual rate expected on the auto loan.
  • Loan term (months): the repayment length. It must be at least 1.

Method and assumptions

The calculator works backward from the target payment: it finds the largest loan whose amortized payment at the entered APR and term equals the target, then subtracts that loan and the trade-in equity from the full price. The remainder is the cash down payment needed. It also reports the down payment as a share of the price and the amount financed.

With the illustrative starting inputs: a $600.00 payment over 72 months at 6.75% supports a loan of $35,441.69. The price is 48,000 and the trade-in covers 7,000, so the cash down payment is 48,000 minus 7,000 minus 35,441.69, which is $5,558.31. That is 11.58% of the price, and the amount financed is $35,441.69.

Reading the result

  • Down payment needed is the headline: $5,558.31 on the starting inputs, the cash required on top of the trade-in to land on the target payment.
  • The percentage line (11.58%) frames the down payment against the price, useful when a lender or plan quotes down payments in percent.
  • The financed line equals the maximum loan: the whole price gap not covered by cash and trade-in.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the sticker price without fees and tax; the field expects the full price, or the financed amount and down payment both come out low.
  • Treating a trade-in with a loan on it at full value; only the equity counts, so subtract the payoff first.
  • Setting an unrealistically low target payment; the needed down payment can then exceed the price minus trade-in, and the page floors it at zero with the financed line showing the shortfall.
  • Assuming the quoted APR is available; the actual rate comes from the lender, and a higher rate raises the down payment needed for the same target payment.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a higher APR raise the required down payment?

Because the same target payment supports a smaller loan when interest costs more. The gap has to come from somewhere, and here it comes from cash down.

What if I put nothing down?

Set the target high enough that the maximum loan covers the price minus trade-in. The down payment line then reads $0.00 and the financed line equals the gap.

Is the trade-in included in the percentage?

No. The 11.58% on the starting inputs is the cash portion only, against the full price.

Important limitations

Approval, lender fees, taxes, insurance and negative trade equity can change the cash needed.

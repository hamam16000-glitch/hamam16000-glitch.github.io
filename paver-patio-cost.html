What this calculator estimates

Estimate a paver patio installation budget. Starting figures are illustrative example inputs, not market prices or recommendations; enter your own measurements and written quotes.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Patio square feet: the finished patio area, length times width. Measure only the area being paved, not the whole yard.
  • Low installed cost per sq ft ($): the lowest written per-square-foot quote for a basic paver and standard pattern.
  • Typical installed cost per sq ft ($): the per-foot quote that best matches the paver and pattern you actually expect to choose.
  • High installed cost per sq ft ($): the highest per-foot quote you would still consider, such as premium pavers with a complex pattern or borders.
  • Excavation and drainage ($): the fixed amount for excavation, grading and drainage work, but only when your per-foot quotes exclude them. If a quote already includes base preparation, leave this lower or at zero.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer applied to everything. Use it for unknowns like poor soil discovered during excavation; it is not a price source on its own.

Method and assumptions

For each of your three rates the calculator adds the fixed work once and then applies the contingency percentage to the whole amount: scenario = (square feet x rate + fixed) x (1 + contingency/100). The headline number is the scenario built from your typical rate. The breakdown line labelled total excluding base fixed charges subtracts the fixed work itself but keeps its contingency share.

With the starting figures (400 sq ft, $12 / $22 / $40 per foot, $1,000 fixed, 10% contingency): low = (400 x $12 + $1,000) x 1.10 = $6,380.00, typical = (400 x $22 + $1,000) x 1.10 = $10,780.00, high = (400 x $40 + $1,000) x 1.10 = $18,700.00, and the variable part of the typical scenario is $8,800.00.

How to compare quantities, quote scope and contingency

Reading the result

  • On larger areas the per-foot rates matter far more than the fixed work; on a small patio the same fixed amount would weigh much heavier per foot.
  • A wide range only shows how far apart your rates are; check whether the quotes assume the same pavers, pattern and base preparation before budgeting.
  • The typical scenario is the arithmetic on your typical rate, not a predicted invoice.

Common mistakes

  • Counting base preparation twice. If the per-foot quote already includes excavation and base work, entering it again in the fixed field inflates every scenario.
  • Measuring the wrong area. Entering the whole yard instead of the paved area multiplies the rates by space that is not being built.
  • Comparing unlike quotes. A basic paver with a running-bond pattern and a premium paver with borders are different scopes; enter them as separate scenarios.
  • Ignoring drainage. If the site needs drainage work and quotes exclude it, a zero fixed field understates every scenario.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my range so wide?

Because the low and high rates you entered are far apart, often reflecting different paver materials and patterns. The calculator only reflects the spread you enter; align the specification and re-enter.

Where does old concrete removal go?

Include it exactly once. If the per-foot quote includes demolition and removal, do not add it again. If it is billed separately, put it in the fixed field.

Should I budget the typical scenario?

Budget with the scenario whose quote you actually expect to accept. The typical line applies the arithmetic to the rate you marked typical; it is not a market average.

Limitations

Prefilled prices for paver patio cost are illustrative budgeting assumptions. Replace them with local written quotes and avoid including 'Excavation and drainage ($)' both in a unit quote and again as a fixed charge. A linear unit-cost model does not capture every scope, access, size, materials or regulatory requirement.

Match the quantity to the quoted scope

Use the finished patio area. Excavation, base depth, drainage, edging and disposal can dominate differences between bids, even when the visible paver material is similar.

At 400 square feet each extra $1 on a rate moves that scenario by $440 after the 10% allowance, so the rates do most of the work on larger patios, while the $1,000 fixed excavation shifts all three scenarios by the same $1,100.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate heat-pump installation cost, incentives, annual energy savings and simple payback using editable equipment and utility assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Heat-pump equipment ($): the price of the heat-pump unit itself. It cannot be negative.
  • Installation labor and materials ($): the installer's labor and fitting materials for the project.
  • Electrical upgrades ($): panel or wiring work the installation needs.
  • Permits and other work ($): permit fees and any other project work not covered above.
  • Rebates and incentives ($): the total you expect to receive back from rebate or incentive programs.
  • Current annual heating and cooling cost ($): what you spend per year on heating and cooling today.
  • Estimated annual cost with heat pump ($): what you expect to spend per year on heating and cooling after the project.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds equipment, labor, electrical and other work for the gross project cost, subtracts the incentives for the net cost, subtracts the new annual energy cost from the old one for annual savings, divides the net cost by the annual savings for a simple payback in years, and shows the savings per month.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the gross cost is 10,500 plus 6,500 plus 1,800 plus 900, which is $19,700.00, and the net cost is $19,700.00 minus 3,000, which is $16,700.00. Annual savings are 2,800 minus 1,750, which is $1,050.00, or $87.50 a month, so the payback is $16,700.00 over $1,050.00, which is 15.9 years.

Reading the result

  • The net project cost is the headline: $16,700.00 on the starting inputs, the $19,700.00 gross minus the entered incentives.
  • Annual savings ($1,050.00, or $87.50 a month) come entirely from the two energy figures you enter, so the payback moves directly with them.
  • The payback (15.9 years here) is a simple division of net cost by annual savings; it assumes both stay flat, which the limitations note calls out.

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting rebates yourself and entering a net figure in the equipment field; enter the full amounts and let the model subtract the incentives once.
  • Entering monthly energy bills in the annual cost fields; both energy fields are per year, so a monthly figure understates the savings.
  • Treating the payback as a forecast; it divides today's net cost by the entered savings and holds them flat, as the limitations note says.
  • Entering a new annual cost above the old one and expecting a year count; with no positive saving the payback line reads No positive payback.

Frequently asked questions

What if incentives are larger than the project cost?

The net cost floors at zero; the model does not produce a negative project cost.

Does the payback include maintenance or repairs?

No. It divides the net project cost by the annual energy saving you entered. Upkeep is outside the model, as the limitations note says.

Where do the rebate figures come from?

From what you enter. Check each program's own current terms for what you qualify for before relying on the net figure.

Important limitations

Sizing, climate, ducts, backup heat, utility rates, incentives and tax eligibility vary. Energy savings require a proper load calculation and contractor proposal.

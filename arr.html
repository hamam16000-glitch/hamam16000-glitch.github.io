What this calculator estimates

Calculate current and projected annual recurring revenue from monthly recurring revenue, annual contracts, expansion and recurring churn assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current monthly recurring revenue ($): the recurring subscription revenue you bill in a typical month. It cannot be negative.
  • Additional annual recurring contract revenue ($): recurring revenue billed on annual contracts, stated per year, on top of the monthly figure.
  • Expected annual expansion rate (%): the percentage you expect recurring revenue to grow over a year from upgrades and seat growth, from 0 up.
  • Expected annual recurring-revenue churn (%): the percentage of recurring revenue you expect to lose over a year to cancellations and downgrades, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator annualizes the monthly recurring revenue, adds the annual contract revenue for current annual recurring revenue, applies the expansion rate to add expected growth, applies the churn rate to subtract expected losses, and reports the net as projected ARR.

With the illustrative starting inputs: annualized MRR is 85,000 times 12, which is $1,020,000.00, and current ARR is $1,020,000.00 plus 180,000, which is $1,200,000.00. Expansion adds 18% of that, $216,000.00, churn removes 10%, $120,000.00, so projected ARR is $1,296,000.00.

Reading the result

  • Projected annual recurring revenue is the headline: $1,296,000.00 on the starting inputs, current ARR plus entered expansion minus entered churn.
  • Current annual recurring revenue ($1,200,000.00) combines the annualized monthly figure ($1,020,000.00) with the annual contracts, so the annualized line shows how much of the base comes from monthly billing.
  • The expansion and churn lines ($216,000.00 and $120,000.00 here) show which assumption moves the projection more; on these inputs growth outweighs loss by $96,000.00.

Common mistakes

  • Entering total monthly revenue including one-off setup or usage fees; the model expects recurring revenue only, and the limitations note lists what it leaves out.
  • Entering the annual contract figure as a monthly amount; that field is per year, so a monthly value understates the base twelve-fold.
  • Entering churn or expansion as decimals like 0.1 instead of 10; the fields take percentages.
  • Reading projected ARR as a forecast of cash; it is the entered growth and loss rates applied to the current base, nothing more.

Frequently asked questions

Does projected ARR count bookings or one-off fees?

No. The model applies your expansion and churn percentages to the recurring base only. Bookings, implementation fees and usage revenue are outside it, as the limitations note says.

Why is the annual contract revenue separate from MRR?

So the annualized line can show how much of the base is monthly billing. The two are added for current ARR either way, so enter each contract in the field that matches how it is billed.

What happens if churn is higher than expansion?

Projected ARR comes out below current ARR by the difference. With churn at 18% and expansion at 10% on the starting inputs, the projection would be $1,200,000.00 minus $96,000.00, which is $1,104,000.00.

Important limitations

Bookings, implementation fees, usage revenue, foreign exchange, contract start dates, downgrades and revenue-recognition rules are not fully modeled.

Keep annual contracts out of MRR when adding them separately

Monthly recurring revenue is annualized by multiplying by 12. The additional annual-contract field is then added, so do not also include those same contracts in MRR. Expansion and churn are hypothetical percentages applied to that combined base. For $10,000 MRR and $20,000 genuinely additional annual contracts, current annualized recurring revenue is $140,000. The resulting projected ARR is a scenario run rate rather than recognized revenue or cash collections.

    Count costs and received value consistently

    Enter the initial cost, any additional costs included in your comparison, and the total amount received or remaining value at the end. The received amount includes the recovery of invested principal; it is not just the gain. When combining a final asset value and distributions, avoid counting a distribution twice if it is already reflected in another entered amount.

    Total return and an annualized equivalent

    Total cost = initial cost + additional costs
    Net gain or loss = received value − total cost
    ROI = net gain ÷ total cost × 100
    Annualized equivalent = [(received value ÷ total cost)1/years − 1] × 100

    Annualization treats all included costs as paid at the start and all receipts as received at the end. It compresses the whole interval into one equivalent annual growth rate. It does not use the actual dates of intermediate deposits, payments or distributions.

    An example where time changes the interpretation

    1,100 of modeled costs and 1,320 received

    Initial cost 1,000 plus additional costs of 100 gives a cost basis of 1,100. The gain is 220 and the total ROI is 20%. Over two years, the annualized equivalent is approximately 9.544512%, because 1,100 multiplied by 1.2 reaches 1,320 over the entire period.

    A loss and a zero-cost boundary

    Receiving 800 against 1,000 of costs gives a −20% ROI. Receiving an amount against zero modeled cost still allows an amount gain to be shown, but percentage ROI is undefined, not zero.

    When to use a dated cash-flow model instead

    The annualized equivalent is not a money-weighted return for irregular cash flows. Costs paid later and proceeds received earlier can produce a different result under a model that uses their actual dates. The calculator also does not apply tax rules, financing costs, inflation or fees you do not enter. An entered future value is a scenario assumption, not a predicted sale price or a promised return. Extremely large annualized results can fall outside the supported numeric range even when an amount gain is calculable.

    ROI questions

    Why divide by cost instead of the received amount?

    ROI compares the gain or loss with the resources used to obtain it. The received amount is not the denominator in the stated formula.

    Can I set elapsed time to zero?

    Only when annualization is switched off. An annualized equivalent needs a positive time interval.

    Does 20% total ROI mean 20% each year?

    No. Total ROI covers the whole selected scenario. The annualized figure uses a separate compounding calculation and timing assumption.

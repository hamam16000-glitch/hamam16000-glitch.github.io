What this calculator estimates

Estimate a planning budget for cyber insurance premiums using revenue, employee count, risk level, coverage and deductible assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual business revenue ($): the year's revenue, used for the revenue-based part of the premium.
  • Number of employees: the headcount, used for the per-employee part.
  • Base rate per $1,000 revenue ($): the rate applied per thousand dollars of revenue.
  • Additional annual cost per employee ($): the flat annual amount added per employee.
  • Industry and controls risk multiplier: a factor for industry and security controls, where 1 means no adjustment.
  • Coverage-limit multiplier: a factor for the chosen coverage limit, where 1 means no adjustment.
  • Deductible credit (%): a percentage credit off the adjusted premium, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds a revenue-based part, revenue per $1,000 times the rate, and an employee-based part, headcount times the per-employee cost, then applies the two multipliers and the deductible credit. It also shows the monthly equivalent and the premium as a percentage of revenue.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the revenue part is $2,250.00 and the employee part is $900.00, totaling 3,150 before adjustments. The factors 1.25 and 1.1 and the 5% credit bring the estimated annual premium to $4,114.69, about $342.89 per month, or 0.08% of revenue.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual premium is the headline; Monthly equivalent divides it by 12.
  • Revenue-based and Employee-based parts show the mix; on these inputs revenue carries most of the cost.
  • Premium as percentage of revenue is a comparison figure across company sizes; 0.08% here is the illustrative inputs' outcome, not a benchmark.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the percentage-of-revenue line as an industry benchmark; it restates your own inputs, and the limitations note says the formula is a budgeting model, not an insurer rating plan.
  • Entering monthly revenue; the revenue field is annual.
  • Setting both multipliers to 1 and assuming the result is a base quote; the starting values are illustrative and real pricing weighs data types, controls and claims, per the limitations note.
  • Forgetting the deductible credit is a percent from 0 to 100, not a dollar amount.

Frequently asked questions

Is this how insurers rate cyber policies?

No. The limitations note is explicit: this is a budgeting model, not an insurer rating plan or quote; data types, controls, vendors, limits and underwriting drive real premiums.

Why does headcount affect the estimate?

The model charges a flat amount per employee on the theory that more staff means more exposure to phishing and account compromise; the per-employee figure is your editable input.

Can I use the percentage of revenue to compare insurers?

Use it only to compare your own scenarios here; cross-insurer comparison needs their actual quotes, since each rates differently.

Important limitations

Cyber premiums depend on data types, controls, claims, vendors, revenue, limits, sublimits and underwriting. The formula is a budgeting model, not an insurer rating plan or quote.

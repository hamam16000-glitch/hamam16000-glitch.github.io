What this calculator estimates

Compare annual gasoline and electric-vehicle energy costs for the same driving distance.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual miles driven: the miles you drive in a year. It cannot be negative.
  • Gas vehicle fuel economy (MPG): the gas car's miles per gallon. It must be above 0.
  • Gas price per gallon ($): what you pay for gasoline. It cannot be negative.
  • EV energy use (kWh per 100 miles): the electric car's consumption. It must be above 0.
  • Electricity price per kWh ($): what you pay for electricity. It cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator divides annual miles by MPG and multiplies by the gas price for the gas car's yearly cost, divides miles by 100 and multiplies by kWh use and the electricity price for the EV's yearly cost, then shows the yearly difference and the five-year difference.

With the illustrative starting inputs: gas costs 12,000 over 28 times 3.5, which is $1,500.00 a year, while the EV costs 120 times 30 times 0.17, which is $612.00. The EV saves $888.00 a year, or $4,440.00 over five years.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the yearly difference: the EV saves $888.00 a year on the starting inputs.
  • The two cost lines ($1,500.00 gas, $612.00 electric) let you see which side of your own assumptions moves the answer.
  • The five-year figure ($4,440.00) simply scales the yearly gap; it assumes both prices and your mileage stay flat.

Common mistakes

  • Entering MPGe or range in the kWh field; the model expects consumption per 100 miles.
  • Mixing monthly miles with annual prices; the miles field is annual.
  • Reading the five-year figure as a forecast; it multiplies the current yearly gap by five with flat prices.
  • Comparing fuel costs alone when the vehicles differ in price; the model covers energy cost only, so purchase price, insurance and maintenance belong in a fuller comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What energy costs does this cover?

Fuel only: $1,500.00 for gas against $612.00 for electricity on the starting inputs. Purchase price, maintenance, insurance and charging equipment are outside the model.

Why five years?

It is a fixed horizon the page applies, multiplying the yearly gap by five: $4,440.00 on the starting inputs. It is arithmetic, not a forecast of prices.

What if gas or electricity prices change?

The gap moves with them. The model uses the prices you enter, so rerun it with current local prices before relying on the difference.

Important limitations

Vehicle price, charging losses, maintenance, insurance, taxes and changing energy prices are excluded.

Use comparable driving and energy assumptions

Keep annual miles equal when comparing gasoline and electricity. Home charging prices, public charging prices and charging losses can differ, while fuel economy and electricity use depend on the vehicle and conditions. The comparison includes only the purchase and running-cost fields shown; an energy saving alone is not the full ownership saving.

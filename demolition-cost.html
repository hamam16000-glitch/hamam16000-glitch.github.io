What this calculator estimates

Estimate a building-demolition project cost from square footage, demolition rate, hazardous materials, disposal, utilities, permits and restoration.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Building area (sq ft): the floor area of the structure being demolished.
  • Demolition cost per sq ft ($): the per-foot demolition rate for the structure type, from a written quote.
  • Hazardous-material allowance ($): the fixed allowance for hazardous-material handling identified by the pre-demolition survey. Enter zero only when a survey rules it out.
  • Debris hauling and disposal ($): the fixed amount for hauling and disposal fees, which the limitations note flags as a real bid driver.
  • Utility disconnects and site safety ($): the fixed amount for disconnecting utilities and securing the site.
  • Permits, surveys and notifications ($): the fixed total for demolition permits, surveys and required notifications.
  • Grading and site restoration ($): the fixed amount to grade and restore the site afterward.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for surprises inside the structure.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the building area by the demolition rate, adds hazardous-material, disposal, utility, permit and restoration amounts as fixed costs, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the area for a per-foot figure.

With the starting inputs: 2,000 square feet at $10 gives $20,000.00 for the demolition itself, hazardous materials and disposal add $10,000.00, utilities, permits and restoration add $6,000.00, and the $36,000 subtotal at 10 percent contingency adds $3,600.00, for a total of $39,600.00, or $19.80 per square foot.

Reading the result

  • Area-based demolition is the only line the area and rate move; everything else is a fixed project cost.
  • Hazardous materials and disposal combines the hazard allowance and hauling; either can dominate on an older structure.
  • Estimated cost per sq ft includes all the fixed costs, so small structures show a much higher per-foot figure than the bare rate.

Common mistakes

  • Zeroing the hazardous-material allowance without a survey; the allowance exists because older structures often need it.
  • Forgetting site restoration, then facing grading costs after the demolition is done.
  • Counting disposal inside a bundled quote and again in the disposal field.
  • Comparing the per-foot result to an advertised rate that excludes hazmat, disposal and restoration.

Frequently asked questions

Where does foundation removal go?

There is no separate field. Add the quoted amount to the grading and restoration field, and note the limitations line lists foundation removal as a bid factor.

Do I really need the hazard allowance?

That depends on the pre-demolition survey, not on this page. If a qualified survey found nothing, enter zero; if no survey happened, the allowance is a placeholder until one does.

How do I model a partial interior demolition?

Use the area being gutted and the per-foot rate your contractor quotes for interior work, and zero the fields that do not apply, such as utility disconnects for a single room.

Important limitations

Structure type, access, recycling, hazardous materials, foundation removal, neighboring buildings, permits, utility coordination and disposal fees affect actual bids.

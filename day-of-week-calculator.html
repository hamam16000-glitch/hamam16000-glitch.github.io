What this calculator estimates

Find the weekday, day of year, ISO week and remaining days for a calendar date.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Year: the year, a whole value.
  • Month: the month, from 1 to 12.
  • Day: the day; the date must exist on the calendar.

Method and assumptions

The calculator looks up the weekday of the entered date and reports it with the date's day-of-year number, its week number, and how many days remain in the year.

With the illustrative starting inputs: July 25, 2026 is a Saturday, day 206 of the year, in week 30, with 159 days remaining.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the weekday: Saturday on the starting inputs.
  • The day-of-year line (206) counts from January 1, and the remaining line (159) is what is left of the 365 days after it, so the two always sum to the year's length.
  • The week number (30) follows the model's week-counting convention; different standards number the same week differently, so treat it as the model's own count.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a date that does not exist; the form rejects invalid calendar dates.
  • Reading the remaining line as including the entered day; it counts the days after it: 365 minus 206 is 159 on the starting inputs.
  • Comparing the week number across standards; the model's count can differ from other conventions, especially around year boundaries.

Frequently asked questions

How is the day-of-year figured?

By summing the month lengths up to the date: the end of June is day 181, and 25 more days lands on 206.

Does the model handle leap years?

Yes. It counts real calendar days, so a February 29 entry is accepted in a leap year and rejected otherwise.

Why do the day and remaining lines sum to 365?

They split the year at the entered date: 206 days through it plus 159 after it is the full 2026 year, which is not a leap year.

Important limitations

Dates use the proleptic Gregorian calendar and UTC to avoid local daylight-saving shifts.

Calendar labels are independent of local clock time

The date is treated as a calendar day in UTC for this calculation, so changing your device’s time zone should not shift the weekday. February 29 is accepted only in a leap year. The date system is the Gregorian calendar in the supported year range; historical local calendar reforms or holiday observance are outside the weekday result.

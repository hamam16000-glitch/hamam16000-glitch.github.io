What this calculator estimates

Estimate a simplified qualified business income deduction from entered business income, the deduction rate and the limitation assumptions you provide.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Qualified business income ($): the qualified business income. It can be 0.
  • QBI deduction rate (%): the deduction rate; the model accepts its built-in twenty percent only.
  • Taxable income before QBI deduction ($): the taxable income before this deduction. It can be 0.
  • Taxable-income limitation rate (%): the limitation rate; the model accepts its built-in twenty percent only.
  • Net capital gain including qualified dividends for the QBI cap ($): the net capital gain that reduces the limitation base. It can be 0.
  • Active QBI for the minimum, after confirming eligibility ($): the active business income used only for the model's minimum rule. It can be 0.
  • Additional limitation already calculated?: choose whether to apply an additional limit you already computed, and enter it below if so.

Method and assumptions

The calculator takes twenty percent of the qualified business income, caps it at twenty percent of the taxable income after removing net capital gain, applies any additional limit you enter, then floors the result at the model's minimum when the entered active business income reaches its threshold. It also shows the income-based amount, the taxable-income cap, and the additional limit line.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the income-based amount is $24,000.00, the cap is $30,000.00, no additional limit is entered, and no minimum applies, so the deduction is $24,000.00.

Worked example

QBI 100,000, taxable income 100,000 and net capital gain 80,000 give a 4,000 ordinary cap. An eligible active QBI scenario of 1,000 can instead produce the 2026 minimum of 400.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the deduction: $24,000.00 on the starting inputs, where the income-based amount is the binding figure because it sits under the $30,000.00 cap.
  • The cap line ($30,000.00) becomes binding when the taxable income is low relative to the business income; entering net capital gain shrinks that cap further.
  • The minimum rule only lifts a small deduction when the entered active business income reaches the model's threshold; it never raises the result above the computed amount otherwise.

Common mistakes

  • Entering gross revenue instead of qualified business income; what counts as qualified follows the applicable tax rules, not the model.
  • Entering a rate other than the model's built-in twenty percent; the page validates the rate fields against its own fixed values.
  • Reading the result as an allowed deduction; the model applies its own simplified cap and minimum, and the actual deduction depends on the filer's full situation and the applicable tax authority's rules, so confirm with a qualified tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the deduction $24,000.00 and not $30,000.00?

The model takes the smaller figure: twenty percent of the 120,000 business income is $24,000.00, under the $30,000.00 cap from the taxable income.

What does the net capital gain field do?

It reduces the cap's base: with 10,000 entered, the cap on the starting inputs would fall to twenty percent of 140,000, which is $28,000.00.

When does the minimum apply?

Only when the entered active business income reaches the model's threshold and the computed deduction would otherwise be smaller; on the starting inputs no minimum applies.

Limitations

This tool does not determine eligibility or compute SSTB, wage/property phase-ins, losses, REIT/PTP components or cooperative reductions. Enter already eligible amounts and any required limitation; active-QBI eligibility must be confirmed separately. For cases outside this scope, use the IRS forms.

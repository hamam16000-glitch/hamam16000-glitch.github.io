What this calculator estimates

Estimate a mortgage escrow shortage or surplus and the adjusted monthly escrow deposit using editable tax, insurance and cushion assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual property tax ($): the yearly property tax bill paid through escrow. It can be 0.
  • Annual homeowners insurance ($): the yearly insurance premium paid through escrow. It can be 0.
  • Other annual escrow items ($): any other yearly amounts paid through the account. It can be 0.
  • Current monthly escrow deposit ($): what you currently pay into escrow each month. It can be 0.
  • Current escrow balance ($): the balance in the account today. It can be 0.
  • Required cushion (months): the months of escrow payments the account must hold in reserve. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator totals the annual escrow items, derives the monthly need, multiplies that by the cushion months for the required reserve, then projects the year-end balance as today's balance plus a year of deposits minus the annual bills. The gap between the required cushion and the projected balance is the shortage or surplus, and any shortage is spread over 12 months on top of the monthly need for the new deposit.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the annual items total 7,200 plus 2,400 plus 0, which is $9,600.00, so the monthly need is $800.00 and the two-month cushion is $1,600.00. The projected balance is 1,000 plus 700 times 12 minus $9,600.00, which is -$200.00, leaving a shortage of $1,800.00 and a new monthly deposit of $950.00.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the gap: a $1,800.00 shortage on the starting inputs, the cushion plus the amount the projected balance falls below zero.
  • The new monthly deposit ($950.00) covers both the $800.00 monthly need and a twelfth of the shortage, so the account recovers over the year on the model's assumptions.
  • A surplus appears instead when the projected balance exceeds the cushion, and the deposit then holds at the monthly need.

Common mistakes

  • Entering monthly tax or insurance figures; the three bill fields are annual, so monthly figures understate the need twelvefold.
  • Entering the full mortgage payment as the escrow deposit; the field takes only the escrow portion.
  • Reading the new deposit as a servicer quote; the model applies its own even 12-month spread, and the servicer's actual analysis follows its own schedule and rules, so confirm against the servicer's statement.
  • Ignoring a negative projected balance; a projected balance below zero means the entered deposits do not cover the bills at all, before any cushion is considered.

Frequently asked questions

How is the shortage calculated?

The model compares the required cushion with the projected year-end balance. On the starting inputs the cushion is $1,600.00 and the projection is -$200.00, a gap of $1,800.00.

How does the new deposit recover it?

It adds a twelfth of the shortage to the monthly need: $800.00 plus a twelfth of $1,800.00 gives $950.00 on the starting inputs.

Will my servicer compute the same figure?

Not necessarily. Servicers run their own analyses with their own cushion and spread rules under the loan's terms, so rely on the servicer's statement for the official amount.

Important limitations

Servicer escrow analyses use payment dates, account histories, legal cushion limits and shortage repayment options. This simplified estimate may differ from the official analysis.

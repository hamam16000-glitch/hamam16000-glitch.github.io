What this calculator estimates

Estimate the monthly disability-income benefit and waiting-period cash reserve you may need.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current monthly income ($): your monthly income before any disability.
  • Target income replacement (%): the share of income you would want replaced, from 0 to 100.
  • Existing monthly disability benefit ($): any monthly benefit you already have, from an employer plan for example.
  • Waiting period before benefits (months): the months before benefits would start.
  • Savings available during waiting period ($): savings you could draw on until benefits begin.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies income by the replacement target, subtracts the existing benefit for the monthly gap, and separately compares income during the waiting period against your savings for the waiting-period shortfall. It also shows the annualized gap.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the replacement target is 60% of 8,000, which is $4,800.00 per month; the existing $1,000.00 benefit leaves a monthly gap of $3,800.00, or $45,600.00 per year. The waiting-period shortfall is 3 months of income minus 10,000 of savings, which is $14,000.00.

Reading the result

  • Additional monthly benefit needed is the headline: the gap between your replacement target and what you already have.
  • Waiting-period shortfall is separate from the monthly gap; it asks whether savings cover the months before benefits start, $14,000.00 uncovered here.
  • This is a planning estimate only; the limitations note says insurers' definitions of disability, benefit caps and underwriting rules are outside it, so review options with a licensed professional.

Common mistakes

  • Entering annual income in the monthly field; the monthly figures drive both the gap and the waiting-period math.
  • Reading the annualized gap as a premium quote; it is the yearly size of the monthly shortfall, not a price for coverage.
  • Ignoring the waiting-period line; a policy can match your monthly target and still leave the waiting months uncovered if savings fall short.
  • Assuming the existing benefit is taxable or tax-free without checking; tax treatment changes what the benefit actually replaces, and this page does not model taxes, as the limitations note implies.

Frequently asked questions

Is the additional monthly benefit a recommendation?

No. It is the arithmetic gap between your entered target and existing benefit. Insurers use definitions of disability, benefit caps and occupation classes to set what they will actually offer, per the limitations note; a licensed professional can review your situation.

Why is the waiting period separate?

Because benefits start only after it ends; until then you live on savings, and the shortfall line shows whether the entered savings cover the entered months of income.

What replacement percentage should I target?

The 60% starting value is illustrative, not a standard. What you need depends on your expenses, other income and the policy terms available to you.

Important limitations

Insurers use definitions of disability, benefit caps, taxes, occupation classes, elimination periods and underwriting rules that this planning estimate does not model.

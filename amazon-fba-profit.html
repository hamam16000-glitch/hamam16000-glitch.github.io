What this calculator estimates

Estimate monthly Amazon FBA revenue, fees, contribution profit and margin using editable product, fulfillment, advertising and return assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Selling price per unit ($): the price the buyer pays per unit. It must be greater than zero.
  • Units sold per month: monthly sales volume. It must be at least 1.
  • Product cost per unit ($): what each unit costs you to buy or make.
  • Inbound shipping per unit ($): freight to the fulfillment center, per unit.
  • Amazon referral fee (%): the percentage of the sale price Amazon charges, from 0 to 100. Fee schedules change; use the current published rate for your category.
  • FBA fulfillment fee per unit ($): the per-unit fulfillment charge from the current fee schedule.
  • Storage and prep per unit ($): monthly storage and prep allocated per unit.
  • Advertising cost per unit ($): ad spend allocated to each unit sold.
  • Return and refund allowance (%): a revenue allowance for returns and refunds, from 0 to 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies price by units for monthly revenue, applies the referral percentage to that revenue, adds the per-unit fulfillment and storage charges for total fees, and reserves the return allowance as a percentage of revenue. Profit is revenue minus the return allowance, minus total fees, minus the per-unit product, inbound and advertising costs. It also shows profit per unit and profit as a percentage of revenue.

With the illustrative starting inputs: revenue is 34.99 times 500, which is $17,495.00. Referral fees are $2,624.25 and fulfillment plus storage adds $2,700.00, so total fees are $5,324.25. The return allowance at 4% is $699.80, and product, inbound and ad costs come to $7,000.00. Monthly profit is $17,495.00 minus $699.80 minus $5,324.25 minus $7,000.00, which is $4,470.95, or $8.94 per unit, a margin of 25.56%.

Reading the result

  • Estimated monthly profit is the headline: $4,470.95 on the starting inputs, before overhead, taxes and any cost you did not enter.
  • Profit per unit ($8.94) is the fastest way to compare products or price points; run each candidate and compare the per-unit line, not just the monthly total.
  • The margin line (25.56% here) tells you how much of each sales dollar survives the entered costs; the lower it is, the more sensitive profit is to fee or ad-cost changes. Fee schedules change, so keep the fee fields aligned with the platform's current published schedule, as the limitations note says.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the referral fee from memory; rates differ by category and change over time, so copy the current published schedule into the field.
  • Putting monthly ad spend in the per-unit advertising field; divide it by units first.
  • Forgetting inbound shipping or storage; both are per unit and scale directly with volume.
  • Treating the return allowance as a cost you only pay when returns happen; the model reserves it as a percentage of revenue every month, which is the more conservative reading.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include Amazon subscription or account fees?

No. It models per-unit economics from the fields shown. Fixed account charges, software and overhead are outside the model; subtract them separately for a full picture.

Why is my fee total different from my Seller Central statement?

Fee schedules change and vary by category, size tier and program, and this page models per-unit economics only. Even with every rate copied from the current published schedule, statement totals also reflect fixed account charges, promotions, taxes and refunds that sit outside the model, as the limitations note says. Use it for planning rather than statement reconciliation.

Can the return allowance exceed my real return rate?

Yes, and using a slightly higher allowance is a reasonable conservative habit. The model treats it as a revenue reduction, so a larger allowance lowers estimated profit.

Important limitations

Actual Amazon fees vary by category, size tier, storage season, inventory age, promotions, taxes, refunds and account terms. Keep every fee assumption current and editable.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate a possible mortgage prepayment penalty using either months of interest or a percentage of the remaining loan balance.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Remaining mortgage balance ($): the balance you would pay off early. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual interest rate (%): the note rate, used for the months-of-interest method.
  • Penalty method: months of interest or percentage of balance; the dropdown chooses which result becomes the headline.
  • Months of interest: how many months of interest the penalty charges under the months method.
  • Balance penalty (%): the percentage of the balance charged under the percentage method.

Method and assumptions

The calculator computes both penalty bases: one month of interest is the balance times the annual rate over 12, the months-of-interest penalty multiplies that by the entered months, and the balance-percentage penalty applies the entered percentage to the balance. The headline follows the selected method, and the final line adds the penalty to the balance for the total payoff.

With the illustrative starting inputs: one month of interest on 325,000 at 6.25% is $1,692.71. The months-of-interest penalty over 6 months is $10,156.25, the balance-percentage penalty at 2% is $6,500.00, and with the months method selected the remaining balance after adding the penalty is $335,156.25.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the penalty under the selected method: $10,156.25 with months of interest on the starting inputs.
  • Both method lines are always shown ($10,156.25 versus $6,500.00 here), so switching the dropdown only changes which one is the headline and which feeds the payoff total.
  • Whether a real loan has a prepayment penalty and how it is computed comes from the loan documents, as the limitations note says; this page prices the two common structures from your figures.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the headline without checking which method is selected; the dropdown controls it, and the two methods differ by $3,656.25 on the starting inputs.
  • Entering the original loan amount instead of the current balance; penalties apply to what remains.
  • Assuming every mortgage has a penalty; many do not, and the loan documents are the only source, as the limitations note says.
  • Forgetting the penalty in a refinance comparison; add it to the payoff when weighing a refinance, which is what the total line does.

Frequently asked questions

Which method applies to my loan?

Only the loan documents say. Select each method in the dropdown to price both, then use the one your note describes; if it describes neither, there may be no penalty to model.

Does the penalty reduce the benefit of paying off early?

Yes. Compare it against the interest you save by paying off or refinancing: on the starting inputs the months-method penalty of $10,156.25 is about 6 months of interest, so the payoff only wins if it saves more than that.

Why does the total line add the penalty to the balance?

Because that is the amount needed to walk away from the loan: the remaining balance plus the penalty the lender charges for early payoff.

Important limitations

Your note and applicable law control whether a penalty exists and how it is calculated. Some loans have declining schedules, exceptions or no penalty. Review the actual loan documents.

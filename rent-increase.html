What this calculator estimates

Estimate a proposed rent increase, annual revenue change and tenant cost under percentage or fixed-dollar assumptions.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Current monthly rent ($): the rent today. It can be 0.
  • Proposed rent increase (%): the percentage increase. It can be 0.
  • Additional fixed increase ($): a flat monthly increase on top of the percentage. It can be 0.
  • Number of units affected: the units the increase applies to. It must be above 0.
  • Months effective in first year: the months the increase is in effect during the first year, from 0 to 12.
  • Expected additional vacancy or concessions ($): the expected offset from vacancy or concessions. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the percentage and the fixed increase to the current rent for the new rent, then annualizes the monthly increase across the units and the effective months, and subtracts the expected vacancy offset for the net first-year impact. It also shows the increase per unit and the new rent's full-year value per unit.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the increase is 5% of 1,850, which is $92.50, the new rent is $1,942.50, the first-year impact across 4 units over 12 months is $4,440.00, no vacancy offset applies, and the new rent is worth $23,310.00 per unit over a full year.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the new monthly rent per unit: $1,942.50 on the starting inputs, a $92.50 increase.
  • The annual impact ($4,440.00) counts only the months the increase is effective; a mid-year start prorates it, which is why the months field matters.
  • The net line equals the annual line here because no vacancy offset is entered; the offset field exists so a realistic concession cost can be weighed against the increase.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the new rent instead of the current rent; the model computes the new rent from the current one and the increase fields.
  • Entering the effective months as a date; the field is a count of months, from 0 to 12.
  • Reading the annual impact as guaranteed income; it assumes full collection for the entered months, and actual results depend on the tenancies themselves. Any legal limits on increases follow the applicable local rules, so confirm with them.

Frequently asked questions

How does a mid-year start change the result?

The months-effective field prorates the impact: at 6 months on the starting inputs, the first-year impact would halve to $2,220.00 across the 4 units.

What is the per-unit annual line?

The new rent times twelve: $23,310.00 on the starting inputs, the full-year value of one unit at the new rent.

Does the model check whether the increase is allowed?

No. It computes the arithmetic only; any limits on increases follow the applicable local rules and the lease terms.

Important limitations

Local rent-control, notice, lease, subsidy and fair-housing rules may restrict increases. This is not legal advice and does not determine allowed rent.

What this calculator estimates

Measure annual pre-tax cash flow as a percentage of cash invested.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Total cash invested ($): the actual cash you put into the deal: down payment, closing costs and initial repairs. It must be greater than zero.
  • Annual pre-tax cash flow ($): the cash the investment returns in a year after operating costs and debt payments, before tax.

Method and assumptions

The calculator divides the annual pre-tax cash flow by the total cash invested for the cash-on-cash return, and also shows the same flow as a monthly figure and as a multiple of the invested cash.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the return is 12,000 over 100,000, which is 12.00%. That is $1,000.00 a month and a 0.120x multiple of the invested cash per year.

Reading the result

  • Cash-on-cash return is the headline: 12.00% on the starting inputs, the year's cash flow as a share of the cash you actually put in.
  • The monthly line ($1,000.00) restates the annual flow, which is easier to compare with a monthly mortgage or rent figure.
  • The multiple (0.120x) is the same ratio unannualized in percentage terms; at this rate the invested cash is repaid in a little over eight years if the flow stays flat.

Common mistakes

  • Using the property price instead of the cash invested; the return is measured on your cash, so a financed purchase with a small down payment shows a very different figure.
  • Entering pre-debt-service rental income; the flow must be after operating costs and loan payments, or the return is inflated.
  • Including tax effects; the model is pre-tax, as the field name says, and after-tax results differ by situation.
  • Comparing this percentage with an appreciation return; cash-on-cash counts cash flow only, not value changes or loan paydown.

Frequently asked questions

Is a higher cash-on-cash return always better?

It means more cash back per dollar invested, but it ignores appreciation, loan paydown, risk and how long the flow lasts. The limitations note keeps the scope to the two entered figures.

Why does the page not ask for the property value?

Because the ratio only needs the cash in and the annual cash out. Value matters for appreciation and equity, which this page does not model.

Can the annual flow be negative?

Yes, and the return then comes out negative, meaning the investment costs you cash each year at the entered figures.

Important limitations

Enter a positive cash investment and the cash flow after operating expenses and debt service. Include acquisition and renovation cash in the investment if relevant. Appreciation, principal repayment, income taxes and sale proceeds are outside this annual cash-flow measure. Negative cash flow produces a negative return.

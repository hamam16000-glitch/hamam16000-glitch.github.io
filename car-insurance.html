What this calculator does

Build an adjustable car-insurance budget from a base premium, vehicle count, driver count, coverage factor and discount.

How to use the calculator

Enter the monthly premium for one vehicle from a real quote as the base, then the number of vehicles and the number of drivers (whole numbers, 1 or more), a coverage factor and an expected discount percentage. A coverage factor of 1.00 leaves the base unchanged, and 1.10 adds 10%. The discount must be between 0 and 100.

Select Calculate to see the monthly budget after discount, the annual figure, the amount before discounts and the discount value. Change one input at a time to see how much each one moves the total.

How the calculation works

Before-discount monthly budget = base premium x vehicles x driver adjustment x coverage factor. The existing planning model uses driver adjustment = 1 + 0.12 x (drivers - 1). The discount is then subtracted, and annual budget is monthly budget x 12. The 12% adjustment is a model assumption, not an insurer rating rule.

Worked example

With a base premium of 150, one vehicle, one driver, coverage factor 1.00 and a 10% discount, the monthly budget is 135 and the annual budget is 1,620. At two drivers, the model applies a 1.12 adjustment before the discount.

How to interpret the result

The large figure is the monthly budget after the discount, and the annual line is that amount times 12. The before-discount line shows what the budget would be with no discount, so the difference is the discount value.

With the defaults (150 base, 1 vehicle, 1 driver, factor 1.00, 10% discount) the page shows $150.00 before discount, a $15.00 discount, $135.00 a month and $1,620.00 a year. For 2 vehicles, 2 drivers, factor 1.10 and the same discount, it shows $369.60 before discount, a $36.96 discount, $332.64 a month and $3,991.68 a year.

Treat the result as a household planning figure to compare against real quotes, not as a prediction of any insurer's price.

Assumptions and limitations

This is not an insurance quote. It does not model age, location, accident history, vehicle details, insurer rules or underwriting. Use a real quote as the base input and compare the result with the policy. The model does not determine eligibility or coverage.

Common mistakes

Entering an annual or six-month premium in the base field. The base is monthly and per vehicle.

Typing the discount as 0.10 instead of 10. The field expects a percentage.

Entering 0 drivers or vehicles. Both must be whole numbers of at least 1.

Entering the household total as the base and also setting a vehicle count above 1, which counts the vehicles twice.

Treating the 12% extra-driver step as an insurer rule. It is a fixed assumption in this model.

Privacy

This form needs only a premium amount, counts of vehicles and drivers, a factor and a discount. It does not ask for a plate number, VIN, license number or driving record. The calculation runs in your browser.

Frequently asked questions

Is the extra-driver adjustment an insurer rule?

No. The fixed 12% adjustment is a disclosed budgeting assumption in this model.

Does coverage factor 1.00 mean full coverage?

No. It means no change to the base premium in this arithmetic model.

Can I enter a discount over 100%?

No. Discounts must be between zero and 100%.

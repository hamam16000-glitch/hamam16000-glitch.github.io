What this calculator estimates

Estimate the gross and net value of unused paid time off.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Hourly pay rate ($): the current hourly rate. It can be 0.
  • Unused PTO hours: the accrued but unused paid time off in hours. It can be 0.
  • Payout percentage multiplier: the share of the hours the policy pays out, as a multiplier up to 2. It can be 0.
  • Estimated withholding rate (%): your estimate of the withholding on the payout. It can be 0 and cannot exceed 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the hourly rate by the unused hours and the payout multiplier for the gross payout, applies the withholding rate, and reports the net, the gross, the withholding, and the hours restated as eight-hour days.

With the illustrative starting inputs: 35 times 80 times 1 gives a gross of $2,800.00, the withholding at 22% is $616.00, so the net is $2,184.00, and the 80 hours are 10 days.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the net payout: $2,184.00 on the starting inputs, the gross after the estimated withholding.
  • The gross line ($2,800.00) is the full value of the unused hours at the current rate; the withholding line ($616.00) is the estimate of what the entered rate holds back.
  • The days line (10) restates the balance in eight-hour days, the unit most policies quote.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the balance in days; the field takes hours, and the model shows the day equivalent separately.
  • Reading the multiplier as a percentage; the field is a multiplier, so half payout is 0.5, not 50.
  • Reading the net as exact; the withholding rate is your own estimate, and the actual payout follows the employer's policy and payroll tables, so confirm with the employer's policy documents.

Frequently asked questions

What does the multiplier do?

It scales the paid hours for policies that pay a share or a premium of the balance: 80 hours at 1 pays $2,800.00 gross on the starting inputs.

Does the model know my employer's payout rules?

No. Whether unused time is paid out, and at what rate, follows the employer's policy and applicable law; the model only prices the scenario you enter.

Why restate the hours as days?

Policies and balances are often quoted in days, so the model shows 10 days alongside the 80 hours on the starting inputs.

Important limitations

Employer policy and state law determine whether PTO must be paid and whether all balances or payout percentages qualify.

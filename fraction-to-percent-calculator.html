What this calculator estimates

Convert a numerator and denominator to a percentage, decimal, simplified fraction and ratio.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Numerator: the top number of the fraction. It must be a whole number.
  • Denominator: the bottom number. It must be a nonzero whole number, as the limitations note says; the sign is normalized automatically when the denominator is negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator divides the numerator by the denominator, multiplies by 100 for the percentage, shows the decimal, simplifies the fraction by the greatest common divisor, and writes the simplified pair as a ratio.

With the starting inputs 3 and 8: 3 divided by 8 is 0.375, the percentage is 37.5%, the fraction is already simplified at 3/8 and the ratio is 3:8.

Reading the result

  • Percentage is the headline; it is the decimal value scaled by 100.
  • Simplified fraction shows whether the entered pair reduces; 3/8 does not, while an entry like 6/16 would simplify to 3/8.
  • Ratio is the simplified pair written with a colon, the same relationship in ratio notation.

Common mistakes

  • Entering decimals; the calculator asks for integer values and rejects non-integers.
  • Entering a zero denominator; the limitations note says it cannot be zero, and the calculator rejects it.
  • Reading 37.5% as 0.375% by misplacing the decimal point; the percentage is the decimal times 100.
  • Assuming percentages above 100 are errors; the limitations note says a numerator larger than the denominator is valid.

Frequently asked questions

What if my fraction is larger than one?

It converts normally to a percentage above 100, which the limitations note confirms is valid; for a mixed-number view use the improper-fraction-to-mixed-number calculator.

How does the simplification work?

Both numbers are divided by their greatest common divisor. For 3 and 8 that divisor is 1, so nothing changes; for 6 and 16 it is 2, giving 3/8.

Can the denominator be negative?

Yes; the sign is moved to the front of the value rather than left on the denominator, so the fraction display stays conventional.

Important limitations

The denominator cannot be zero. Large percentages are valid when the numerator is larger than the denominator.

A fraction can exceed one whole

The denominator defines the whole. For 7/4, divide 7 by 4 to obtain 1.75, then multiply by 100 to obtain 175%. A negative denominator is moved to the numerator in the simplified fraction. Zero over a nonzero denominator is valid and gives 0%; division by zero is undefined. Integer components must be within the exact supported input range.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate an agency’s monthly operating profit, margin, revenue per employee and revenue required to reach a target margin.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Monthly fee revenue ($): the agency's total client billing for one month. It cannot be negative.
  • Payroll and benefits ($): monthly salary and benefits cost for the team.
  • Contractor and freelancer cost ($): monthly spend on contractors and freelancers.
  • Software and tools ($): monthly subscriptions and tool costs.
  • Sales and marketing expense ($): monthly spend on winning new business.
  • Office and other overhead ($): rent, utilities and any other monthly overhead.
  • Full-time equivalent employees: the team size used for the revenue-per-employee line. It must be greater than zero.
  • Target operating margin (%): the profit margin you want to plan toward, from 0 up to (but not including) 100.

Method and assumptions

The calculator totals the five monthly cost categories, subtracts them from revenue for monthly operating profit, expresses that profit as a percentage of revenue for the margin, divides revenue by the team size, and works out the revenue needed to hit the target margin at the current cost base: costs divided by (1 minus the target margin).

With the illustrative starting inputs: the five cost lines total $263,000.00 (155,000 plus 38,000 plus 12,000 plus 22,000 plus 36,000). Profit is 320,000 minus $263,000.00, which is $57,000.00, a margin of 17.81%. Revenue per employee is 320,000 over 18, which is $17,777.78, and reaching a 20% margin at these costs needs revenue of $263,000.00 over 0.8 (that is, 1 minus the target), which is $328,750.00.

Reading the result

  • Monthly operating profit is the headline: $57,000.00 on the starting inputs. It is an operating estimate from the categories you entered, not accounting net income.
  • Operating margin of 17.81% sits just under the 20% target on the starting inputs, and the target line shows the gap in revenue terms: $328,750.00 of monthly billing would be needed at the current cost base.
  • Revenue per employee ($17,777.78 on the starting inputs) is a simple productivity check; compare it across months rather than treating any single reading as a benchmark.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing monthly and annual figures; every field on this page is monthly, so an annual salary total entered once inflates costs twelve-fold.
  • Leaving Full-time equivalent employees at zero; the page rejects it because revenue per employee cannot be computed.
  • Entering the target margin as a decimal like 0.2 instead of 20; the field takes a percent from 0 to 99.
  • Reading the target revenue as a forecast; it is the billing level that would produce the target margin only if costs stayed exactly as entered.

Frequently asked questions

Is the profit figure the same as net income?

No. It is an operating estimate from the revenue and cost categories you enter. Taxes, depreciation, financing and one-off items are outside the model; the limitations note says actual results depend on your full accounts.

Why does the target revenue depend on my costs?

Because the margin target is a percentage of revenue, so the required billing is the current cost base divided by (1 minus the target). Lower costs would lower the required revenue just as higher billing raises it.

Can I model a cost cut instead of more revenue?

Yes. Lower any cost field and recalculate; the margin and the target-revenue line both update, so you can compare the revenue path and the cost path to the same target.

Important limitations

Pass-through media, project timing, utilization, write-offs, owner compensation, taxes and capital costs may need separate treatment.

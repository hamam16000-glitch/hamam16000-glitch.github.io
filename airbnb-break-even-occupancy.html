What this calculator estimates

Estimate the occupancy rate and booked nights needed for a short-term rental to cover fixed and variable operating costs.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Average nightly rate ($): the nightly price guests pay, before platform fees.
  • Available nights per month: nights the listing can be booked in a month. It must be at least 1.
  • Platform fees (%): the percentage of the nightly rate the platform keeps, from 0 up to (but not including) 100.
  • Variable cost per occupied night ($): utilities, consumables and other per-night costs.
  • Monthly fixed costs ($): mortgage or rent, insurance and other costs that do not change with bookings.
  • Net cleaning fee contribution per stay ($): cleaning fee charged minus cleaning cost per stay; negative values are allowed if cleaning costs more than you charge.
  • Average stay length (nights): the typical booking length; it spreads the cleaning contribution across nights. It must be greater than zero.

Method and assumptions

The calculator works out the nightly contribution: the nightly rate after the platform percentage, minus the variable cost per night, plus the net cleaning contribution spread across the average stay. If that contribution is not positive, the page says so instead of computing. Break-even nights are the fixed monthly costs divided by the nightly contribution, and the headline occupancy is those nights as a percentage of the available nights. It also shows the booking revenue at break-even and how far the break-even sits below an 80% reference occupancy built into the model.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the nightly contribution is 220 times 0.85 minus 25 plus 10 over 3.5, which is $164.86. Covering 3,600 of fixed costs takes 3,600 over 164.86, which is 21.8 nights, an occupancy of 72.79% of the 30 available nights. Booking revenue at that point is $4,866.55, and the buffer to the 80% reference is 7.21%.

Reading the result

  • Break-even occupancy (72.79% on the starting inputs) is the headline: the share of available nights you must fill just to cover the entered costs, before any profit.
  • Nights needed (21.8) says the same thing in booking terms, which is easier to compare with an actual calendar.
  • The buffer line (7.21% on the starting inputs) compares the break-even with a fixed 80% reference occupancy built into the calculator, not with any market average; a negative buffer would mean the break-even sits above that reference.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the platform fee as a decimal like 0.15 instead of 15; the field takes a percent.
  • Putting the gross cleaning charge in the net cleaning field; the model wants fee charged minus cleaning cost, because only the difference contributes.
  • Forgetting that a non-positive nightly contribution stops the calculation; if platform fees and per-night costs exceed the rate, no occupancy level can break even on these inputs.
  • Treating the 80% buffer as an industry benchmark; it is a fixed reference inside the model, and the limitations note says local rules, seasonality and demand are outside this estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What does a break-even occupancy above 100% mean?

The entered costs cannot be covered even with every available night booked. To bring it down, raise the nightly rate or the net cleaning contribution, or lower platform, variable or fixed costs, then recalculate.

Why is my nightly rate reduced by the platform fee?

Because the model counts only money you keep. A 15% platform fee on a $220.00 rate leaves 85% of it, which is $187.00 before per-night costs.

Is the 80% buffer a recommended occupancy?

No. It is a fixed reference line inside the calculator so the spread is easy to read. What occupancy is realistic depends on your market and season, which this page does not estimate.

Important limitations

Seasonality, taxes, discounts, minimum stays, cleaning timing, cancellations, maintenance and local restrictions are not fully modeled.

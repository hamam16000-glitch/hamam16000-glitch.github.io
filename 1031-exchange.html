What this calculator estimates

Estimate taxable boot and deferred gain in a simplified like-kind exchange.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Relinquished property sale price ($): the sale price of the property given up. It can be 0.
  • Selling and exchange costs ($): the costs of the sale and the exchange. It can be 0.
  • Adjusted tax basis ($): the adjusted basis of the relinquished property. It can be 0.
  • Replacement property price ($): the price of the replacement property. It can be 0.
  • Cash retained from exchange ($): cash kept out of the exchange. It can be 0.
  • Net debt relief not replaced ($): debt relief not replaced in the exchange. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator nets the selling and exchange costs out of the sale price, takes the gain as the amount that net exceeds the adjusted basis (never below zero), then treats as taxable boot the smaller of the gain and the largest of the retained cash plus unreplaced debt relief or the shortfall when the replacement costs less than the net proceeds. The deferred gain is what remains of the gain after the boot.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the net proceeds are 900,000 minus 60,000, which is $840,000.00, the gain is $340,000.00 over the 500,000 basis, and because the 950,000 replacement costs more than the net proceeds and no cash or debt relief is retained, the boot is $0.00 and the full $340,000.00 is deferred.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the taxable boot: $0.00 on the starting inputs, because nothing is retained and the replacement property costs more than the net proceeds.
  • The gain line ($340,000.00) is the realized gain over basis; the deferred line ($340,000.00) is the part the model carries into the replacement property instead of taxing now.
  • Boot rises when cash is kept, debt relief is not replaced, or the replacement property is cheaper than the net proceeds; the model caps it at the gain.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the sale price without the selling costs; the model works from net proceeds, so enter both the price and the costs.
  • Entering the replacement property's equity instead of its full price; the shortfall test compares the full replacement price against the net proceeds.
  • Reading $0.00 of boot as proof no tax is due; the model only applies its own simplified boot arithmetic, and actual exchange treatment follows the taxpayer's own facts and the applicable tax rules, so confirm with a qualified tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the boot zero on the starting inputs?

The replacement property (950,000) costs more than the net proceeds ($840,000.00) and no cash or debt relief is retained, so the model finds no boot and defers the full $340,000.00 gain.

What creates boot in this model?

Retained cash, unreplaced debt relief, or buying a cheaper replacement property. The model taxes the smaller of the gain and the largest of those amounts.

Does this match my actual exchange?

Not by itself. The model uses simplified arithmetic on your figures; real exchange rules and their timing and identification requirements are outside it, so confirm with a qualified tax professional.

Important limitations

1031 exchanges require strict property, timing, intermediary and debt/equity analysis. Seek qualified tax and legal advice.

This is a boot and gain illustration

The entered tax basis and sale/exchange costs determine the gain used in the arithmetic. Cash retained and unreplaced debt relief affect the modeled boot. These inputs do not establish exchange eligibility, applicable deadlines, use of a qualified intermediary or recognition rules for every transaction. A replacement property price alone does not prove full tax deferral. Verify the transaction structure before treating a computed amount as a tax-return figure.

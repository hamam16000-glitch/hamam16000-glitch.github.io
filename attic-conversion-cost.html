What this calculator estimates

Estimate an attic conversion budget including finished area, stairs, insulation, HVAC, permits and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Finished attic area (square feet): the floor area that will become finished living space, not the whole roof footprint. Measure the area that meets your headroom and layout plan.
  • Base conversion cost per square foot ($): the per-foot rate for framing, drywall, flooring and finishes, from a written quote for the finish level you expect.
  • Stair and access work ($): the fixed amount for building code-compliant stairs or other access. This is often a large single line; include it exactly once.
  • HVAC, insulation and electrical work ($): the fixed total for extending heating and cooling, insulation and wiring into the attic, only when the per-foot rate excludes it.
  • Design, permits and inspections ($): the fixed total for plans, permits and required inspections.
  • Contingency (%): a percentage buffer on the subtotal for structural surprises like sistering joists or roof repairs.

Method and assumptions

The model multiplies the finished area by the base rate, adds the fixed stair, systems and permit amounts, then applies contingency. It also divides the total by the finished area, so fixed costs are spread across the square feet.

With the starting inputs: 750 square feet at $110 gives a base of $82,500.00. Stairs $12,000, systems $18,000 and permits $7,500 add $37,500.00, and contingency at 12 percent adds $14,400.00, for a total of $134,400.00, or $179.20 per finished square foot.

Reading the result

  • Base conversion estimate is the only line that scales with area and finish rate; stairs, systems and permits are fixed.
  • Estimated cost per finished square foot includes the fixed costs, so a smaller finished area shows a much higher per-foot figure with the same fixed work.
  • Estimated contingency is 12 percent of everything, reflecting how often structural surprises appear in conversions.

Common mistakes

  • Using the whole roof footprint instead of the area that will actually be finished to code.
  • Counting stair or HVAC work both in a bundled per-foot quote and in the fixed fields.
  • Leaving the systems field at the default when the existing HVAC already covers the attic.
  • Treating the per-foot result as a market rate; it carries your fixed stair, systems and permit costs inside it.

Frequently asked questions

Why is converting my small attic so expensive per foot?

Stairs, systems and permits are fixed amounts that barely change with area. Dividing the total by fewer finished feet concentrates those costs. This is a property of the arithmetic, not a market claim.

Does the base rate include insulation?

Only if your quote's per-foot rate includes it. Otherwise keep insulation inside the HVAC, insulation and electrical field so it is counted exactly once.

What about dormers or raising the roof?

The model has no dormer field. Add a contractor's quoted amount to the stair and access or systems field as a fixed cost, and read the limitations note: headroom and structure can materially change cost.

Important limitations

Headroom, structure, egress, fire code, roofing, plumbing, dormers, location and finish level can materially change the cost.

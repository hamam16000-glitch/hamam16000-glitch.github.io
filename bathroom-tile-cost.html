What this calculator estimates

Estimate bathroom tile installation cost for floors and walls including material, labor, waterproofing, demolition and contingency.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Floor tile area (sq ft): the floor area getting tile.
  • Wall and shower tile area (sq ft): the wall and shower area getting tile, measured separately from the floor; wall installation prices differently.
  • Tile material cost per sq ft ($): the tile price per square foot for the tile you plan. Tile size and pattern affect installation cost, as the limitations note says.
  • Floor installation per sq ft ($): the per-foot labor rate for floor tile.
  • Wall installation per sq ft ($): the per-foot labor rate for wall and shower tile, usually higher than floor work.
  • Waterproofing and backer board ($): the fixed amount for waterproofing and backer board, the substrate work the tile sits on.
  • Demolition and disposal ($): the fixed amount for removing old tile and disposal.
  • Trim, niches and accessories ($): the fixed amount for trim pieces, niches and accessories.
  • Waste and contingency (%): a combined percentage buffer covering tile waste and surprises. Pattern choice affects waste, as the limitations note suggests.

Method and assumptions

The model prices tile material on the combined floor plus wall area, prices floor and wall installation at their own per-foot rates, adds waterproofing, demolition and trim as fixed costs, then applies the waste and contingency percentage.

With the starting inputs: 260 square feet at $12 gives $3,120.00 in material, 80 floor feet at $18 plus 180 wall feet at $25 give $5,940.00 in installation, waterproofing, demolition and trim add $4,900.00, and the $13,960 base at 12 percent adds $1,675.20, for a total of $15,635.20.

Reading the result

  • Tile materials prices the combined floor and wall area at one material rate; different tiles for floor and wall need separate runs or an averaged rate.
  • Floor and wall installation separates the two rates; shower-heavy projects ride the higher wall rate.
  • Waste and contingency is the combined buffer line; complex patterns want a higher percentage.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing wall tile at the floor rate; the fields exist because the work differs.
  • Skipping waterproofing to lower the estimate before checking the actual quoted scope; waterproofing scope varies by project, so confirm what the quote for your bathroom includes before treating it as optional.
  • Leaving demolition at the default when there is no old tile to remove.
  • Using one material rate when floor and wall tiles price differently; run the calculator per surface or use a weighted rate.

Frequently asked questions

Why one material rate for floor and wall?

The model keeps one material field and prices the areas separately for labor. If your floor and wall tiles differ in price, run the calculator per surface or weight the rate by area.

Does the waste percentage cover pattern waste?

It is meant to: the field combines waste and contingency. Diagonal or complex patterns waste more, so raise the percentage for them.

Where do heated floors go?

There is no field; the limitations note lists heated floors. Add the quoted amount to the waterproofing and backer board field as a fixed cost.

Important limitations

Tile size, pattern, substrate condition, curbless showers, niches, plumbing changes, heated floors and local labor rates affect cost.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate refinance proceeds, cash left in a BRRRR deal, rental NOI, monthly cash flow and DSCR.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Purchase price ($): the price paid for the property. It can be 0.
  • Rehab cost ($): the renovation budget. It can be 0.
  • Closing and holding costs ($): the buying and holding costs before refinance. It can be 0.
  • After-repair value ($): the appraised value after rehab. It must be above 0.
  • Refinance loan-to-value (%): the share of the after-repair value the new loan covers. From 0 to 100.
  • Refinance interest rate (%): the annual rate on the new loan. It can be 0.
  • Refinance term (months): the new loan's length in months. It must be above 0.
  • Monthly gross rent ($): the expected monthly rent. It can be 0.
  • Vacancy allowance (%): the share of rent lost to vacancy. From 0 to 100.
  • Monthly operating expenses excluding debt ($): the monthly costs other than the loan payment. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator totals the purchase, rehab and closing costs as the money in, sizes the refinance loan at the entered loan-to-value on the after-repair value, and reports the cash left in the deal as the shortfall when the loan does not cover the money in. It then prices the loan payment, the rent after vacancy minus operating expenses as net operating income, and the monthly cash flow with its coverage ratio.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the money in is 185,000 plus 65,000 plus 18,000, which is 268,000, the loan is $255,000.00 at 75% of the 340,000 value, so $13,000.00 stays in the deal. The payment is $1,696.52 a month, the rent after 6% vacancy minus 1,050 of expenses gives $24,624.00 of annual net operating income, and the monthly cash flow is $355.48, a 1.21× coverage of the payment.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the cash left in the deal: $13,000.00 on the starting inputs, the part of the 268,000 invested that the $255,000.00 refinance does not pull back out.
  • The coverage figure (1.21×) is the monthly net operating income divided by the $1,696.52 payment; above one the rent covers the debt on these inputs.
  • The monthly cash flow ($355.48) is what remains after the payment; it is the model's arithmetic on the entered rent, vacancy and expenses, not a guaranteed return.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving closing and holding costs out of the money in; the model adds all three cost fields before comparing against the loan.
  • Entering the original purchase loan instead of the refinance terms; the loan the model prices is sized on the after-repair value at the entered loan-to-value.
  • Reading the cash left as profit; it is capital still tied up in the property, and the model does not price the sale or market risk of the deal.

Frequently asked questions

When does cash left reach zero?

When the refinance loan covers the total money in: on the starting inputs a loan of 268,000 or more against the 340,000 value, which is about 79% loan-to-value, would return everything.

What does the 1.21× figure mean?

The monthly net operating income divided by the loan payment. Above one the modeled rent covers the debt; below one the property runs at a monthly shortfall on the entered figures.

Does the model include repairs after refinance or taxes?

No. It counts the entered vacancy allowance and operating expenses only; insurance, taxes and future repairs belong in that expense field or sit outside the model.

Important limitations

Lender seasoning, appraisal, loan fees, taxes, reserves, capital expenditures, rehab overruns and interest during construction can materially change results.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate a contractor insurance budget combining general liability, workers compensation, tools coverage, commercial auto and umbrella insurance.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual contractor revenue ($): the year's revenue, used for the general-liability line.
  • General-liability rate per $1,000 revenue ($): the liability rate applied per thousand dollars of revenue.
  • Annual payroll ($): the year's payroll, used for the workers compensation line.
  • Workers compensation rate (%): the percentage of payroll for workers compensation.
  • Tools and mobile equipment value ($): the insured value of tools and mobile equipment for the inland-marine line.
  • Inland-marine rate (%): the percentage applied to the tools and equipment value.
  • Commercial vehicles: how many commercial vehicles to include.
  • Commercial-auto premium per vehicle ($): the annual premium for each vehicle.
  • Umbrella and excess premium ($): the flat annual cost of umbrella or excess coverage.
  • Policy fees ($): flat fees added at the end.

Method and assumptions

The calculator builds the estimate in four lines: general liability from revenue per $1,000, workers compensation as a percentage of payroll, inland marine as a percentage of tools value, and a remainder line of vehicles times per-vehicle premium plus umbrella plus fees. The headline is their sum.

With the illustrative starting inputs: general liability is $3,375.00, workers compensation is $33,800.00, inland marine is $2,520.00, and the vehicles, umbrella and fees line is $14,200.00, for a total of $53,895.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated total annual cost is the headline; the four lines below it show exactly where it comes from.
  • Workers compensation dominates the illustrative total at $33,800.00 of $53,895.00, because payroll percentages usually outweigh the flat lines on these inputs.
  • The limitations note warns that trade class, payroll codes, audits and claims materially change real costs; treat this as a budgeting breakdown and get insurer quotes for actual pricing.

Common mistakes

  • Entering monthly payroll; the workers compensation line uses annual payroll against the annual rate.
  • Mixing up the two percentage rates with the per-$1,000 liability rate; the field labels say which basis each uses.
  • Entering a fleet total premium in the per-vehicle field; the calculator multiplies by the vehicle count.
  • Treating the split as how insurers would package coverage; the four lines are a budgeting model, and real programs bundle and audit differently, per the limitations note.

Frequently asked questions

Why is workers compensation so large in the example?

Because it is a percentage of payroll, and payroll is large relative to the other bases on the illustrative inputs: 650,000 times 5.2% is $33,800.00. Your own inputs can shift the balance.

Is the umbrella premium estimated here?

No; you enter it as a flat figure from your own quote, and the calculator adds it to the total.

Does this replace insurer quotes?

No. Trade class, subcontractor use, payroll codes, claims, contracts and audits shape actual costs, as the limitations note says; this page organizes a budgeting estimate from your own rates.

Important limitations

Trade class, subcontractors, payroll codes, claims, contracts, states, vehicle use, limits and insurer audits can materially change actual costs.

Formula, worked scenarios and limitations

This is a planner using the percentages you enter

Enter gross pay for one paycheck, three percentage deduction assumptions, other deductions for that paycheck and the number of paychecks in a year. The form applies those percentages to the full entered gross amount. It does not look up tax brackets, withholding elections or annual payroll tax limits.

Use percentages taken from your own planning assumptions or a comparable pay statement. The starting 7.65% payroll-tax input is editable example data, not a determination that the same percentage applies to every worker, country, pay level or year.

Reproduce one paycheck before annualizing it

Percentage deductions = gross pay × (federal + state/local + payroll percentages) ÷ 100. Planned net pay = gross pay − percentage deductions − other deductions. Annualized net pay = that paycheck amount × the entered yearly paycheck count.

With gross pay of 3,000, rates of 12%, 5% and 7.65%, percentage deductions total 739.50. Subtracting another 250 leaves 2,010.50. Repeating an identical paycheck 26 times gives 52,273 for the year.

Biweekly and twice-monthly pay are different

Biweekly payroll commonly has 26 paychecks in a year; twice-monthly payroll has 24. The same 2,010.50 paycheck multiplied by 24 gives 48,252. Check the actual payroll calendar, including an extra pay period where applicable.

The yearly multiplier assumes the same gross pay and deductions every time. It does not recalculate withholding as year-to-date pay changes or apply a bonus schedule.

Inconsistent deductions are an error, not zero net pay

The three percentages cannot total more than 100% in this model. Percentage deductions plus the other deductions also cannot exceed gross pay. If they do, the form clears the result and asks for a consistent scenario instead of silently replacing a negative amount with zero.

Other deductions here are subtracted after the simple percentage calculation. The form does not give pretax retirement or benefit deductions their actual tax treatment. For U.S. federal withholding, consult the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator and payroll records; do not use this planner to prepare a return or set payroll automatically.

What this calculator estimates

Trade-In Equity Calculator

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Estimated trade-in value ($): what the dealer offers for your current vehicle. It cannot be negative.
  • Loan payoff balance ($): what you still owe on it. It cannot be negative.
  • Replacement purchase price before sales tax ($): the price of the next vehicle before tax. It must be above 0.
  • Additional cash down payment ($): extra cash you put in. It cannot be negative.
  • Sales tax rate where the trade-in qualifies for a credit (%): the tax rate applied with the trade-in credit. It cannot be negative.
  • Taxes and fees not offset by credit ($): other taxes and fees added to the deal. It cannot be negative.

Method and assumptions

The calculator subtracts the payoff from the trade-in value for the equity, computes the tax saving from applying the trade-in credit against the taxable price, adds positive equity, cash and the tax saving for the total applied, and sizes the new loan as price plus full tax and fees plus any negative equity minus everything applied.

With the illustrative starting inputs: equity is 26,000 minus 18,500, which is $7,500.00. The tax saving is 7% on the 26,000 credit, which is $1,820.00, so $7,500.00 plus 3,000 plus $1,820.00 applies $12,320.00 against the deal. The new loan is 42,000 plus its 7% tax of $2,940.00 plus 2,500 of fees, minus the $12,320.00 applied, which is $35,120.00.

Worked example

A 42,000 purchase, trade value 26,000, old loan 18,500, cash down 3,000, sales tax 7% and fees 2,500 give equity 7,500, tax credit 1,820 and financing 35,120.

Reading the result

  • The equity is the headline: $7,500.00 on the starting inputs, what the trade-in contributes after clearing its loan.
  • The tax credit ($1,820.00) is real money in the deal: the model taxes only the price net of the trade-in, which is why it computes the saving separately.
  • The financed amount ($35,120.00) is what the next loan must cover; negative equity would be rolled in and added to it, shown on its own line as $0.00 here.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the trade-in value as the equity; the model subtracts the payoff balance itself, so enter both figures.
  • Applying the tax rate to the full price yourself; the model already credits the trade-in against the taxable amount, up to the price.
  • Leaving negative equity out of the comparison; when the payoff exceeds the trade-in value, the model rolls the difference into the new loan and shows it on the negative-equity line.
  • Reading the tax credit as universal; the model applies the credit structure you enter, and trade-in tax treatment varies by jurisdiction, so confirm the rule that applies to your purchase before relying on the saving.

Frequently asked questions

How does the tax credit work in the model?

Tax is charged on the price minus the trade-in value, up to the price: 7% on 26,000 saves $1,820.00 on the starting inputs against the full $2,940.00 of tax. Whether your purchase qualifies for this treatment depends on local rules, so confirm before counting it.

What if I owe more than the trade-in is worth?

The equity goes negative, the model rolls the shortfall into the new loan, and the negative-equity line shows it. On the starting inputs the equity is positive, so that line is $0.00.

What is actually applied against the new purchase?

Positive equity plus cash plus the tax saving: $12,320.00 on the starting inputs, leaving $35,120.00 to finance.

Limitations

Purchase price is before sales tax. This calculation assumes the trade qualifies for a full eligible tax credit; local rules may exclude or limit it. Fees are added after the calculated tax, and excess available credit is not a negative loan.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate potential reverse-mortgage proceeds using an editable principal-limit assumption.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Home value ($): the current appraised or estimated value. It must be greater than zero.
  • Estimated principal limit (%): the share of value the program would lend, from 0 to 100. Actual limits depend on borrower age, rates and program rules; this is an illustrative input.
  • Existing mortgage payoff ($): any current mortgage that must be paid off first.
  • Estimated upfront costs ($): closing costs and fees paid from the proceeds.

Method and assumptions

The calculator applies the principal-limit percentage to the home value for the gross borrowing limit, then subtracts the existing mortgage payoff and the upfront costs for the estimated net proceeds, flooring at zero.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the gross borrowing limit is 45% of 500,000, which is $225,000.00. Subtracting the $80,000.00 mortgage payoff and $12,000.00 of upfront costs leaves estimated net proceeds of $133,000.00.

Reading the result

  • Estimated net proceeds is the headline: $133,000.00 on the starting inputs, the cash potentially available after paying off the existing mortgage and covering upfront costs.
  • The gross limit ($225,000.00) is what the percentage allows; the payoff and cost lines show how much of it is already committed before you see anything.
  • The principal-limit percentage is an illustrative input, not a quote: actual limits depend on age, current rates, program rules and the appraisal, and the limitations note directs you to lender and program sources for real figures.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a principal-limit percentage from advertising as if it were a quote; the real figure is set by program rules and current rates.
  • Forgetting the existing mortgage; it must be paid off from the proceeds first, which is why the net line subtracts it.
  • Reading net proceeds as income; they are loan proceeds that reduce home equity and accrue interest over time, as the limitations note emphasizes.
  • Ignoring upfront costs; they come out of the same proceeds, so the net line is the usable figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is this what I would actually receive?

No. It is a simple percentage model. Actual principal limits depend on borrower age, current rates, program rules and the appraisal, and counseling is typically required; the limitations note directs you to program and lender sources.

What if the mortgage and costs exceed the gross limit?

The net line floors at zero: there would be no proceeds left after the required payoff, which is a sign the structure may not work on these figures.

Why does a reverse mortgage need to pay off the existing one?

Because the reverse mortgage must be the primary loan on the home. The model subtracts the existing balance from the proceeds automatically, which is what the payoff line shows.

Important limitations

The percentage is a user assumption, not an age-and-rate-based HECM limit calculation. The model does not apply lending limits, required set-asides, initial-disbursement restrictions, eligibility or payment-option rules. A zero result can indicate a shortfall; it does not mean the loan can close without additional funds.

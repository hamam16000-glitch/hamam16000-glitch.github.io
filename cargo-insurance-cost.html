What this calculator estimates

Estimate an annual cargo-insurance premium from shipped value, rate, commodity risk, deductible discount and policy fees.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Annual value of cargo shipped ($): the total value of goods shipped in a year.
  • Base premium rate (%): the yearly rate applied to the shipped value before adjustments.
  • Commodity and route risk factor: a multiplier for the cargo type and route, where 1 means no adjustment. It must be greater than zero.
  • Deductible discount (%): a percentage credit off the adjusted premium, from 0 to 100.
  • Policy fees and endorsements ($): flat fees and add-ons added at the end.

Method and assumptions

The calculator multiplies the annual cargo value by the base rate, applies the risk factor and the deductible discount, then adds fees. It also shows the monthly equivalent, the base premium before adjustments, the adjusted premium, and the effective rate on the shipped value.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the base premium is 2,000,000 times 0.4%, which is $8,000.00. The 1.2 risk factor and 5% discount bring it to $9,120.00, and adding 500 of fees gives an annual estimate of $9,620.00, about $801.67 per month, an effective rate of 0.48% on the shipped value.

Reading the result

  • Estimated annual premium is the headline; Monthly equivalent divides it by 12.
  • Base premium and Risk-adjusted premium isolate what the commodity and route factor contributes: 1,120 here.
  • Effective rate restates the final premium as a share of cargo value, useful for comparing against other quotes; 0.48% here against the 0.4% base rate.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a single shipment's value instead of the annual total; the rate applies to the year's shipped value.
  • Setting the risk factor below 1 expecting a discount; that is what the deductible discount field does, while the risk factor scales the premium for commodity and route.
  • Forgetting fees sit outside the percentage math; the $500.00 is added after rate, factor and discount.
  • Treating the estimate as an insurer quote; the limitations note lists commodities, routes, conveyance and insurer minimums that shape actual pricing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the effective rate what an insurer would quote me?

Not necessarily; it is your entered inputs restated as one percentage for comparison. Actual pricing weighs cargo type, routes, claims history and insurer minimums, per the limitations note.

Why does the risk factor apply before the discount?

That is the calculator's order: base rate, then the commodity and route factor, then the deductible discount, then flat fees.

Can I model one expensive shipment?

This page prices a year of shipping; for a single shipment, enter its value as the annual value and read the annual premium as that shipment's modeled cost.

Important limitations

Cargo limits, commodities, theft exposure, routes, conveyance, storage, claims history, deductibles, exclusions and insurer minimum premiums affect actual pricing.

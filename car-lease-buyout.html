What this calculator estimates

Estimate a lease buyout price and compare it with the vehicle’s current market value.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Lease residual or buyout amount ($): the purchase price written into the lease contract for buying the car at lease end.
  • Purchase option and dealer fees ($): the option fee plus any dealer charges on the buyout.
  • Sales tax rate (%): the tax applied to the residual plus fees in the calculator's simplified arithmetic; actual tax treatment of lease buyouts varies by jurisdiction.
  • Estimated vehicle market value ($): what the car is actually worth now, from listings or valuation guides.

Method and assumptions

The calculator adds the fees to the residual, applies the sales tax rate to that taxable amount for the total buyout cost, then compares the total with the market value: the difference is positive equity (the car is worth more than it costs) or negative equity. It also shows the fees-plus-tax portion on its own.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the taxable amount is 24,000 plus 500, which is 24,500, and adding 7% tax gives a total buyout cost of $26,215.00. Against a $27,000.00 market value the equity is positive at $785.00, and the fees-and-tax portion beyond the residual is $2,215.00.

Reading the result

  • Total buyout cost is the headline: $26,215.00 on the starting inputs, everything paid to own the car outright at these figures.
  • The equity line is the decision view: positive equity of $785.00 here means buying at the residual costs less than the car is worth; a negative figure means the opposite.
  • The fees-and-tax line ($2,215.00) shows how much the total sits above the residual itself, the part that contract terms and jurisdiction tax rules control.

Common mistakes

  • Using the car's original price instead of the contractual residual; the residual in the lease documents is the buyout base.
  • Forgetting the purchase-option fee; lease contracts commonly charge one, and it is taxed with the residual in this model.
  • Assuming the tax treatment is universal; how buyouts are taxed differs by jurisdiction, so confirm with the lessor or local rules before relying on the total.
  • Skipping the market check; without the market value there is no way to tell whether the buyout is a good price for the car.

Frequently asked questions

What does positive equity tell me?

The car costs less to buy out than it is worth: $26,215.00 against $27,000.00 on the starting inputs. That gap can make buying attractive, or support negotiating a trade, subject to the lease terms.

Is financing the buyout included?

No. The total is the cash cost; a buyout loan would add interest, and the limitations note keeps financing outside the model.

Can the buyout price be negotiated?

That depends on the lessor and the contract. The page computes from the residual you enter, so enter the figure your documents actually state.

Important limitations

Actual payoff quotes, taxes, registration, inspection, financing and disposition rules vary by contract and state. Request the lessor’s current written buyout quote.

What this calculator estimates

Estimate first-year ROI from payroll software automation and error reduction.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Payroll hours saved per month: the staff hours the software saves each month. It can be 0.
  • Loaded hourly staff cost ($): the loaded hourly cost of the staff doing payroll. It can be 0.
  • Annual payroll errors and penalties avoided ($): your estimate of the yearly errors and penalties the software avoids. It can be 0.
  • Monthly payroll software cost ($): the subscription cost per month. It can be 0.
  • One-time implementation cost ($): the setup cost paid once. It can be 0.

Method and assumptions

The calculator values the saved hours at the loaded hourly cost over a year, adds the avoided errors and penalties for the annual benefit, totals the subscription year plus the setup for the cost, and reports the net, the return on the cost, and the months the setup takes to pay back from the net monthly benefit.

With the illustrative starting inputs: the benefit is 35 hours times 50 over twelve months plus 10,000, which is $31,000.00, the cost is $10,000.00, the net is $21,000.00, the return is 210%, and the setup pays back in 1.9 months.

Reading the result

  • The headline is the first-year return: 210% on the starting inputs, the net as a share of the $10,000.00 first-year cost.
  • The benefit line ($31,000.00) splits into time savings and avoided errors; both are your own estimates, so the return moves with them.
  • The payback line (1.9 months) is the cash-flow view: the setup cost clears in under two months of net monthly benefit on these figures.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the hourly wage instead of the loaded cost; the loaded figure includes taxes and benefits, and a bare wage understates the savings.
  • Entering the software's annual price in the monthly field; the model multiplies the monthly cost by twelve and adds the setup once.
  • Reading the return as guaranteed; the benefit rests on your own estimates of saved hours and avoided errors, so treat it as the arithmetic of your scenario, not a vendor promise.

Frequently asked questions

How is the payback computed?

The setup cost divided by the net monthly benefit: the benefit per month minus the subscription, giving 1.9 months on the starting inputs.

What if the software never pays back?

When the net monthly benefit is not positive, the model says so instead of showing a payback period.

Does the return count the second year?

No. The figures cover one year with the setup included; later years drop the setup, which raises the return on the same inputs.

Important limitations

Adoption, integration, training, contract terms, productivity estimates and avoided losses may differ.

ROI is undefined when the total modeled cost is zero. The associated cash-flow amounts can still be calculated; an undefined return percentage is not a zero-percent return.

First-year cost and realizable benefit

Use payroll-processing time across the same staff and pay cycles. An avoided payroll error already valued in staff hours should not also be included as a second cash saving.

Annual benefit = monthly hours saved × loaded hourly cost × 12 + annual avoided costs. First-year cost = monthly subscription × 12 + implementation. Net benefit is annual benefit minus first-year cost; ROI divides that net by first-year cost. This does not discount future money or assume the setup fee repeats every year.

With 10 hours saved per month at $30, no other savings, a $100 monthly fee and $600 setup, annual benefit is $3,600 and first-year cost is $1,800. Net benefit is $1,800 and ROI is 100%. Compare a second year separately if implementation is a one-time expense; the same first-year ROI formula should not be reused with that cost twice.

Separate payroll subscription from setup recovery

Use hours actually removed from payroll work rather than the full payroll department’s hours. At 10 hours saved per month valued at $50, a $200 monthly subscription and $1,200 setup, net monthly benefit is $300 and implementation payback is 4 months. If subscription cost equals or exceeds the monthly benefit, the page reports no positive net monthly benefit instead of a misleading finite recovery time. Savings are assumed to begin immediately and remain level.

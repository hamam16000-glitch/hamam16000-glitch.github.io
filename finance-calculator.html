What this calculator estimates

Project future value from a starting balance, recurring monthly contribution, annual return and time.

Inputs and units

Fill in each field, then select Calculate to update the results. Required fields cannot be blank.

  • Starting balance ($): the amount you begin with. It cannot be negative.
  • Monthly contribution ($): the amount added every month, treated as arriving at each month's end.
  • Expected annual return (%): the yearly growth rate assumption, converted to a monthly rate for compounding. It can be negative down to minus 100 for declining scenarios.
  • Years: the horizon. The page compounds monthly over the rounded number of months.

Method and assumptions

The calculator compounds the starting balance and each monthly contribution at the monthly equivalent of the annual return: the future value of the starting lump plus the future value of the contribution stream. It then shows how much of the result is money you put in (contributions total) and how much is growth, with the no-growth line equal to the contributions alone.

With the illustrative starting inputs: over 10 years, which is 120 months, at 7% a year the starting 10,000 plus 500 a month grows to $106,639.02. Contributions total 10,000 plus 500 times 120, which is $70,000.00, so growth supplies the remaining $36,639.02.

Reading the result

  • Future balance is the headline: $106,639.02 on the starting inputs, the projection at the entered return.
  • The growth line ($36,639.02) shows what compounding added beyond the $70,000.00 put in; over longer horizons this share typically grows faster than contributions, because earlier money compounds longer.
  • The no-growth line equals the contributed line by construction, a reference for what the same saving pattern produces without any return.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the projection as a forecast; the entered return is an assumption applied uniformly, and real returns vary year to year, which the limitations note flags.
  • Entering a yearly contribution in the monthly field; the model multiplies it by every month, inflating the result twelve-fold.
  • Ignoring inflation; the figure is in nominal terms, so its future purchasing power is lower unless the return is a real (after-inflation) rate.
  • Comparing against accounts with different contribution timing; this model credits contributions at each month's end.

Frequently asked questions

How is the annual return converted?

To a monthly rate by dividing by 12, compounded each month. On the starting inputs that is 7 over 12 per month in percent terms, applied to the balance and each contribution.

Can the return be negative?

Yes, down to minus 100 percent: the growth line then goes negative and the balance finishes below the contributed total.

Where do fees and taxes fit?

Outside the model. The limitations note keeps fund fees, account fees and taxes out, so the projection is gross of all of them.

Important limitations

Returns are not guaranteed. Taxes, fees, contribution timing and market volatility are not modeled.

End-of-month deposits and nominal return

The entered annual return is divided by 12, then the balance compounds monthly with contributions at each month end. The horizon rounds to the nearest whole month. This nominal-rate convention differs from converting an effective annual yield into a monthly rate. A constant return is a scenario assumption and does not represent a variable investment path, fees, taxes or inflation.
